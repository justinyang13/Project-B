# PART ONE: LOW TIDE

## 1. The Words That Swim
Synopsis: We meet Wren, who loves stories but whose words swim on the page, her fast-reading classmate Ravi, and her dad's ferry.
Header: *Rule 1 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Every book is a door. Knock gently.*
1. The trouble with words, Wren Tidewell decided, was that they would not stay still: they swam like minnows; the harbor town of Saltmarrow, the lighthouse, the ferry Gull, and ten-year-old Wren in coral-red sweater, overalls and yellow boots who has heard hundreds of stories and can tell every one back.
2. Room 4 reading aloud: Ravi Nair reads a whole page smoothly; Wren's turn comes, the words wriggle, she stumbles over "harbor", the class is not cruel but the silence is worse; she asks to go to the nurse with a stomachache she doesn't have.
3. After school at the ferry dock Ravi, stiff and kind in his own way, tells her he read forty-one books this summer and reading is easy, "you just read the words"; Wren, who has listened to a hundred, says nothing and thinks about swimming.
4. The ferry Gull crossing back with her dad Ben, in his green wool cap; he tells her a story of the sea that she memorizes on the spot; he says stories aren't in the words, "the words are only the boat."
5. Bedtime in her attic room above the harbor: the tide table on her wall, the lighthouse beam sweeping across the ceiling; she wishes for one place where words hold still; far out and far down, something turns over in its sleep.

## 2. The Upturned Boat
Synopsis: Wren's refuge, the Saltmarrow Library and gentle Mrs. Pell, and the news that the library may close.
Header: *Rule 2 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: A library is only closed when its last reader stops coming.*
1. Saturday morning: the Saltmarrow Library, an old customs house shaped like an upturned boat, with a creaking floor, a reading nook in the curve of the hull and the smell of paper and salt; Mrs. Pell, white curls and beaded glasses, nods hello and says nothing, which is what Wren loves.
2. Mrs. Pell never makes her read aloud; she reads to Wren from the nook chair, and lets Wren tell the story back afterwards, every word right; "That is reading too, dear. It is just reading by ear first."
3. The notice pinned on the door: the Harbor Council will vote on the last day of summer whether to close the library and sell the old building; Mr. Hollis Grubb, chair, in his tweed suit and watch chain, stops to say something about the leaking roof and the budget; Mrs. Pell straightens her cardigan and says nothing.
4. A bucket catching drips in the poetry corner; Mrs. Pell says quietly, "Books don't close, dear. People just stop opening them."
5. Wren walks home along the sea wall past the old white Saltmarrow Light, angry and sad, and promises herself she will fix it, though she has no idea how a girl who can't read can save a library.

## 3. The Storm Tide
Synopsis: A summer storm, a night of waves, and at dawn a strange book on the sand.
Header: *Rule 3 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Anything borrowed must be returned, however long it takes.*
1. That night a storm rolls into Saltmarrow; the ferry stays tied up, Dad checks the lines, rain hammers the attic roof, and Wren counts the seconds between the lightning and the thunder as the lighthouse beam stumbles through the rain.
2. Dad sits on her bed and tells her the harbor's oldest story about a lost library that sank with a ship; "Nobody ever found it," he says, "but nobody ever stopped looking."
3. Dawn: quiet, washed clean, a pearl-grey sky; the beach is littered with kelp, driftwood and shells; Wren in yellow boots walks the tideline the way she always does after storms.
4. Half-buried in the wet sand at the foot of the sea wall she finds a thick book in a green sea-worn cover, heavy as a loaf and warm, and strangely dry inside though the water has covered it all night.
5. She opens it, expecting the usual swimming; the first words lie perfectly still on the page, like boats tied up at a dock.

## 4. The Book That Held Still
Synopsis: Wren reads for the first time without the words swimming, finds a bookplate and a card, and lies to Ravi.
Header: *Rule 4 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Fines may be paid in stories.*
1. On the sand she reads the first line aloud, slowly: "There is a kind of rain that falls only on Tuesdays"; every word stays put; she reads a whole page, then another, so slowly and so happily that the tide creeps up round her boots.
2. The book is A Book of Small Weathers: short stories about tiny storms, a drizzle that sulked, a fog that fell in love with a lighthouse; she laughs out loud and startles a heron.
3. Inside the front cover a bookplate: THE LIBRARY AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA, and a faded stamp reading OVERDUE, and a card in curling handwriting: "Please return to the Returns Door at the foot of the Saltmarrow Light, at the lowest tide. Fines waived for the honest."
4. Ravi appears along the beach with his canvas satchel, hunting shells for a project; "What have you got?" Wren, who has never lied to him, hides the book under her sweater and says "Just a piece of driftwood."
5. Ravi frowns; he knows a lie when he hears one; she walks home, the book hot against her chest, feeling like a thief and a treasure-finder both.

## 5. The Lowest Tide
Synopsis: Wren checks the tide table, finds the Returns Door and posts the book, and the sea opens.
Header: *Rule 5 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: The lowest tide opens the smallest door.*
1. That night Wren reads two pages of the book under her blanket with a flashlight, then studies the tide table on her wall: the lowest tide of the year is the morning after next at dawn, a spring tide at the full moon; Dad's tide rhyme: "Full moon, low moon, the sea goes far to see."
2. Before dawn she creeps down the stairs, leaves a note for Dad, takes the book in her satchel and walks in her yellow boots to the Saltmarrow Light in the pearl-grey light while the sea pulls back farther than she has ever seen.
3. At the very foot of the old lighthouse, where the sea wall meets the rock, the water has drained away from a small round green door no bigger than a barrel lid, with a brass slot shaped like an open book and barnacles round the frame.
4. She feeds A Book of Small Weathers into the slot; there is a deep "chunk" like an enormous stamp; the door swings open; behind it a spiral of water is holding itself up like a glass staircase, and a small white moon-shell on a cord drops into her hand with a note: "Borrower's shell. Wear it. It lets you breathe, speak and stay dry in the pages."
5. Wren hangs the shell round her neck, says "Right. One page," and steps onto the first glowing step of the Spiral.

# PART TWO: THE LIBRARY AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA

## 6. The Returns Desk
Synopsis: Wren meets Madame Ottoline and Squib, and learns she owes 212 years of overdue fines, payable in one story.
Header: *Rule 6 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Whisper. The deep carries every sound.*
1. The Spiral winds down through green water full of drifting light; Wren finds she can breathe and even talk, though her voice comes out small and echoing; she comes to an enormous round door of coral and old brass and pushes.
2. The Returns Desk: a vaulted hall of glowing glass, shelves rising out of sight, and behind a huge desk Madame Ottoline, a plum-purple octopus with pearl pince-nez and a silver ribbon over her head, stamping three books at once with three arms while a fourth holds the inkwell.
3. "Overdue by two hundred and twelve years," says Ottoline, examining A Book of Small Weathers; Squib, a small speckled cuttlefish with wavy pupils, turns pink with astonishment; "The fine, however, is waived for the honest. We ask only for one story, told aloud."
4. Wren goes cold: read aloud? She confesses in a tiny voice that she can't; Ottoline, whose eight arms go still, says gently, "A story does not have to be read, child. It only has to be told."
5. So Wren tells the story of the ferry and the storm that she heard from Dad, word for word, and at the end the whole hall is quiet; Squib turns gold; Ottoline blows her nose on a bookmark: "Fine paid in full."

## 7. The Growing Stacks
Synopsis: A tour of the Library where books grow like coral, and the lanterns above are dim.
Header: *Rule 7 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Books grow best when they are read.*
1. Squib, who has never met a surface child, gives Wren the tour, flashing colors as he talks: the Growing Stacks, gardens of books that sprout from the sea floor like coral, spines opening like sea fans.
2. New books grow slowly, one page a year; a ripe one glows and is picked with a silver hook; Wren holds a freshly grown book and smells warm, salty paper, and it whispers a first line.
3. They meet Bartleby, an ancient green sea turtle with barnacles and a whole small bookcase, the Slow Shelf, strapped to his shell; "There is no hurry in a good book," he says, taking a full minute to say it, and gives Wren a book with pictures.
4. The Lantern Hall: a huge round room hung with strings of small amber lantern fish, the Glimmers, meant to fill the hall with golden light, but most hang dim and sleepy like lamps running out of oil.
5. "They glow when someone reads aloud to them," explains Squib, "and nobody has for a very long time." Wren looks up at the dim lights and has an odd feeling in her chest, like the lowest tide.

## 8. Never Chase a Word
Synopsis: Ottoline gives Wren a sea-glass reading guide and teaches her to let words settle.
Header: *Rule 8 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Never chase a word. Sit still and let it settle.*
1. In the reading room, a circle of chairs shaped like shells, Ottoline asks Wren to read one line from an ordinary book; the words swim; Wren's ears burn and she says the thing she has never said aloud: "The words won't stay still."
2. Ottoline does not laugh; three hearts beat in her, one for stories, one for facts and one for the people who need them; she says that many of her best readers began as swimmers.
3. She places a curved piece of green sea-glass flat over one line so that only that line shows; the other lines fade; "Never chase a word," she says, "put a small window round it and let it come to you."
4. Wren reads one line, then another, slowly, with the glass moving down the page like a small green boat; each word settles when she waits; it takes five minutes for a paragraph and it is the happiest paragraph of her life.
5. Ottoline gives her the sea-glass to keep, "on permanent loan"; Bartleby, who has been listening for an hour from the doorway, says "There is no hurry" and Wren discovers she has a whole page done.

## 9. The Deep Stacks
Synopsis: Wren and Squib discover the fading Deep Stacks where unread books turn to sand, and the Ledger with an ancestor's name.
Header: *Rule 9 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: A book no one opens slowly turns to sand.*
1. Squib, colors flickering nervously, leads her against Rule 6 down a long stair to the Deep Stacks, a huge dim cavern of shelves where the light is grey and cold.
2. Here the colors drain: the Drift, a slow grey cloud of silt, drifts along the shelves, and wherever it settles the books crumble to fine pale sand; Wren picks up a book that was a story about a whale and it falls apart in her hands like a sandcastle.
3. "Nobody reads them," says Squib, going grey, "so the Drift takes them, one shelf a year"; Captain Clack, a crab as big as a wheelbarrow in a small blue cap, appears with a clatter and orders them out.
4. Ottoline finds them and, instead of scolding, opens the Ledger of the Founders' Shelf: the last borrower's name, in faded ink 212 years ago: "Wren Tidewell, aged ten. Borrowed: A Book of Small Weathers."
5. Wren stares at her own name; "Anyone can return a book, child," says Ottoline. "That is the whole point of a library." But she looks up at the dim lights, and Wren knows there is more.

## 10. Squib's Question
Synopsis: Wren and Squib swap worlds, and Wren must go home before the tide turns.
Header: *Rule 10 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Every reader was once a beginner.*
1. On a bench in the Growing Stacks Squib asks all about the surface: what is a school, what is a ferry, what does rain look like from underneath; Wren answers with the same vividness she tells stories.
2. She tells him about the Saltmarrow Library, its leaky roof and the vote at the end of summer; Squib's colors go grey: "A library that closes? Then where do the stories go?" Wren doesn't know.
3. Bartleby, who is very slow and always on time, says the tide is turning; she must go before the Spiral closes; Ottoline stamps a small card: "Borrower's card, one only, use with care."
4. The Spiral carries her up in a long silver rush; she steps out onto the wet sand as the sea comes back over the door; her yellow boots are perfectly dry.
5. Dad is on the dock, worried and warm and gruff; "Where were you?" "Shell hunting." It is her first real lie to him, and she carries it up the hill like a stone in her pocket.

# PART THREE: THE DRIFT

## 11. The Lanterns Flicker
Synopsis: Wren returns at every low tide, and reads aloud to the Glimmers, but stops when they flicker at her mistakes.
Header: *Rule 11 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: The Glimmers do not grade. They only glow.*
1. For a week Wren visits at every low tide from the tide table on her wall; she practices with the sea-glass and a book with pictures; Squib teaches her to sort books by their shelf-marks; Ottoline stamps everything with a satisfied "chunk."
2. Ottoline asks her to try reading aloud to the Lantern Hall: Wren stands in the middle, her voice a thread, and the first line makes a few Glimmers brighten; she feels a happiness like sunlight.
3. Then she stumbles over a word, and a string of Glimmers flickers out; she thinks they are laughing; she stops, crimson, and runs from the hall.
4. Squib finds her behind a shelf and tells her, clumsy but sure, that Glimmers flicker whenever a reader takes a breath; "It isn't a mistake, it's just the light blinking." Ottoline adds: "They do not grade. They only glow."
5. Wren wants to believe it; she goes back in and reads one more line and this time the light holds, but her heart is still shaky and she goes home early.

## 12. Ravi Follows
Synopsis: Ravi, hurt by Wren's secrets, follows her to the lighthouse and sees her vanish.
Header: *Rule 12 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Secrets weigh more underwater.*
1. At school Wren is tired and distracted; Ravi watches her yawn through reading time and thinks she is avoiding him; he says something sharp about her "fake stomachaches"; she says something sharp back.
2. That night Wren tells herself she'll tell Ravi about the library tomorrow, and does not; the tide table says low tide at half past six.
3. Ravi, red sneakers pounding, follows her along the sea wall at dawn because he has been counting how many mornings she has vanished; he ducks behind a fishing net at the foot of the lighthouse.
4. He sees the small round door open in the wall and Wren step onto a staircase of water and disappear; the door closes with a "chunk"; he stands there with his mouth open.
5. He waits until she reappears at dusk, dry as a bone, and says nothing but "I know you're lying," and walks away; Wren realizes she has lost a friend.

## 13. The Notice
Synopsis: Mrs. Pell packs boxes; Wren realizes the two libraries share the same problem.
Header: *Rule 13 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Do not lock away what you love.*
1. At the Saltmarrow Library Mrs. Pell is packing books into cardboard boxes "just in case," her beaded glasses trembling on her chain; the poetry corner bucket is now three buckets.
2. Mr. Grubb arrives with a clipboard and a man measuring the walls for a seafood restaurant; Wren hears him say gently "It's only a building" and sees Mrs. Pell go very still.
3. Wren lies awake thinking: the sea library dims because no one reads, the town library shuts because no one visits; "Books don't close," Mrs. Pell had said; "People just stop opening them"; the same problem, on two sides of the same sea wall.
4. She tells Dad a little of the truth: she found something wonderful and needs to help it; he listens with his cap in his hands and doesn't ask her to explain, only says, "The sea keeps her own hours."
5. Wren resolves to ask Ottoline how many readers it would take to keep the lanterns bright.

## 14. The Warden
Synopsis: Captain Clack wants to seal the Deep Stacks, and Wren and Clack quarrel.
Header: *Rule 14 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: You cannot save a library by locking it.*
1. In the Library an emergency: the Drift has crept a full shelf closer overnight; Captain Clack, in a blue captain's cap, bangs his brass-capped claw on the desk and declares he will seal the Deep Stacks with stone doors and save what remains.
2. Ottoline, all eight arms folded, refuses: half the library's oldest stories are in the Deep Stacks; Clack says he lost half the library once already, two hundred and twelve years ago when the last borrowers stopped coming.
3. Wren, forgetting to whisper, says "You can't save a library by locking it. That's the whole point of one."
4. Clack scuttles up to her, enormous and red, claw clicking: "You're a surface child who reads like a fish swimming upstream; you'll leave, they always leave; what use are you?"
5. Wren feels the words land like a stone; she opens her mouth to argue and can only say the truth: "I'm not any use. I can barely read." The Glimmers dim; she turns and runs.

## 15. What Dad Said
Synopsis: Wren, upset, is found by her dad, who tells her his own secret about reading.
Header: *Rule 15 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Slow readers read deepest.*
1. Wren scrambles out of the Returns Door in tears and finds Dad waiting on the sea wall with a thermos, having followed the lantern light on the water; "I didn't go far," he says. "I just waited."
2. On the ferry bench she tells him everything: the words that swim, the stomachaches, the library at the bottom of the sea, Clack's words; Dad listens, watching the gulls.
3. He opens the ferry's log book, and for the first time she notices he reads it with his finger under each line, slowly; "I was the slowest reader in my class, Wren-bird; I still am. I learned the tide tables as rhymes because it was quicker."
4. "Slow readers see everything; they can't skim; they stay with each word," he says; "That's why you remember every story you ever heard."
5. Wren leans her head on his sweater and laughs for the first time in days; the ferry rocks; the lighthouse turns; she decides to go back down, and to bring the truth with her.

# PART FOUR: THE SPRING TIDE

## 16. Ravi Sees the Library
Synopsis: Wren tells Ravi everything and takes him to the Library, where he learns to slow down.
Header: *Rule 16 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Two readers see more than one.*
1. At school Wren walks up to Ravi at lunch and says "You were right. I lied. Come with me tomorrow at half past six and I'll tell you the truth," and he says "Okay" stiffly and shows up in his striped shirt and red sneakers.
2. At the Returns Door she gives him the spare moon-shell, and Ravi, who has never believed anything he could not read in a book, steps onto the Spiral with his satchel hugged to his chest.
3. In the Library Ravi is speechless for three whole minutes, a new record; Squib turns every color; Ottoline, delighted, says "A fast reader! We have not had one in ages. Fast readers are exactly what the Growing Stacks need to sort the ripe books."
4. But when Ottoline hands him a single book and asks him to tell her what it was about, Ravi, who has read forty-one books this summer, finds he cannot remember a single one; Wren, who can't read fast, can retell them all.
5. Ravi says quietly, "I don't think I've ever really read a book. I've only finished them"; Wren says "Then read one with me."

## 17. Two Readers
Synopsis: Wren and Ravi read one story together, and the Drift retreats a little.
Header: *Rule 17 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: One voice makes a candle; a thousand make a sun.*
1. In the Lantern Hall the two children sit side by side: Ravi reads a line quickly; Wren says "Wait: read it again and slower" and he does; a small string of Glimmers lights up above them.
2. Wren reads the next line with her sea-glass; Ravi listens, really listens, and laughs at a joke he would have skimmed; for the first time he goes back and reads a page just because he wants to.
3. Together they read a whole chapter, alternating; a great swath of the Lantern Hall glows gold; far off in the Deep Stacks the Drift shrinks back from the nearest shelf.
4. Squib flashes with joy and then grey with worry: it is only a small part; there are hundreds of shelves and only two readers.
5. Ottoline says the Library needs a thousand voices; Ravi, counting in his head, says slowly: "How many children are there in Saltmarrow?"

## 18. The Plan
Synopsis: The two children plan a reading of the whole town on the beach, since the sea carries every sound.
Header: *Rule 18 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Read it anyway.*
1. Wren remembers Rule 6: "The deep carries every sound." If the sea carries sound, then a reading on the beach at the lowest tide would echo all the way down to the Lantern Hall.
2. Ravi makes lists, of course: every class, every teacher, every fisherman with a book on his shelf; Wren remembers the stories every child in Saltmarrow already loves; they make posters in her attic with no fancy lettering and one plain line: "Bring a book. Bring your voice. Low tide, sunrise, the Saltmarrow Light."
3. Squib, by shell-post, promises that the whole Library will be listening; Ottoline sends a note: "We shall be delighted. Please do not chase any words."
4. Wren has to tell Mrs. Pell and asks if the library's reading nook can be used to plan; Mrs. Pell, packing her last box, unpacks it instead and says "Dear, I have waited a long time for someone to ask me to open a door."
5. Dad promises to bring the ferry lantern and the ferry's foghorn to wake the town; the night before, Wren looks at the tide table: the lowest tide of the whole summer is at dawn the next day; her stomach flips with fear and joy.

## 19. The Reading on the Beach
Synopsis: The town's children and grown-ups gather on the beach at dawn and read aloud, and the Library glows.
Header: *Rule 19 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: The bravest sentence in any language is "I was wrong."*
1. Dawn at the lowest tide: the sea has drawn back farther than anyone alive has seen; Wren and Ravi wait on the wet sand, sure no one will come; then the first family arrives with a picnic basket, then a whole class with Ms. Alvarez, then the fishermen with a battered book of poems.
2. A hundred people sit on the sand with books open; Mrs. Pell in her plum cardigan; Mr. Grubb arrives stiffly with his clipboard and stands at the edge with his mouth open.
3. Wren takes a breath, puts the sea-glass over the first line of A Book of Small Weathers, and reads it aloud, slowly, stumbling over "harbor", and simply carries on to the next word; one by one, others begin reading too, all at once, a sea of voices.
4. Down below, the whole Lantern Hall blazes gold; the Drift shrinks along every shelf; Ottoline weeps ink; and Captain Clack, at the Returns Door, listens with his claw pressed against the wood, and then does a thing he has not done for two hundred and twelve years: he opens the door of the Deep Stacks.
5. A crab in a blue cap climbs up the Spiral to the beach, tips his cap to Wren in front of a hundred astonished people and says clearly, "I was wrong. Please forgive an old crab. Please keep reading."

## 20. The Returns Slot
Synopsis: The Council vote, both libraries saved, and the rule rewritten.
Header: *Rule 20 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Every book is a door. Knock gently, then go in, and bring a friend.*
1. The Harbor Council meets on the last day of summer in the library itself, under a bucket; Mr. Grubb clears his throat, admits the roof needs mending and the town clearly needs the library; he moves to keep it open, and the vote is unanimous, including his own.
2. The whole town volunteers to mend the roof; the fishermen bring tar, the children bring buckets and Ravi brings a list; Mrs. Pell cries into her beaded glasses and calls it the finest day in the library's history.
3. In the Deep Library the unread books are rescued and the Drift is gone from all but one dusty shelf; Ottoline, Clack, Squib and Bartleby hold a very small ceremony; Wren receives Borrower's Card Number One Thousand, and Ravi Number One Thousand and One.
4. At the foot of the Saltmarrow Light the Returns Door now has a second slot, a small one, marked with a shell; every low tide a child from the town returns a book to the sea and borrows one back; the two libraries are sister libraries.
5. That night in the attic Wren reads one page of A Book of Small Weathers aloud to her dad by lantern light, slowly, with her finger under the line; he reads the next, and the sea listens; one plain line: nobody has to be fast to be a reader.
