# WREN AND THE LIBRARY AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA — story bible

Cozy sea-fantasy for ages 10-12: warm, funny, wondrous, gently adventurous, a picture-book classic for readers who are just getting brave. A small harbor town, a lighthouse, a library that is closing, and a second library at the bottom of the sea where books grow like coral and octopus scholars stamp the returns. No gore, no death, no romance, no modern slang, no scary monsters: the "danger" is quiet (a library that could fade away), never a chase or a threat to a child's life. Magic is small, watery and domestic (a moon-shell that lets you breathe, lanterns that glow when you read to them, a turtle who carries a whole bookshelf).

MAIN GOAL OF THE BOOK: make the reader LOVE books and feel that reading is for everybody, including slow readers. Enjoyment first; the life lesson is dramatized, never preached.

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Wren, a warm storyteller narrator with a light, lyrical, funny touch and lots of sea imagery (tide, salt, kelp, lantern light). Short paragraphs, gentle jokes, sensory detail (the smell of old paper and salt, the sound the sea makes at low tide). Dialogue is quick and funny; the sea creatures are formal, fussy and kind. When something hurts, the narrator gets plain and quiet. Each chapter opens with one italic rule on its own line, then a blank line: "*Rule N of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: ...*". The rules are TRUE and wise (unlike a pompous almanac) and the chapter shows why; rule 20 is a warm rewrite of rule 1.
Voice sample:
"*Rule 1 of the Library at the Bottom of the Sea: Every book is a door. Knock gently.*

The trouble with words, Wren Tidewell had decided, was that they would not stay still.

They swam. On the page they looked perfectly ordinary, black and small and lined up in rows like good children, but the moment she looked straight at them they began to wriggle, like minnows in a tide pool, and by the time she had caught one, three more had swum away."

## The premise
Wren Tidewell, 10, lives in Saltmarrow, a small harbor town, with her dad Ben, who pilots the little ferry *Gull* out to Gannet Island. Wren adores stories (she has heard hundreds and can tell every one back word for word) but reading them herself is hard: the words "swim" on the page and she is the slowest reader in Room 4. She hides it with stomachaches and jokes. Her favourite place is the Saltmarrow Library in the old customs house, shaped like an upturned boat, where Mrs. Pell lets her sit in the reading nook. The Harbor Council will vote on the last day of summer whether to close it. After a storm, Wren finds a strange thick book washed up on the sand at dawn, dry inside, whose words HOLD STILL for her. The bookplate reads THE LIBRARY AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA, and inside the cover a card says "Overdue. Please return to the Returns Door at the foot of the Saltmarrow Light, at the lowest tide." At the year's lowest tide she finds the small round door in the sea wall, posts the book through its brass slot, and is given a moon-shell that lets her breathe underwater. Below is the Library: books that GROW like coral in gardens, lanterns of glowing fish that shine only when someone reads aloud to them, and the head librarian Madame Ottoline. The Library is fading: no reader has come in 212 years, the lanterns are dim, and the unread books in the Deep Stacks are slowly turning to sand (the Drift). Wren learns to read at her own speed, discovers that the lanterns do not grade, only glow, brings her friend Ravi and finally the whole town's children to read aloud on the beach at the lowest tide, and saves both libraries.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Wren Tidewell**, 10. Deep-brown skin, short bouncy black curls, a coral-red hooded sweater (hood down), dark-blue denim overalls, yellow rubber boots (worn everywhere, also underwater), and from ch5 a white moon-shell necklace on a cord. Curious, funny, stubborn, a great listener and rememberer; hides her reading trouble with jokes. Habit: taps the rhythm of a story on her knee. Refrain when scared: "Right. One page." (one page at a time)
- **Ben Tidewell**, Wren's dad, 40s. Tall, brown skin, short black beard with some grey, a green wool cap, a navy fisherman's sweater, black rubber boots. Pilots the ferry *Gull*, tells wonderful stories, knows the tides by rhyme. Secret: he was a slow reader too and still reads the ship's log with a finger under each line. Warm, calm, teasing. Calls her "Wren-bird".
- **Mrs. Pell**, the librarian, 70s. Short white curls, round tortoiseshell glasses on a beaded chain, a plum-purple cardigan, a long grey skirt, sensible brown shoes. Fair skin. Quiet, dry, kind; never rushes anyone. "Books don't close, dear. People just stop opening them."
- **Ravi Nair**, 10, Wren's classmate and neighbor. Medium-brown skin, straight black hair with a side part, a blue-and-white striped shirt, khaki shorts, red sneakers, a brown canvas satchel full of books. Reads fast (forty-one books this summer, he counts) but only to finish; he has never once re-read a page for love. Clever, a bit stiff, lonely, loyal. Arc: learns to slow down and listen; the two children complement each other.
- **Mr. Hollis Grubb**, chair of the Harbor Council. Heavyset, bald, big grey mustache, a brown tweed suit, gold watch chain. Not wicked: worried about the leaky roof and the budget; changes his mind.
- **Madame Ottoline**, head librarian of the Deep Library: an enormous plum-purple octopus with pearl-rimmed pince-nez spectacles, a silver ribbon bookmark draped over her head like a sash, eight arms always carrying books, stamps, an inkwell. Formal, fussy, tender; speaks in full grammatical sentences. Three hearts ("one for stories, one for facts, one for the people who need them").
- **Squib**, a small cuttlefish, the library's apprentice and Wren's best sea friend: teal and gold speckled, wavy W-shaped pupils, changes color with his feelings (pink when shy, gold when happy, grey when worried), wears a tiny brass key on a cord. Eager, curious, jokes badly. Has never seen the surface.
- **Bartleby**, an ancient green sea turtle with barnacles and a whole small bookcase strapped to his shell (the Slow Shelf, a mobile library). Very slow, very wise, sleepy, likes to say "There is no hurry in a good book."
- **Captain Clack**, the Library's harbor-warden: a crab as big as a wheelbarrow, red-orange, one claw tipped with a brass cap, a small blue captain's cap. Gruff, protective; lost half the Library to the Drift once; wants to seal the Deep Stacks to save the rest. Learns to say "I was wrong."
- **The Glimmers**: strings of small amber-gold lantern fish that hang like lamps in the Lantern Hall; they glow only when someone reads aloud to them, and they do NOT judge mistakes.
- **The Drift**: a slow grey cloud of silt that dulls colors and turns unread books to sand. Not evil: nature. It fades where stories are read aloud.
- Places: Saltmarrow (harbor, ferry dock, fish market, school Room 4), the Saltmarrow Library (old customs house shaped like an upturned boat, reading nook), the Saltmarrow Light (old white lighthouse on the rocks), the Returns Door in the sea wall at its foot, the Spiral (glowing water stair), the Returns Desk, the Growing Stacks (book gardens), the Lantern Hall, the Slow Shelf (Bartleby), the Deep Stacks (dim, grey), the Founders' Shelf and the Ledger, Gannet Island.

## The plan of the story
PART ONE, LOW TIDE (ch1-5): Wren, the swimming words, Ravi, the library that may close, the storm, the washed-up book whose words hold still, the lowest tide and the Returns Door. PART TWO, THE LIBRARY AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA (ch6-10): Ottoline and the 212-year fine, the Growing Stacks, the Slow Shelf, Squib, the rule "never chase a word" and the sea-glass reading guide, the Lantern Hall, the Deep Stacks turning to sand, the Ledger with "Wren Tidewell" (an ancestor who borrowed the book and never came back). PART THREE, THE DRIFT (ch11-15): repeated visits, the lanterns flicker and Wren stops reading aloud; Ravi follows her and is hurt by her secret; the council notice; Captain Clack wants to lock the Deep Stacks; Wren says the wrong thing, is called useless, flees; Dad confesses he too read slowly. PART FOUR, THE SPRING TIDE (ch16-20): Wren tells Ravi the truth, he sees the Library and learns to slow down; the plan (the sea carries every sound, so read to it); the beach reading of the whole town at dawn; Clack apologizes; the Council vote; the returns door becomes a shared door, both libraries saved.

## Facts to teach through story (accurate, light touch)
Octopus: three hearts, blue blood, arms with their own nerve clusters, can squeeze through any gap larger than its beak, can shoot ink. Cuttlefish: change color, W-shaped pupils, cuttlebone. Sea turtles live many decades and navigate far. Lantern fish and other deep-sea fish make their own light (bioluminescence). Tides: the moon's pull; lowest tides come at new and full moon (spring tides). Kelp forests. Coral grows slowly. Silt and sediment. Library words to teach: overdue, fine, ledger, catalog, borrower, returns, stacks, archive, reference, shelf-mark, spine, appendix. Reading strategies shown gently: reading with a finger or a ruler under the line, reading aloud, re-reading a page, listening to stories. Never diagnose Wren: it is "words that swim", and there is no shame in it.

## Motifs
The rules; "Every book is a door"; moon-shell necklace; the sea-glass reading guide (a curved piece of sea glass Ottoline gives Wren; laid over a line it makes the words settle); the tide table on Wren's wall; Dad's tide rhymes; Bartleby's "no hurry"; Squib's color changes; the Glimmers; the Drift; Ravi counting books; stamps that go "chunk"; the smell of old paper and salt.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Reading is not a race. Everybody is a beginner at something hard, slow readers read deepest, and being brave means reading it anyway, out loud, mistakes and all. Libraries are kept alive by readers, and a shared story is stronger than a locked door. Only one plain line near the end names the moral. Do NOT make the Council or Captain Clack villains: they are worried and can change. No one is shamed for reading slowly except by their own fear.

## Things to avoid
No death, no monsters, no cruelty, no romance, no phones or technology, no modern slang. No "chosen one": Ottoline says "anyone can return a book, that is the whole point of a library." Don't let Wren magically read fluently: she improves slowly and still reads slowly at the end, but she loves it. No real-language words (this is not a culture book). Keep facts about sea animals accurate.
