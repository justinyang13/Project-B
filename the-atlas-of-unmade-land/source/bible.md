# THE ATLAS OF UNMADE LAND — story bible

Epic fantasy for readers 14-18 (High School shelf, author Yang, publisher Yang Ink). A surveying-and-engineering adventure: in a kingdom where an exact, honestly measured map physically fixes the land it describes, a sixteen-year-old apprentice surveyor is handed the commission to chart a stretch of drifting, unfixed country called the Unmade. Inspiring, clever, warm, funny in places, with light clean romance. NOTHING scary: wonder, suspense, stakes and a deadline, never horror, gore or menace to the body.

MAIN GOAL: the reader finishes wanting to measure something, build something, and tell the truth about their work. Engineering is the hero: every plot turn is solved by real surveying, levelling, structure or hydrology, explained clearly enough to learn from, never as a lecture.

LESSON: **Accuracy is a form of care. What you make with your hands and numbers decides what other people get to live in, so measure twice, show your work, and own your errors.** Dramatized through choices; one earned plain statement near the end (Master Vasht, ch24; Isla, ch25).

## Reading level and voice
SAT-level prose: precise, varied vocabulary (e.g. meticulous, tenuous, surmise, assiduous, unprecedented, ephemeral, intrepid, ostensibly, ambivalent, laconic, pragmatic, perfunctory, tenacity, culminate, scrupulous, discrepancy, corroborate, ingenuity, equivocate, pernicious, fastidious), longer sentences with subordinate clauses, irony and dry wit, interior thought. THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Isla, a literate, wry, lyrical narrator in the tradition of Philip Pullman and Terry Pratchett for older readers. Paragraphs of 2-6 sentences, brisk dialogue, concrete sensory detail, one dry joke per page at most. Teen characters speak like teens: quick, funny, unfinished sentences, no baby talk and no slang that dates. Pages are about 400 words. Every chapter opens with one epigraph line in italics from "The Surveyor's Catechism" (the guild's book of sayings), then a blank line.
Voice sample:
"*The Surveyor's Catechism, First Saying: A line is only as honest as the hand that holds it.*

On the morning she was supposed to learn whether she had a future, Isla Corrin was lying flat on her stomach on the roof of the Meridian Guild with one eye pressed to a brass sightglass, trying to decide whether the chimney of the Royal Mint was leaning or whether the entire city of Vell was."

## How the magic works (rules of the world: keep them exact)
- **Fixing.** Land in Aster can be "fixed": a map drawn in guild ink from honest, redundant measurement binds the land to its shape, so hills stay put, rivers keep their beds, roads stay where they were laid. Unmapped land is "loose" (it drifts, slowly, like a tide: hills creep, streams wander, fog pools). The Unmade is a wide stretch of loose country at the kingdom's edge, a blank sheet on the great Atlas.
- **Honest numbers.** The ink obeys only measurement that is truthful AND checked. A careless or lying number makes the land bend where the error is (a hill sags, a river runs uphill for a stretch, a field tilts). Redundancy fixes this: measure a thing two ways and the numbers must agree (a triangle's angles must add to 180 degrees; a traverse must close back on its start). The size of the "closing error" tells you how far to trust the map.
- **A steady hand** (a gift not magic: patience and trained muscle) lets a surveyor draw a true line. Isla's gift: she can hold a line perfectly, and she checks everything three times, a habit people call stubborn.
- Fixing is gentle and beautiful: when a sheet is sealed, the land settles with a sound like a long breath and a ripple of light across it. Never frightening.
- **Choices become land.** Where there is no existing shape, the surveyor decides (where the road runs, where the river is held, which land is common, where a bridge crosses) and whatever the sealed sheet says becomes real. That is the responsibility at the heart of the book.

## Engineering used (accurate; explain in-scene)
- Baseline measured twice with a 100-link Gunter-style chain corrected for sag and temperature, later with a steel tape; pegs, plumb bobs, tension spring.
- Triangulation: one baseline plus angles read with a theodolite (here a "sightglass") gives every other distance by the law of sines; a "strong figure" has well-shaped triangles (avoid thin ones); redundancy; check angles sum to 180.
- Traverse and the closing error; spreading the error across the legs (Bowditch-style, "the honest sharing of a mistake"); magnetic declination (compass north differs from true north; find true north from the Pole Star).
- Levelling: backsight and foresight with a level and staff; gradient; a mill-race or aqueduct needs a steady fall (e.g. 1 in 1,500); contour lines show slope.
- Hydrology: meanders, floodplain, why a river wants to wander; sluice, weir, spillway.
- Bridge-wright work: arch thrust and the keystone, timber truss with triangles, cofferdam, falsework, load testing by walking the wagon over slowly.
- Scale, projection, legend, field-book discipline: write every reading down at the moment you take it, never from memory.

## Cast (fixed looks; each has a distinct hair, main color, accessory, footwear)
- **Isla Corrin, 16.** Hero. Light-brown skin, long dark-auburn hair twisted in a high knot held by two crossed brass pencils, ink-stained fingers, mustard-yellow canvas surveyor's coat with a dozen pockets, dark-green trousers, brown knee-high boots with brass buckles, a small brass sighting-compass on a cord around her neck. Daughter of a ferry-chain maker in the river town of Corrin Ferry, scholarship apprentice at the Meridian Guild; the other apprentices are gentry. Exacting, funny when relaxed, ashamed of how her family name sits in the guild (see Hester). Fear: being merely adequate. Want: to prove precision matters. Need: to forgive error, including her own and her family's.
- **Master Orrin Vasht, 63.** Her teacher. Dark-brown skin, white beard clipped short, grey wool coat, a battered brown felt hat with a pencil in the band, a carved walking stick, grey eyes. Gentle, dry, patient, hates hurry. Breaks his ankle in ch10 and stays at the base camp in a chair, directing by letters. Holds a secret: he once reported Hester Corrin's survey as faulty (he was a junior clerk and simply followed the guild's finding) and has regretted the haste all his life. Confesses in ch19.
- **Joss Harrowgate, 17.** Bridge-wright's apprentice and Isla's friend then crush. Tall, pale freckled skin, untidy sandy hair, sleeves rolled, rust-red work shirt, brown leather apron, grey trousers, worn black work boots, a carpenter's pencil behind one ear, a coil of rope over his shoulder. Cheerful, handy, makes jokes when nervous, thinks in loads and forces. He and Isla balance each other: she measures, he builds.
- **Mags Okoro, 15.** The expedition's chain-bearer and cook. Deep-brown skin, short black twists tied with a bright blue scarf, teal jacket, patched cream trousers, red canvas shoes, an iron cooking pot tied to her pack. From a river-barge family, fearless, funny, a natural at mental arithmetic, makes porridge that "could hold up a bridge".
- **Sera Quillon, 18.** Rival, later ally. Pale skin, sleek black bob with a sharp fringe, crisp plum-purple velvet guild coat with silver buttons, cream breeches, tall polished black boots, a slim silver rule in her breast pocket. The best student in the guild by speed; trusts estimates. Sent by the Chancellor to "assist". Cuts a corner in ch14 and makes a lopsided valley; learns, apologizes, and later saves the final survey with a bold re-reading. Not a villain.
- **Lord Chancellor Aldous Vane, 55.** Pearl-grey frock coat, silver hair, gold-topped cane, thin smile. Wants the Unmade as private grain estates and a reservoir; presses for speed and a "fast sheet". Dishonest in method (rigged benchmarks), never violent. Loses by being checked in public.
- **Queen Cassia of Aster, 40s.** Deep-blue gown, a plain silver circlet, sharp eyes, dry humor. Fair; asks to see the field-book.
- **Old Perrin Ashdown, 78.** Elder of Ennis Vale, the lost hamlet found in the Unmade. Tiny, wiry, white whiskers, a patched green coat, a stick; remembers Hester Corrin with affection and tells Isla the truth about her.
- **Hester Corrin (Isla's great-grandmother).** Appears in her field-book and in memory. Surveyed Ennis Vale seventy years ago; her sheet was condemned for a closing error; the valley slid out of the Atlas and became "loose". Truth: her work was right; the guild's own benchmark in Vell was wrong by a hair (found in ch21).

## Places
- **Vell**, the capital: a hill-city of chimneys and clocktowers; the **Meridian Guild** (grey stone, brass domes, roof observatory, the great vaulted Atlas Room with sheets as tall as doors). **Corrin Ferry**, Isla's river town.
- **The Marches**, last fixed farmland; **Cairn Fell**, the first ridge before the Unmade; **the Pale** (the drifting fog-plain); **the Wend**, a river that wanders; **Ennis Vale** (hidden hamlet: slate roofs, a mill, an orchard, a bell in a stone tower, lost 70 years); **Gantry Gorge** (needs Joss's bridge); the **Salt Flat** (the long dead-flat place used for the baseline).
- Base camp: a wagon, tents, a field desk, a cookfire. Instruments: sightglass (theodolite), chain and tape, level and staff, plane-table, field-book, a chronometer, a plumb bob.

## Arc (25 chapters)
Part One, THE BLANK SHEET (1-5): Isla in Vell; the Queen's commission; Vane's deadline (Midsummer, 40 days); the party forms; they leave.
Part Two, THE BASELINE (6-10): the Marches to the Pale; Salt Flat baseline; first triangulation; first land fixes under her hands; Joss's first bridge; Sera arrives; Vasht's ankle.
Part Three, THE TRAVERSE (11-15): Isla leads; chain across the Pale; the drifting hills; closing error; Ennis Vale's smoke; the village; Hester's story; midpoint: the valley is findable but only a perfect survey can bring it home.
Part Four, THE CLOSING ERROR (16-20): the Chancellor's fast-sheet team; Sera's corner-cut flood and recovery; levelling for the mill race; Isla's own mistake (a transposed digit), found and owned; Vasht confesses; the two maps race.
Part Five, THE HONEST LINE (21-25): the guild's benchmark error found; the gorge bridge in a rising river; the all-night closure; Midsummer before the Queen: the Chancellor's rigged sheet is exposed by checking in public; the final choices (commons, free ferry, mill race); epilogue.

## Themes and motifs
Honesty in work; owning mistakes; showing your work (the open field-book); inherited blame; precision as love; "measure it twice, say it once"; triangles (strong shapes); the Pole Star as the fixed point; Isla's crossed pencils; the sound of land settling; porridge.

## Things to avoid
Horror, gore, menace to children, preachiness, info-dumps longer than a page, romance beyond hand-holding and a kiss near the end, real-world brand/place names, invented "ancient" foreign words, modern slang. No villain violence. No guns. Do not mention "magic school", "wizard".
