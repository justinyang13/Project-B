# PART ONE: THE BLANK SHEET

## 1. The Leaning Chimney
Synopsis: On the roof of the Meridian Guild, Isla Corrin discovers that the Royal Mint chimney is not leaning, the city's benchmark seems off by a hair, and she reports it to a patient old teacher who tells her to check it three more times.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, First Saying: A line is only as honest as the hand that holds it.*
1. Open on the Guild roof at dawn, Isla flat on her stomach at a brass sightglass, deciding whether the Mint's chimney leans or the whole city of Vell does; the pencils in her hair, her ink-stained fingers, the morning bells; the day she will learn whether the Guild will keep her on.
2. The Guild's world: fixing, loose land, the great Atlas; she is the only scholarship apprentice, a ferry-chain maker's daughter among gentry; Dorian Pike, a smug third-year, mocks her for reading the same angle four times.
3. She descends to the Atlas Room (sheets tall as doors, brass rails, the hush) and finds her teacher Master Orrin Vasht asleep under his hat with a pencil in the band; he wakes and listens to her reading without comment.
4. Vasht makes her take the reading again with the second sightglass, then from the other tower; the numbers agree to the width of a hair; he says the chimney is straight and the city's old benchmark may be a hair wrong; she is thrilled and terrified in equal measure; he says "A hair is a very small thing and a very large one."
5. A page boy arrives breathless: the Queen has sent for Master Vasht at noon, and for his apprentice; Isla glances at her stained coat, then at the Atlas Room's one blank sheet at the end of the long shelf, the Unmade; she has a sudden feeling that her life has just been drawn without her.

## 2. The Commission
Synopsis: Queen Cassia and the Lord Chancellor commission Master Vasht to chart the Unmade by Midsummer, forty days away, and Vasht insists Isla come as his apprentice.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Second Saying: Ask what the land is for before you ask what it is.*
1. The walk to the palace: Vell's streets in the order of their surveying (Isla points out which were fixed first), Vasht limping with his stick and complaining about the cobbles, the palace gates, a footman's disdain at her coat.
2. The Queen's study: a plain room with a blue-draped map table; Queen Cassia, direct and dry; Lord Chancellor Aldous Vane, pearl-grey, gold-topped cane; the Queen explains that the Unmade, a wide stretch of loose country, drifts nearer the Marches every year, taking farms.
3. Vane wants it done by Midsummer, in forty days, for the harvest levy and because the Marches farmers are leaving; Vasht says a true survey of that size takes a year; Vane suggests a "serviceable sheet", an estimated survey; Vasht: "Serviceable is what we call a bridge before the flood."
4. The Queen asks what the Guild needs; Vasht says a baseline, three pairs of honest hands and no interference; she gives him the commission and a hundred crowns, and Vane adds one of his own people, Sera Quillon, to "assist" later; the Queen asks to see the field-book at the end, a request that makes Vane's smile thin.
5. Outside, Vasht tells Isla he's chosen her because she checks things three times; she says her family name is unwelcome at the Guild; Vasht does not answer, which she notices; she writes to her father in Corrin Ferry from the palace steps.

## 3. Mags and the Pot
Synopsis: Isla and Vasht gather the expedition: Mags Okoro the chain-bearer from the river barges and Joss Harrowgate, a bridge-wright's apprentice borrowed for the rough ground.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Third Saying: A chain is only as long as its weakest link, and a crew is only as good as its quietest member.*
1. Packing night in the Guild yard: crates of chains, tapes, plumb bobs, two sightglasses wrapped in felt, a level and staff, a wagon with a cranky mule named Ledger; Vasht reading the list aloud; Isla recounts every item and finds one missing.
2. Mags Okoro arrives from the river docks: fifteen, teal jacket, blue scarf, an iron pot tied to her pack; she can add a column of figures in her head faster than Isla can find her pencil; she announces she will cook, carry and count, "in that order of importance".
3. Mags tests Isla with an arithmetic riddle about a barge, a lock and a tide; Isla answers it correctly, and then shows the working; Mags: "You're the sort who shows the working." "Always." "Good. I'm the sort who checks."
4. Joss Harrowgate arrives late, soaked, with a coil of rope and a half-eaten pie, sent by a bridge-wright master who owes Vasht a favor; he cheerfully admits he knows nothing about surveying but a great deal about holding things up; he looks at Isla's pencils and says they would make a decent truss.
5. Night: the three apprentices in the yard under the stars with the wagon packed; Isla finds the Pole Star and explains to Mags how a surveyor finds true north; Joss makes a joke about getting lost; Vasht, listening from the doorway, says quietly that getting lost is the beginning of a map.

## 4. The Pale Road
Synopsis: The party travels from Vell to the Marches, learning each other's habits, and sees the first evidence of the loose land.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Fourth Saying: The country does not care what you think of it. Measure it anyway.*
1. Departure from Vell at dawn, the wagon creaking over the old stone bridge; Isla looks back at the Guild domes shining like brass buttons; Mags sings a barge song about a lock-keeper who wouldn't open; Ledger the mule refuses a hill.
2. The road through the fixed farmland: hedgerows straight as ruled lines, barns standing square; Vasht teaches the party to read a landscape: field boundaries as old survey lines, a milestone as a benchmark; Joss admires a stone arch bridge and recites its load path.
3. At a Marches inn, farmers talk about drifting: a barn that moved forty yards in a year, a pond that wandered into a neighbor's field; a woman has lost her farm to the fog; Isla's resolve.
4. A first sight of the Pale from Cairn Fell: a vast soft plain of pale gold haze, hills adrift like boats at anchor; the wonder of it; Mags goes quiet; Joss: "It looks like the world forgot to finish."
5. Camp on the fell: stew, Vasht's story of the Guild's founding sheet; Isla spots a faint line of lights far inside the Pale, a thin thread of smoke, and says nothing, because she is not yet sure she saw it; she writes it in the field-book with the time.

## 5. The Edge of the Sheet
Synopsis: They descend to the border of the Pale and find that the first test of the Guild's ink shows how loose land behaves; Isla draws her first true line, and a hillock obeys.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Fifth Saying: Write it down at the moment you see it. Memory is a flatterer.*
1. Dawn at the edge: the fixed land ends at a ditch and a line of white stones, and beyond it the ground looks like water at rest; Vasht tests it with a stone: the stone sinks a finger deep and rises slowly, as if the ground is thinking.
2. Vasht teaches the first rule: a map fixes land only if its numbers are honest and checked; he shows the ink, made from oak gall and river iron and a pinch of salt; Isla makes a small test sheet of a nearby hillock, measuring it twice and then a third way.
3. The first test, tense and quiet: Isla sets up the plane-table, draws the hillock's outline and seals the sheet with her thumb and the guild stamp; nothing happens for a long moment; Joss mutters; then the hillock settles with a sound like a long breath, a ripple of light crossing the grass.
4. Mags whoops; Joss is awed and promptly tests the new hillock by jumping on it; Vasht is moved but only says "Again, with the second hillock, and this time lie to me by a hand's width"; the lie makes the second hillock sag like a deflating cushion, which proves the rule.
5. Vasht lets Isla choose the party's first baseline site; she chooses the Salt Flat she saw on the old sheets, thirty miles in; that night she writes to her father and then, by lamplight, draws a tiny triangle in the margin of her field-book and looks at it for a long time.

# PART TWO: THE BASELINE

## 6. Thirty Miles of Nothing
Synopsis: Crossing the Pale to the Salt Flat tests the party's nerves and navigation; Isla uses the stars and the compass declination to keep them from drifting in circles.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Sixth Saying: A compass points to somewhere, but the Pole Star points to the truth.*
1. The first day inside the Pale: haze like warm milk, no horizon, the wagon wheels on soft ground; Ledger the mule balks; Joss ties a rope between wagon and tent pole to keep everyone together.
2. Midday: the compass needle wanders; Vasht explains magnetic declination and asks Isla to find true north from the sun's shadow with a plumb-line and a stick; she does it and then confirms it at dusk with the Pole Star.
3. Mags counts paces beside the wagon wheel; at the second night she notices that their trail has curved a quarter-turn and they have been circling a drifting hill; Isla checks the compass, the star and the pace-count; all three disagree, and she has to decide which to trust.
4. She chooses the star and reasons aloud: the compass is pulled by something in the loose land, the paces were counted on moving ground, but the star is far away and cannot be pushed; Vasht, hiding a smile, says nothing; Joss: "So the thing furthest away is the one that doesn't lie."
5. They corrected course by the star; at dawn the Salt Flat appears like a pale sheet of glass at the edge of the haze; Isla stands at its edge feeling for the first time that a dead-flat line a mile long is the most beautiful thing she has ever seen.

## 7. The Baseline
Synopsis: Measuring a one-mile baseline twice, in the cold, with chain and tape, is the dull, essential heart of the whole survey; Isla and Mags develop a rhythm and Joss builds a tension frame.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Seventh Saying: Everything is built on one line. Get the line right.*
1. Vasht explains the plan: one baseline measured so well that every other distance in the Unmade can be computed from it; the party sets pegs every hundred links; Joss builds a tripod tension frame to hold the chain at a constant pull with a spring balance.
2. The work: Mags carries the chain, Isla reads and records, Vasht checks, Joss levels each peg; the dull rhythm of "Chain! Mark! Tally!"; the cold; the sun glare off the salt; jokes to stay awake.
3. Temperature correction: the steel chain expands in the noon heat and contracts at dawn; Vasht has Isla record the thermometer at each stretch and apply a correction; Mags realizes the first half-mile is 0.3 links short and is delighted by the arithmetic.
4. They finish at dusk, then Vasht says they will do it again tomorrow in the opposite direction; Joss groans; Isla recognizes that two honest measures that agree are worth more than one that is perfect.
5. The second measurement: the numbers differ by the width of two fingers over a mile; Isla and Mags stay up to find why and discover a peg set a link off because of a salt crust; they correct it; Vasht: "Now you have a baseline. Not before."

## 8. Triangles
Synopsis: With the baseline fixed, Isla begins triangulation, reading angles to far hilltops and learning to build a strong figure; Joss builds a signal tower on a hummock.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Eighth Saying: Three angles that add to two right angles are the only gossip worth believing.*
1. Vasht explains triangulation to the apprentices with string and pegs on the salt: given one side and two angles, the rest of the triangle must follow; Mags works out the sine rule on a flat stone while Isla sets the sightglass.
2. Joss builds three signal towers on the nearest hummocks, each a bundle of poles with a bright cloth that can be seen at ten miles; he insists on a braced triangle at the base ("a triangle is the only shape that cannot be pushed out of itself").
3. Isla reads the angles at the first station: three hours of patient work, each angle read in both faces of the instrument and the readings averaged; Mags records; they find that one triangle sums to 180 degrees and two minutes of arc, and Isla spends a long time wondering where the two minutes went.
4. Vasht explains a thin triangle magnifies errors: they must choose the stations so that no angle is under thirty degrees; Isla redraws the whole network on the plane-table to plan a "strong figure"; Joss says it looks like a cat's cradle; Vasht says it is better than a cat's cradle.
5. First computed distance: Mags works out that the hill with the red flag is eleven miles and 214 links away; Isla measures the distance to the next station by pacing as a loose check and finds it matches within thirty yards; the unreality of knowing the land by thought alone.

## 9. A Hill Is Held
Synopsis: Isla fixes a drifting hill by surveying it properly, and the first small piece of the Unmade becomes solid; the effect of her choices on the shape of the land begins to show.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Ninth Saying: Where the land has no shape, you will be asked what shape it should have.*
1. A tall drifting hill appears on the horizon, creeping a few paces a day; Vasht says it must be fixed before they move on or all their triangulation will wander; the party moves to its foot.
2. The detailed survey of the hill: contour lines by levelling, Joss and Mags holding the staff, Isla reading backsight and foresight; Vasht teaches contouring (lines of equal height); she realizes the hill is about two hundred feet high, a gentle slope on one side and a cliff on the other.
3. Drawing the sheet: Isla notices that the hill has no shape on its north face (the loose land has not decided); she must choose, and she draws a gentle slope with a spring at the foot so the farmers' cattle could drink; Vasht observes the choice in silence.
4. She seals the sheet and the hill settles, its north slope turning to grass and a thin silver spring appearing at its foot; Mags tastes the water and pronounces it "sweet, like a secret"; Joss laughs at how pleased Isla looks.
5. That night Vasht tells her that every sheet she seals is a promise, that someone will live on the hill she shaped, and that she should never draw anything she isn't willing to live under; she writes this in the margin of her field-book and underlines it twice.

## 10. A Message and an Ankle
Synopsis: Sera Quillon arrives with the Chancellor's letters, and the party's first accident leaves Vasht with a broken ankle; Isla is suddenly responsible.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Tenth Saying: When the master cannot stand, the apprentice must.*
1. A rider on a lathered horse emerges from the haze: Sera Quillon in plum-purple velvet and polished boots, carrying the Chancellor's letter of "assistance" and a stack of sealed sheets; she is gracious and cool, and she asks which of them is in charge.
2. Sera inspects the baseline and the triangulation notes with raised eyebrows; she says she could have done the whole thing in a week with estimates; Isla bristles; Vasht, with dry courtesy, asks her to take the next hill's angles and to show her working.
3. Sera takes the angles quickly and neatly and gets a different answer by four seconds of arc; Isla points out she didn't read in both faces; Sera says it makes no practical difference; Vasht says that is the sentence under every collapsed bridge.
4. The accident: while Joss and Vasht are testing a wagon ramp across a ditch, a plank gives (a flaw Joss missed and instantly owns up to); Vasht falls and breaks his ankle; Mags splints it with a tent pole and a scarf; the ordinary fear and the way everyone steadies.
5. Vasht, pale and pragmatic, has the wagon turned into a field hospital and base camp; he tells Isla in front of everyone that the survey is hers now, the Queen's commission naming "Master Vasht or his designated hand"; Sera's face does something complicated; Isla says: "I'll need to see the plan again." Vasht: "You wrote it."

# PART THREE: THE TRAVERSE

## 11. Chain of Command
Synopsis: Isla learns that leading is not the same as measuring; she must delegate, decide, and bear the weight of the others' trust, with Sera and Joss pulling different ways.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Eleventh Saying: The loudest voice on the hill is rarely the one holding the plumb line.*
1. Morning after the accident: a camp with a lamed master, a mule who despises Sera, and three apprentices each assuming someone else will make the porridge; Isla draws up the day's duty list and discovers that it takes longer to write it than to do it.
2. Sera suggests splitting the party to double speed; Isla refuses because the traverse needs the same instrument eyes; Joss, tactfully, proposes a compromise: two crews that swap instruments and check each other's field-books at noon; Isla realizes the compromise is better than both plans.
3. Vasht, from his chair, can only advise by notes; he sends her a letter every night, always one question: "What are you least sure of?"; she begins to answer honestly and finds that the answers are the work.
4. A small crisis: Mags's tape has been mis-stretched and gives a 2-foot error over a half mile; the blame wants to fall on Mags; Isla instead takes it as a procedure failure and installs a spring balance at every pull; Mags, relieved, says she'll stop apologizing if Isla stops apologizing for her.
5. Evening: Sera, off alone, laughs at the camp's slowness, and Isla overhears her reading aloud from a private letter to the Chancellor, a report on their pace; Isla says nothing but starts keeping a second copy of every field-book page in her coat.

## 12. The Drifting Hills
Synopsis: The traverse runs across a stretch of loose hills that creep during the survey itself; Isla must find a way to measure a moving thing.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twelfth Saying: To measure what moves, measure what moves with it.*
1. Entering the drift: hills that creep a pace an hour, a stream that changes course while they eat lunch; Joss's cairns shift overnight; Mags names the hills after her aunts.
2. The problem: a sight to a flag on a moving hill changes between readings; Isla thinks it through with Joss's pegs and Mags's pace-counts; she reasons that the hills move together as a slab, so relative distances stay true even if absolute ones do not.
3. The method: they set a fixed ring of pegs on the stable salt-clay below and measure every hilltop relative to the ring by simultaneous sighting, with Mags and Joss on the far hills signalling with mirrors at set minutes; the timing, the heliograph flashes, the thrill of a plan working.
4. A comic setback: Ledger the mule wanders onto a drifting patch and rides a hillock a quarter mile in forty minutes without moving; they retrieve him by a rope relay; Sera, forced to hold the rope, gets her boots muddy and half-laughs against her will.
5. At dusk they plot the hills on the sheet; for the first time all stations agree to within a yard across six miles; Isla looks at the sheet and thinks she might be good at this after all; then, at the far edge of the plot, a thin line of smoke rises where no one should be.

## 13. Smoke in the Haze
Synopsis: Isla follows the smoke and finds the hamlet of Ennis Vale, unmapped for seventy years; its people are glad, wary and astonished.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Thirteenth Saying: Do not assume that a blank on the map is a blank on the ground.*
1. Isla, Joss and Mags follow a bearing to the smoke, the haze shifting around them; they find a stone bridge, mossy and perfect, across a stream that the survey says should not exist; an apple orchard in blossom out of season.
2. The village appears: slate roofs, a mill with a slowly turning wheel, a bell tower of grey stone; children stop playing; an old man in a patched green coat with a stick, Perrin Ashdown, steps out and asks in a thin steady voice whether they are the Guild.
3. Perrin tells them the hamlet has existed for four hundred years but was "left off" seventy years ago; the roads vanished one by one, the tax collector stopped coming, the post stopped; they lived on what they could grow, and the valley has held itself together by stubbornness and a very good mill.
4. Isla's notes: the hamlet's position in her survey is within a hundred yards of the old maps' Ennis Vale, a place the Atlas lists as "dissolved"; she realizes she is standing in a place the Guild erased; she learns that every one of its 112 people has been cut off from the kingdom they belong to.
5. Perrin looks at her coat, her pencils and her compass and asks her name; when she says "Corrin," he goes very still and then smiles slowly: "Hester's child, are you? We wondered when someone would come back."

## 14. Hester
Synopsis: Perrin tells Isla about Hester Corrin, her great-grandmother, who surveyed Ennis Vale seventy years ago and was blamed for the closing error that unmade it.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Fourteenth Saying: A shame inherited is a debt nobody owes.*
1. In the mill-house kitchen, tea and oatcake, Perrin at the table; his daughter Wynne, brisk and kind, puts out a plate of apples; Perrin begins: seventy years ago a young woman surveyor came with a chain and a laugh, and stayed three months and mended the mill-race with a spirit level.
2. The survey: Hester measured the whole valley and sealed her sheet; for a week the valley shone; then a Guild inspector came and said her closing error was too great; the sheet was struck from the Atlas; the hamlet slowly slid loose; Hester was recalled and never allowed to survey again.
3. Isla's family story: she has always known her great-grandmother as "the one who made the mistake" and has hidden the name at the Guild; Perrin takes a battered field-book from a wooden chest, Hester's own, left behind in the rush; his hands tremble as he gives it to her.
4. Isla reads the pages by lamplight: a hand like her own, the same habit of writing every reading at the time; the closing error is small and honest; she finds a note in the margin, "Vell benchmark reads 2 links low? Ask Orrin"; Orrin; Master Vasht; she feels the floor tilt.
5. Mags and Joss find her still reading near midnight; Joss puts a blanket on her shoulders and says nothing; Mags says "People who write everything down don't make careless errors."; Isla says "Then someone else did", and for the first time she wonders whether she should trust the man she has loved like a grandfather.

## 15. Midpoint: The Valley Remembers
Synopsis: Isla decides to survey Ennis Vale properly and bring it home; the villagers help, and the first night of work shows how the valley wants to be found.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Fifteenth Saying: A place remembers the hands that measured it.*
1. A council in the mill-yard: Perrin, Wynne, the miller, the schoolmistress; they will lend labor, hands, ladders and the bell tower as a survey station; Isla promises nothing about the result and everything about her honesty.
2. The survey begins: villagers hold staffs and signal flags, children carry water to the surveyors, the schoolmistress turns out to be a natural at angle-reading; Mags and Wynne trade porridge recipes; Joss and the miller examine the mill-race.
3. They locate Hester's old benchmark in the church wall, a bronze disc with a tiny cross; Isla recomputes the position from the new baseline and finds it differs from Hester's by a few inches in the direction of the Vell benchmark error she suspects.
4. Sera, having followed them, stands at the edge of the orchard watching the work; she sees the bell tower used as a station and softly says "You'd need to close the traverse across the whole Unmade, not just this valley, to have this sheet hold."
5. Isla agrees; the survey of Ennis Vale cannot be sealed alone, it must tie into the wider network, which means the entire Unmade must be surveyed before Midsummer: thirty-one days; the stakes of the whole book become exactly clear and Isla, to her own surprise, feels steady.

# PART FOUR: THE CLOSING ERROR

## 16. The Fast Sheet
Synopsis: The Chancellor's surveying party arrives with a quick estimated map, offering a bribe of speed and comfort; Sera is torn.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Sixteenth Saying: A shortcut is a road that someone else will have to pay for.*
1. A caravan with the Royal crest: a dozen hired surveyors, a wagon of prefabricated pegs, a field kitchen with real eggs; the Chancellor's deputy, a smooth man named Lyle Corwin, offers to pool the work and finish by the first of the month.
2. The "fast sheet": estimated distances by pacing and eye, angles read once with cheap instruments, nothing checked; it looks beautiful, with clean contours; Isla studies it and finds that the contours put Ennis Vale at the bottom of a "reservoir".
3. Corwin explains the Chancellor's plan: a dam at the narrows to water new grain estates in the Pale; Ennis Vale would be "a regrettable inconvenience" and the villagers could be compensated with money and cottages in Vell.
4. Isla understands what is at stake in her choices about the shape of the land and tells Corwin she will not seal a sheet she has not checked; he laughs politely and says the Queen will be given the sheet that is finished.
5. That night Sera finds her at the cookfire and tells her, quietly and a little furiously, that she has seen the fast sheet and knows it is wrong; Sera was taught that a map must be beautiful and quick; she says she doesn't know any other way to be good; Isla says "Then let me teach you the slow way, and you can tell me if it was worth it."

## 17. Sera's Valley
Synopsis: Sera, trying to prove herself, cuts a corner on the northern valley and the result is a lopsided flooded dell; she and Isla fix it together.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Seventeenth Saying: The honest fix is the one you announce.*
1. Sera takes a crew to survey the northern valley and, short of time, estimates one leg by eye; she seals it with confidence at dawn.
2. The valley settles lopsided, one side three feet lower than the other, and the stream pools into a muddy lake swallowing a farmer's turnip field; the farmer, a gruff woman named Hobb, stands in boots with her arms folded; Sera, white-faced, says "It's mine. I did it."
3. Isla goes to look, does not scold; she and Sera work out how the error propagated: a single estimated leg skewed the bearings of all that followed by eleven minutes of arc; Joss shows how a small tilt in a base becomes a large lean in a tower.
4. The repair: Isla teaches Sera to re-measure the leg with chain and level, then to "unseal" a sheet by redrawing it with a small correction and an apology to the land; the valley sighs and rights itself; Hobb is not satisfied until Sera helps dig out her field.
5. Sera, muddy to the thighs, laughs for the first time; she asks Isla how she bears the slowness; Isla says "I don't. I just can't bear the other thing." Sera adopts the field-book habit and becomes, for the first time, a member of the crew rather than a visitor.

## 18. The Fall of the Water
Synopsis: Joss and Isla use levelling to design a mill-race and a channel for Ennis Vale, discovering that the water's fall is the key to the whole valley's future.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Eighteenth Saying: Water is the only honest surveyor. It always finds the level.*
1. Joss and Isla walk the valley's stream with a level and staff, measuring the fall over two miles: ninety-one feet, the old mill-race took it in a single long arc; Joss explains why a mill needs a steady head of water and Isla works out the gradient, 1 in 1,500, needed for a canal without erosion.
2. Wynne shows them the old race, half-silted, and the miller's dream of a new wheel; Joss sketches an overshot wheel and a sluice gate; Isla suggests a small spillway so that floods go around the village rather than through it.
3. Late-night calculation in the mill-house: the two of them, shoulders almost touching, working out how much water the race will carry; the stub of candle, the ink on her nose; Joss gently wipes it off with his thumb and goes quiet.
4. Morning: an argument over whether to take the race along the east or west side of the valley; the east is shorter, the west keeps the orchard dry; Isla chooses the west, longer by half a mile, because "somebody will live under that orchard"; Joss, smiling, says he was hoping she would.
5. They stake the line: Mags and Perrin's grandchildren carry stakes; a long line of white flags marching down the valley; Perrin watches and says it looks like a procession; Isla thinks she has never been so happy or so tired.

## 19. What Master Vasht Wrote
Synopsis: Vasht, brought forward in the wagon, confesses to Isla that he was the junior clerk who struck Hester's sheet from the Atlas; their relationship is tested.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Nineteenth Saying: Confession is only a map of where you went wrong. It is not the road back.*
1. Vasht arrives at Ennis Vale on a litter pulled by Ledger, with a bundle of papers; he looks at the village with an expression Isla cannot read; Perrin takes one look at him and says "Orrin Vasht. You've gone grey."
2. Alone in the mill-house, Vasht tells it: he was nineteen, a Guild clerk, when the inspector's report came; the Guild's own benchmark in Vell had been reset two years earlier and nobody told the field parties; he noticed the discrepancy and was told to say nothing; he signed the report because he was young and afraid.
3. Isla's anger: she says she trusted him with everything; she says he let her carry her family's shame while knowing its cause; Vasht does not defend himself; he says only "I chose you to take this journey because I wanted someone honest to find it out."
4. Isla walks out into the orchard, angry and crying; Joss follows at a distance and sits on a stump without speaking; after a long time she says "He should have told me." Joss: "Yes. And he's also the person who taught you to check things three times, which is how you caught him."
5. She returns and says she will forgive him when the survey is closed, not before; Vasht nods, relieved and chastened; she asks him to teach her the Guild's inspection procedures so that the final sheet cannot be disputed; the two begin planning the final closure in the cramped light of one lamp.

## 20. The Transposed Digit
Synopsis: Isla's own error, a transposed digit in the field-book, is discovered by Mags; Isla owns it publicly and the honesty saves the survey.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twentieth Saying: The error you find is a gift. The error you hide is a trap.*
1. Computing the western half of the traverse, Mags notices a leg that doesn't match: the field-book says 4,321 links but the computed total demands 4,231; she checks three times and finally says "Isla, I think it's yours."
2. Isla stares at her own handwriting; the horror and the cold; the transposed digits have run through two days of work, three stations and a sealed hillside; her first instinct is to quietly correct it and hope nobody notices.
3. She thinks of Hester and of Vasht and of every secret in the Guild; she gathers the party and the villagers in the mill-yard and says exactly what she did wrong, how she found it, and what will be done; Sera, astonished, says "You just told everybody."
4. The repair takes a night and a day: re-observing two stations, adjusting the traverse using the honest sharing of the closing error across all the legs; Perrin's schoolmistress checks every figure; the village's trust, instead of falling, grows.
5. At dawn the traverse closes to within four feet over forty-one miles; Vasht, studying the new figures, says that it is the best closure he has ever seen; Isla says it is only so good because it was checked by eleven people; Vasht: "That is exactly why."

# PART FIVE: THE HONEST LINE

## 21. The Benchmark in Vell
Synopsis: To prove Hester's work was right, Isla and Sera travel to Vell by night to check the Guild's own benchmark; they find the error and a witness.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twenty-First Saying: The proof is always in the place where nobody has looked.*
1. Isla realizes that the entire network hangs from one reference: the Guild's benchmark brass disc in the Atlas Room floor; if it is two links low, as Hester's marginal note suspected, every sheet drawn from it has been slightly skewed; she asks Sera to come with her to Vell.
2. The night ride: Sera on her tall horse, Isla on a borrowed pony; Sera confesses that she was never actually good, only quick; Isla says speed is a talent and honesty is a discipline and one can have both; Sera has the grace not to argue.
3. The Guild after hours: the vaulted Atlas Room silent, a night porter who knows Isla and lets them in; the bronze disc in the floor; they set up a sightglass and a steel tape between the disc and the four old reference towers.
4. The discovery: the disc has been shifted by a hair over the decades by the building settling, and the Guild's papers show a record of its reset two years before Hester's survey; the old records in a locked drawer confirm that Hester's sheet was correct and the inspection wrong; Dorian Pike, working late, surprises them and in a moment of decency says he saw nothing.
5. They copy the records with a witness signature from the night porter; Sera whispers "It's real, isn't it? She was right." Isla: "She was right. Now we have to get it to the Queen." They ride back in the dawn with a satchel of proof and ten days to Midsummer.

## 22. Gantry Gorge
Synopsis: The final sheet requires crossing Gantry Gorge to close the traverse; Joss designs and builds a truss bridge in a race against rising water.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twenty-Second Saying: A bridge is a promise made out of triangles.*
1. The last unclosed gap: a three-hundred-foot gorge with a river at the bottom, needed for the survey's final tie; Joss surveys the abutments with Isla, calculates the span and proposes a timber truss with falsework; a plan sketched on the back of a map in charcoal.
2. Building: Mags runs the supply line, the villagers of Ennis Vale bring oak beams from the mill; Joss explains why each member of the truss is in tension or compression and gives each volunteer a role; Perrin, ancient, insists on holding a rope end.
3. Rain on the high fells: the river rises; the falsework must be cleared before the flood; Isla takes continuous level readings of the river against a staff; the number climbs.
4. A tense night: the keystone-equivalent, the last truss member, must be set by morning; Joss and the miller's sons haul it by block and tackle; Isla and Mags call out the river height every ten minutes; the member drops into place with a deep wooden knock.
5. Dawn: the bridge stands, the falsework is knocked clear, and the river roars under it; Joss insists on a load test, walking the loaded wagon across at a crawl with Ledger blindfolded and furious; the bridge does not even creak; Isla kisses him on the cheek in front of everybody; he turns the color of a beet.

## 23. The Long Night of Closing
Synopsis: With the gorge crossed, Isla must read the final angles and compute the closure of the whole traverse before sunrise on the eve of Midsummer; Sera and Mags do the arithmetic in relays.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twenty-Third Saying: The last reading is the same as the first. Give it all your attention.*
1. The last station on the far side of the gorge: Isla sets up the sightglass at dusk, feeling the whole survey resting on this one reading; Vasht, in his chair, tells her to read as if it is the first.
2. The computation: eleven people in the mill-house with slates and field-books; Sera, Mags and the schoolmistress each computing a third of the legs, then trading to check; the long rhythm of numbers, tea and candles; Joss drawing tiny triangles in the margins to keep Isla awake.
3. At midnight the figures disagree by two feet; Sera finds the cause in a forgotten convergence correction and, for once, takes the slow route to prove it by hand; the group holds its breath and the numbers come into line.
4. Closing error: 3.1 feet over 63 miles; the slimmest margin; Vasht, on his feet for the first time on his bad ankle, reads the total, and silently, solemnly takes off his hat.
5. Isla draws the final sheet by lamplight with her steady hand: the road, the river, the commons, the ferry, the mill-race, the reservoir that is not there; she sets the question aloud to herself, "What is this land for?"; she decides, and draws every line as if someone's life would be lived under it.

## 24. Midsummer in Vell
Synopsis: Before the Queen and the Court, the Chancellor's fast sheet and Isla's true sheet are laid side by side, and Isla invites the Queen to check them in public.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twenty-Fourth Saying: Show your work. A truth that cannot be checked is only an opinion with good posture.*
1. The Great Hall of Vell on Midsummer morning: nobles, Guild masters, farmers from the Marches in their best clothes, Perrin and Wynne from Ennis Vale in a corner; Isla in her mustard coat, with Vasht on his stick and Joss, Mags and Sera at her shoulder; the two sheets on a long table.
2. The Chancellor presents the fast sheet, eloquent and beautiful: a reservoir, grain estates and a grand road; the Queen listens; Vane pronounces Isla's survey "a charming hobby of unregistered apprentices".
3. Isla asks leave to answer; she lays down Hester's field-book, the Guild's own records of the benchmark, and her full set of field-books with all the corrections; she says that she will not ask the Queen to believe her but to check it; Queen Cassia calls for the Royal Surveyor and a level.
4. The public test: in the Hall, the Royal Surveyor sets up a level, reads two known points, then the same two points on both sheets; the fast sheet is out by thirty feet; Isla's is within the width of a thumb; the Chancellor's rigged benchmarks are named; murmurs; the Queen asks Vane a pointed question about the dam's financing.
5. Vasht gives his testimony about Hester Corrin's sheet and the benchmark, and his own part in it; the Queen's expression; the Guild Master, ashen, rises to say the Guild will reopen the sheet; the Hall hums; Isla finds she has been gripping Joss's hand without noticing.

## 25. What the Land Is For
Synopsis: The Queen seals Isla's sheet and the Unmade becomes land; the choices Isla made shape the new country; the Guild is changed; the final image is of the first sunrise over a fixed Ennis Vale.
Header: *The Surveyor's Catechism, Twenty-Fifth Saying: Draw as if someone will have to live there. Someone will.*
1. The sealing ceremony in the Hall: the Queen's seal beside Isla's thumb; the ripple of light that crosses the world; far away in the Pale, forty-one miles of fog settle with a long, enormous breath, and the Hall seems to smell of rain and orchards.
2. The Queen's ruling: Ennis Vale restored to the Atlas with the Guild's apology and Hester Corrin's sheet reinstated; the commons, the free ferry and the mill-race are fixed in law; the Chancellor's estates are struck; Vane leaves without a word, leaning on his gold cane; Sera is made Deputy Royal Surveyor.
3. The Guild's reform: Isla is offered a master's chair and a title; she declines the title and asks instead for open apprenticeship for any child who can show a good field-book; the Guild Master, after a long breath, agrees; Dorian Pike is the first to sign the petition.
4. The journey home to Ennis Vale in a procession: the bridge at Gantry Gorge now hung with ribbons, Perrin tolling the old bell for the first time in seventy years, the kingdom's first letter in seventy years delivered to the mill; Isla stands in the orchard and reads Hester's last marginal note: "If someone finds this, the numbers were good."
5. Epilogue at sunrise on the bell-tower: Isla, Joss, Mags and Sera on the parapet, Vasht below on his stick, Ledger the mule eating an apple tree's worth of blossom; Isla sets up the sightglass and finds the Pole Star fading into dawn; she says a land is a promise made of true numbers; Joss says something clumsy and kind; Mags says porridge is ready; the last line: "She wrote the time in her field-book, and the date, and underneath, in her steadiest hand, her name."
