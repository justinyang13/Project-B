# THE WIZARD SCHOOL DROPOUT CLUB — story bible

Joyful comedy fantasy for ages 10-12: a funny, warm, fast, laugh-out-loud picture-book classic with a big heart. A wizard school full of perfect students, a shed full of "failures", and a plot twist where the failures' "useless" quirks turn out to be the only magic that can save everybody. Slapstick, running gags, silly names, cozy food, a goose who used to be a hat. No gore, no death, no romance, no spooky or ghost content, no real danger to children: the "peril" is a funny curse (everyone turns into garden gnomes) that is never frightening. Everyone is restored by the end.

MAIN GOAL OF THE BOOK: make the reader laugh and fall in love with reading. Enjoyment first; the lesson is dramatized by the characters' choices, never preached.

LESSON: **What other people call your flaw may be your tool. Confidence comes from within; you don't need to be "normal" to belong or to matter. Find your people.** (Also covers: never let anyone tell you that you can't; "wrong" is just right in a different direction.) One earned moment of plain statement is allowed near the end (Toby, ch17; Marnie, ch20).

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, a chatty, dry, affectionate narrator close on Marnie, in the tradition of Roald Dahl, Terry Pratchett's young-reader books and Lemony-Snicket-without-the-gloom: funny asides, absurd precise numbers ("fourteen toes"), lists, understatement, the occasional direct address to the reader ("you may think..."). Short paragraphs, quick dialogue, one joke per paragraph at most, and a plain sincere beat when something hurts. Each chapter opens with one italic line, then a blank line: "*From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule N: ...*" The rules are silly on first read and wise on the second; each chapter dramatizes its rule. Rule 20 is a warm rewrite of the idea behind Rule 1.
Voice sample:
"*From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 1: A hat is only a hat until it is a goose.*

Marnie Pennywhistle was expelled from Humbleton Academy of Practical Wizardry at fourteen minutes past nine on a Tuesday morning, which she thought was rude of them, because she hadn't even had breakfast.

To be fair, there was a goose involved."

## Cast (fixed looks, distinct hair/top/footwear for each)
- **Marnie Pennywhistle, 11.** Hero. Freckled, light-brown skin, wild copper-red hair in two puffy buns with a pencil stuck in one, mustard-yellow cardigan with mismatched buttons under a forest-green school cloak with a crooked silver clasp, red high-top sneakers. Her spells never do what the book says: a candle spell makes a fish, a levitation spell makes a teacher's moustache stand up. Warm, stubborn, quick-mouthed, ashamed of being "wrong", secretly wants one correct spell. Wild-card transmutation: anything she aims at turns into something else, usually a small animal.
- **Gerald, the goose.** Plump white goose, orange beak, a purple-and-silver-star ribbon (the hatband) around his neck, a permanent look of offended dignity. He was Principal Thistlewood's tall purple hat until Marnie's spell. Steals pencils and socks, honks at the worst moment, loyal. Twist: he is the Founder's Hat (see below). Plants: ch5 he flickers into a hat for one heartbeat; ch12 he is never in the Principal's office when the Hat is mentioned.
- **Tobias "Toby" Abara, 11.** Dark-brown skin, short black hair, big round gold glasses, navy knitted vest over a white shirt, red bow tie, grey trousers, polished brown lace-up shoes. His spells work perfectly BACKWARDS ("Illuminate!" puts the light out; "Extinguish!" lights it). Careful, precise, dry, a list-maker, deeply lonely until the club. He has worked out that his backwards magic is the most consistent in the school.
- **Lulu Vasquez, 10.** Tan skin, a black braid down to her knees with a pink ribbon, bright orange hoodie, two mismatched socks (one striped, one polka-dot) and teal rain boots. Youngest student ever admitted. Every spell she casts conjures socks (always mismatched, always lavender-scented, always in a heap). Apologizes constantly ("I'm so sorry"), generous, secretly brave.
- **Bunt, a young troll, "nearly ten".** Grey-green skin, nine feet tall, a tuft of moss-green hair, a patched brown vest the size of a tent, enormous boots made from two old barrels, a red-spotted handkerchief as big as a tablecloth. Gentle, slow-talking, loves biscuits. Allergic to magic: near a spell his nose itches, "ah... ah... AH-CHOO!", and the spell fizzles. Humbleton's Assistant Groundskeeper.
- **Gran Pennywhistle.** Round, rosy, grey bun with a broom-straw stuck in it, red-rimmed spectacles, a lavender apron with pockets full of twine and nails. Runs Pennywhistle's Broom Mending in the village of Dimblewick. Delighted by the goose. Wisdom: "A spell is like a sneeze. You can't tell it where to go."
- **Principal Ambrose Thistlewood.** Tall, thin, curled white moustache, purple robe with silver stars. Pompous, kind underneath, terrified of looking foolish. Wore a tall purple hat with silver stars for forty years. In gnome form: squeaky and furious.
- **Cassius Pomeroy, 12.** Leader of the Golden Circle (the school's top students, in gold-trimmed cloaks). Pale, perfect slicked golden hair, crisp royal-blue blazer with gold buttons, shiny black shoes, silver wand. Not evil: terrified of making a mistake. Politely cruel ("How cozy. Like a hamster cage."). His Perfectly Perfect Spell triggers the Gnoming. Apologizes stiffly and then, unexpectedly, sincerely.
- Golden Circle: Dahlia (tall, braids with gold beads) and Rex (round, big glasses), Cassius's echoes.
- **Professor Quillfeather.** Gnome librarian, owlish, squeaky, helpful. **Mayor Fenwick** of Dimblewick (tall pink hat, big sash). **Mrs. Humphreys**, the baker (flour on everything).
- **Gnomes (the cursed):** two feet tall, pointed red hat, white beard (even the children), bright blue jacket, shiny painted-clay look, a fixed cheerful smile. Alive and talking, but they cannot cast spells and move in short waddles.

## World and rules
- Dimblewick: a hillside village of crooked chimneys; a bakery whose bread rises by itself; a lamppost that announces the time; Pennywhistle's Broom Mending at the bottom of Crumb Lane. Humbleton Academy of Practical Wizardry: on the hill above, a big, tidy, golden-stone school with a Great Hall, the Great Library, the Founders' Fountain in the central courtyard with a stone statue of the founder, Wilhelmina Humbleton, bareheaded, and at the back the kitchen garden with the old Potting Shed (the club's headquarters: flowerpots, a pot-bellied stove, rafters carved with dropout names back 300 years, "Ermintrude, 1743: turned my own nose into a turnip").
- The school's motto: "Precision Above All." Teachers call spells "correct" or "incorrect".
- THE GNOMING (the curse): At the Founders' Day Ceremony, Cassius casts his Perfectly Perfect Spell, meant to make everything at Humbleton flawless. It has no balance: everything perfect starts turning into garden gnomes. The hex then spreads, in a slow gold glitter wave, whenever ANYONE casts a spell perfectly correctly. It does NOT affect wonky magic. After 72 hours it becomes permanent.
- THE FOUNDER'S HAT (the twist): The Principal's tall purple hat is secretly the school's Founder's Hat, enchanted by Wilhelmina Humbleton to soak up the overflow of perfect magic and keep things balanced. For forty years it quietly did. Marnie's wild spell turned it into Gerald, so Cassius's spell had nothing to catch it (his replacement hat was an ordinary one) and the Gnoming happened. The Founding Charter says: "Only magic that is a little wrong can balance magic that is entirely right." Fix: restore Gerald to the Hat (needs Toby's backwards version of Marnie's own spell), place it crookedly on the founder's statue while the wave hits (Bunt's biggest sneeze cancels the wave's perfection, Lulu's socks soak up the glitter, Marnie places the hat; her sideways "Straighten!" tilts the hat jauntily and it holds). Every gnome pops back.
- The goose cannot be reversed by Marnie's own perfect spell (ch16 mistake).
- Magic words are plain English with silly flourishes, not Latin pastiche beyond "Perfectus" for Cassius.

## Arc (see outline.md for the 100 beats)
Part One: The Goose Incident (ch1-5): Marnie expelled; meets Toby, Lulu, Bunt in the Potting Shed; Dropout Club formed.
Part Two: The Dropout Club (ch6-10): club life, a runaway broom, Cassius's mockery and notebook, village odd jobs, Founders' Day: the Perfect Spell.
Part Three: The Great Gnoming (ch11-15): everyone gnomed, the wonky four are immune, the hex rules, the Great Library, the Founder's Hat revealed.
Part Four: Wonky Magic (ch16-20): Marnie's low point (drinks the Normalizing Tonic, becomes a gnome), the combined rescue, the Fountain, the Hat, a new Department of Wonky Magic; callback to the opening.

## Running gags and payoffs
- Toby's opposites ("Illuminate" / "Extinguish"); he learns to say the opposite on purpose.
- Lulu's socks and her apologies; sock counts rise ("one hundred and four socks, and none of them a pair"); final: hundreds of socks soak up the wave.
- Bunt's sneezes ("ah... ah... AH-CHOO!") and his huge handkerchief; biscuits always.
- Gerald steals things, honks at truths, hates the stove; ends as a hat on odd days.
- Marnie's accidental animals (fish, duck, a rabbit named Dust Bunny); the "goose" total.
- Principal Thistlewood's moustache lifts when he is lying.
- Cassius's hair stays perfect in wind; when it flops, he is truly shaken.
- The Handbook rules, written in chalk then in ink.

## Vocabulary and learning value (use naturally, in context)
precision, irregular, enchanted, ceremony, ridiculous, reluctant, stubborn, dignity, apologize, immune, counter-, balance, peculiar, mischievous, anxious. Facts a reader takes away: how a pulley or lever works (Bunt), what "immune" means, why balance matters (a see-saw), wool absorbs sound.

## Banned
Spooky/ghost content, romance, modern slang, brand names, any real person. Narrator moralizing. "stone in her throat", "heart hammered", "held her breath", "It wasn't X. It was Y." (max once per chapter).
