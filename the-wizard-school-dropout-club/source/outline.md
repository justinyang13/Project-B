# PART ONE: THE GOOSE INCIDENT

## 1. The Goose Incident
Synopsis: Marnie is expelled from Humbleton Academy of Practical Wizardry for turning the Principal's hat into a goose in front of the whole school.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 1: A hat is only a hat until it is a goose.*
1. Open in the Principal's office at fourteen minutes past nine on a Tuesday: Marnie Pennywhistle has just been expelled, "which she thought was rude, because she hadn't even had breakfast"; the office full of portraits that sigh, trophy hats, a clock that ticks politely, and on the desk a plump white goose in a purple star-spangled ribbon, honking at a paperweight.
2. What happened ten minutes ago at Founders' Day rehearsal in the Great Hall: the Principal's tall purple hat was crooked, Marnie only wanted to straighten it, and her spell turned it into a goose, which flew once round the Hall, knocked over a choir, bit a vice-principal's toe and landed in the punch.
3. Principal Ambrose Thistlewood, tall and thin with a curled white moustache, reads the charges: one goose, fourteen pecked toes, three flattened choirs, one punch bowl; at the door Cassius Pomeroy, golden hair perfect even indoors, watches with a smile like a freshly ironed shirt.
4. Her record: in two years her candle spell made a fish, her levitation spell made a teacher's moustache stand straight up, her "Tidy" spell made the dust bunnies into real rabbits; the Principal says the goose spell appears permanent, "which is highly irregular", and that some people are simply not suited to magic; Marnie says sorry and means it, and the sorry makes things worse.
5. She leaves with her trunk on its squeaky wheel and the goose waddling after her because nobody else will have him; Cassius says "Goodbye, Pennywhistle. Do try to stay… correct," and the big doors boom shut; the goose honks at them as if to say the school could do with a few hats of its own, and Marnie laughs for the first time that day, a small wet laugh that surprises her.

## 2. The Broom Mender
Synopsis: Marnie goes home to Dimblewick and her grandmother's broom shop, where Gran is delighted by the goose and nobody is angry, which is almost worse.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 2: Gran is always right, especially when she is wrong.*
1. The walk down the hill into Dimblewick, with crooked chimneys, a bakery whose bread rises by itself, and a lamppost that announces the time ("Twenty to ten. Cloudy. Good luck."); villagers whisper "that's the Pennywhistle girl" and "goose, they say"; the trunk wheel squeaks every second step and the goose honks at a cat.
2. Pennywhistle's Broom Mending at the bottom of Crumb Lane: brooms hanging from the ceiling like bunches of onions, a smell of straw and linseed oil; Gran, round and rosy with a grey bun and a broom-straw stuck in it, looks up from a twig bundle, sees the goose, and laughs until she has to sit on a bucket.
3. Gran: "Ambrose Thistlewood has been wearing that hat for forty years. I always said it looked like a goose." Tea and toast with too much jam; Marnie lists her whole record and Gran says a spell is like a sneeze, you can't tell it where to go; Marnie: "Everyone else's goes where they point it." Gran: "Everyone else is pointing at where it was already going."
4. The goose is named Gerald, because Gran says he has the look of a Gerald; Gerald bites Marnie's toast, hisses at the stove, and inspects the bread bin as though he's thinking of buying it; Gran refuses to hear a word about being sorry: "Sorry is for dropped eggs."
5. Night in her old attic room under the sloping beams: Marnie tries a tiny spell to light a candle and gets a very small, very confused trout in the candlestick; she cries a little into Gerald's feathers and he allows it; then she remembers her wand is in the school's confiscated-things box in the old Potting Shed, and the groundskeeper locks up at dawn, so she will have to go back tonight.

## 3. The Backwards Boy
Synopsis: Marnie sneaks into Humbleton's kitchen garden for her wand and finds Toby Abara, a boy whose spells always do exactly the opposite.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 3: If a spell goes the wrong way, check which way you were facing.*
1. Dusk: Marnie creeps through the back gate of Humbleton into the kitchen garden, past rows of sulky stone garden gnomes with painted grins, a pumpkin patch that seems to be listening, and a scarecrow that salutes her out of habit; Gerald marches ahead like an offended general.
2. A light in the old Potting Shed with the windows fogged up; through a clear corner she sees a boy in a navy knitted vest and red bow tie, round gold glasses, standing in a mountain of flowerpots saying "Illuminate!" to a lantern, which instantly goes out; "Drat. Dark again." Then "Extinguish!" and the lantern blazes.
3. He spots her, not at all alarmed: Tobias Abara, Toby to his friends, "I have one friend, so it's mostly Toby"; his spells work perfectly backwards, the most reliable magic in the school; nobody has ever given him a grade for it; he's been hiding in the shed since the Spring Exam when he tried to levitate a feather and sank the examiner's chair through the floor.
4. Marnie finds her wand in the confiscated box; Toby asks her to demonstrate; she aims it at a flowerpot and says "Straighten"; the pot turns into a very small, very offended duck; Gerald honks with superiority; Toby is delighted: "You're not even backwards. You're sideways! That's far more interesting."
5. Toby tells her there are others who come here at night; at that moment the lantern goes dark and something heavy lands on both of them in the dark: a warm, soft avalanche that smells of lavender; out of the heap comes a very small voice: "I'm so, so sorry."

## 4. A Great Many Socks
Synopsis: Marnie meets Lulu Vasquez, who can only conjure socks, and discovers the Potting Shed has been a secret refuge for failed wizards for three hundred years.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 4: Socks are not a spell. Socks are a lifestyle.*
1. Marnie and Toby dig out of a drift of socks: wool, stripy, polka-dot, one with a hole shaped like a small country; a girl with a braid down to her knees climbs down from a rafter apologizing nonstop: Lulu Vasquez, ten, "I was trying to make a candle. It was a candle yesterday too. And the day before. It's always a candle I mean to make."
2. Lulu explains: she's the youngest student Humbleton ever admitted; every spell she casts, whatever she says, conjures socks (rain spell: socks; flying spell: socks); her teacher said "Miss Vasquez, this academy is not a laundry"; she was too embarrassed to go home and has been sleeping in the shed's hammock for nine days; Toby has been feeding her cheese sandwiches.
3. The socks are always mismatched and always smell of lavender; Lulu apologizes that they're never pairs, and Toby says it's actually very consistent; the rafters are covered in carved names back 300 years: "Ermintrude, 1743: turned my own nose into a turnip", "Barnaby: my wand exploded (twice)"; the shed has always been where Humbleton's failures go.
4. The three compare records and Marnie realizes for the first time she isn't the only one; she laughs properly; Lulu offers her a sock as a welcome gift because "it's all I can do", a soft green one with a hole in the toe, and Marnie says it's the nicest present she's ever had and finds she means it.
5. The shed's door creaks and the whole shed tilts to one side as if someone enormous has leaned on it; Lulu whispers "Oh, that'll be Bunt," and a hand the size of a coal shovel pushes through the door holding a tray of biscuits.

## 5. The Troll Who Sneezed
Synopsis: Bunt, Humbleton's gentle troll groundskeeper who is allergic to magic, joins them, and the four decide to form a club.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 5: When in doubt, stand upwind of the troll.*
1. Bunt ducks in sideways: nine feet tall, grey-green, a tuft of moss-green hair, a patched brown vest the size of a tent, barrel-shaped boots, a red-spotted handkerchief as big as a tablecloth; he speaks slowly and kindly ("Biscuit? They're ginger. I made them. They're a bit hard. You can dunk.") and apologizes to the ceiling for bumping it.
2. Marnie, to cheer everyone up, aims her wand at his tray to make the biscuits glow; Bunt's nose wrinkles: "ah... ah... AH-CHOO!" the sneeze knocks the whole room: every sock vanishes with a pop, the duck turns back into a flowerpot, the glow fizzles; and for one heartbeat Gerald looks suspiciously like a tall purple hat, then he honks and is a goose again; nobody notices except Marnie, who shrugs it off.
3. Bunt explains: magic makes his nose itch; the school hired him to carry stones and mend fences because trolls are strong, but whenever a teacher cast a spell near him: "ah-choo", and the spell fizzled; they said he disrupted the Wizarding Order and asked him to stay three fields away from classrooms; he is allowed to stay as long as he is quiet and unmagical, "which I am, mostly."
4. Under the carved rafters the four sit among flowerpots eating ginger biscuits (Bunt was right: dunk), and discover each has been told "You can't" in a different way; Marnie says "We should have a club," Toby says "A club for people expelled from a school for wizards?" and Lulu says "A Dropout Club," and Bunt asks gravely whether clubs have biscuits.
5. They shake hands (Bunt offers one finger), Gerald honks as secretary, and Marnie chalks Rule 1 on the shed door; at that moment across the garden a brass band starts up, practicing for Founders' Day in three days; the Golden Circle will cast a Grand Spell; the Dropouts watch the lights of the school through the foggy window and have no idea that the biggest spell in Humbleton's history is about to go wrong, and that it will be their problem.

# PART TWO: THE DROPOUT CLUB

## 6. Meetings Begin With Biscuits
Synopsis: The Dropout Club moves into the shed, makes it a home with four kinds of wrong magic, and writes its Handbook.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 6: Meetings begin with biscuits.*
1. Dawn: Marnie sneaks out of Gran's with a packed basket (eight jam sandwiches, one spare broom, a note saying "Good luck with whatever this is"); the shed at sunrise with dew on the pots and Bunt already outside holding a kettle the size of a cauldron.
2. Redecorating: Toby points at a rickety bench and says "Demolish!" and it builds itself into a cozy sofa; "Lock!" opens the door; Marnie says "Tidy" and the dust bunnies become real bunnies (one sticks around and is named Dust Bunny); Lulu says "Please clean" and conjures 104 socks that become curtains, cushions, a rug and a hammock.
3. Writing the Handbook: Marnie dictates the rules, Toby edits them, Bunt holds the pencil like a toothpick, Lulu apologizes for the rule about apologizing, and Gerald steals the pencil and has to be bargained with using a biscuit; they elect Gerald "Honorary Chair" because he cannot be argued with.
4. First meeting, agenda: what each would do if their magic ever worked; Toby: light one candle the right way; Lulu: a pair; Bunt: stand in a classroom and not sneeze; Marnie says very quietly she'd cast one correct spell, just once, so nobody could ever say she couldn't; nobody says she shouldn't want it.
5. Evening: Bunt sneezes (he's allergic to the sparkles drifting from the school) and a hole of quiet blows across the garden; far above on the hill, a golden light blooms: the Golden Circle rehearsing the Grand Spell; it is so bright and perfect it makes every flowerpot's shadow stand up straight; Marnie feels an odd little chill she can't explain.

## 7. Practice Makes Something
Synopsis: The club tries to learn proper magic and fails gloriously, but when a runaway broom races through the village their four wrongs make a right.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 7: Practice makes... something. Not always perfect.*
1. Training day: Marnie borrows textbooks, Toby reads the spells aloud, deduces the backwards versions and tries them; Lulu tries "Float" and gets a floating sock; Marnie turns a teapot into a frog and the frog into a cheese; Bunt holds the textbook as a lectern and sneezes the pages into a snowstorm.
2. The scoreboard Toby draws: Marnie 3 fish, 1 duck, 2 mice, 1 cheese; Lulu 212 socks; Toby 0 spells in the correct direction; Bunt 31 sneezes; they conclude they're excellent at being wrong and miserable at being right, and Lulu apologizes to the scoreboard.
3. A shout from the lane: a runaway broom, the Dimblewick bakery delivery broom, has slipped its leash and is towing Mrs. Humphreys's cart of cream buns down the hill at a gallop, while Mrs. Humphreys, covered in flour, runs after yelling "Not the sponges!"
4. Without a plan the four race into the lane: Toby says "Fly!" and the broom, working backwards, stops dead; the cart skids, Lulu throws a whole armload of socks and the buns land safely in a mountain of wool; Bunt catches the cart with one hand; Marnie, aiming her wand at the broom to calm it, turns the cart's wheel into a wheel of cheese and, to everyone's surprise, a wheel of cheese turns out to be an excellent brake.
5. Mrs. Humphreys gives them a seed cake and a sack of buns, saying "Well, that was either very clever or very lucky"; the four sit on the bakery wall eating buns in the sun and Toby says quietly: "That was… all of us."; none of them notices the boy in a royal-blue blazer watching from the top of the lane with a golden notebook.

## 8. The Golden Circle Comes to Tea
Synopsis: Cassius Pomeroy and the Golden Circle visit the shed to mock the club and accidentally reveal the secret of their Perfect Spell.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 8: Never invite a Golden Circle student to anything with a floor that can open.*
1. Cassius arrives at the shed with Dahlia and Rex in gold-trimmed cloaks that never wrinkle, their shoes shiny even in mud; Cassius's hair stays perfect in a stiff breeze; he looks around the shed and says, "How cozy. Like a hamster cage," and hands Marnie a gold-edged card inviting her to "watch" the Founders' Day ceremony from the back.
2. He mocks each in turn with perfect politeness: Toby's backwards spells, Lulu's socks ("a laundry, how modern"), Bunt, "Is he supposed to be indoors?"; Marnie holds back until he calls Gerald "that unfortunate hat", and Gerald hisses so hard Rex drops his glasses.
3. Cassius, unable to resist boasting, opens his golden notebook and explains the Perfectly Perfect Spell, an invention of his own that will make "every last thing at Humbleton flawless, without a single error, forever", and the Principal has approved it as the Founders' Day showpiece; his hand shakes for half a second and he pretends it didn't.
4. Bunt rubs his nose; "ah... ah... AH-CHOO!" blows across the notebook, the golden shimmer dies and Cassius's hair flops in front of his eyes for the first time in recorded history; he flees with Dahlia and Rex, trips on a sock, and the whole Golden Circle slides down the slope on their perfect shoes.
5. A page has fallen out of the notebook; Toby reads it: the last line of the spell says "requires a balance..." and then a word has been scribbled out; they argue about warning the teachers; Toby: "They won't listen to dropouts." Marnie: "Then we'll watch. If something goes wrong..." Toby: "We'll know exactly who to blame. And exactly what to do." He shouldn't have said it.

## 9. A Bad Spell in the Right Place
Synopsis: The club becomes Dimblewick's odd-jobs team, and Marnie begins to feel she belongs, though she still longs for one correct spell.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 9: A bad spell is only a good spell in the wrong place.*
1. A montage of Dimblewick odd jobs in one breathless page: unclogging the well (Bunt's sneeze blasts out a century of leaves), starting the town clock (Toby says "Stop!" and it ticks), padding a sagging roof with socks, and trading Marnie's accidental fish to a delighted cat-owner for a week of free jam.
2. A hand-painted sign on the shed door by Lulu, finished with one troll finger and a lot of apologies: "THE DROPOUT CLUB: ODD JOBS & ODDMENTS (NO SPELLS GUARANTEED)"; villagers start leaving gifts: a jar of pickles, a knitted hat for Gerald (he eats it), a blanket, a very small, very old teapot.
3. A quiet night on the shed roof under the stars: Marnie confesses to Toby that she still wants one real spell, one that goes the way it should, so no teacher could ever say she can't; Toby takes off his glasses and says he'd like to light one candle forward, once; both of them are quiet, then Toby says "we could be wrong about what wrong is," and neither of them is ready to believe it.
4. Gran visits the shed with a broom and a pie, inspects the crooked sofa and the sock curtains and says it's the best-made hideout she's seen in forty years; she tells Marnie the secret of broom mending: "The best broom is always the one with the little wobble. It's the only one that dances."
5. The night before Founders' Day a gold-edged card is pushed under the shed door in the Principal's own spidery hand: "Humbleton Academy cordially invites the Dropout Club to the Founders' Day Ceremony, in the interest of public relations"; the four read it three times; Toby: "He means he wants us where he can see us." Lulu: "I'll wear my good socks."

## 10. Founders' Day
Synopsis: Founders' Day begins with a brass band and bunting, and ends with Cassius's Perfect Spell going very, very wrong.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 10: Sit at the back, near the exit, and close to the snacks.*
1. Morning at Humbleton: bunting, parents, a brass band whose instruments play different tunes at once, a long table of cakes arranged by size; the Dropouts arrive in their best: Toby with a polished bow tie, Lulu in her one matching pair of socks (a miracle), Bunt with a handkerchief tied over his nose like an outlaw, and Marnie in a clean cloak with Gerald riding on her head like a hat.
2. Principal Thistlewood's speech beneath a replacement purple hat that is too small and held on with seven pins: "Precision above all! The art of magic is the art of never, ever being wrong!"; his moustache lifts when he sees Gerald; Marnie remembers the old hat and feels a flicker of guilt she can't place.
3. Rows of perfect students sit in gold-trimmed rows; Cassius steps onto the stage, notebook in hand, hair immaculate; the Great Hall stills; Bunt, near the tent flap, starts to rub his nose and Toby gently holds his arm, "Not yet."
4. Cassius lifts his silver wand and says "Perfectus Universalis!" A wave of gold glitter sweeps the Hall: bunting straightens, the band plays in perfect tune, crumbs vanish, every shoelace ties itself in a double bow; the crowd gasps and claps; the Principal's moustache rises in delight.
5. But the wave doesn't stop: it grows quiet, then there is a soft "bong", like a spoon on a bell, and little pointed red hats pop onto every head in the Hall; Marnie looks at where the Principal was standing: a two-foot-tall man with a long white beard, a pointed red hat, a bright blue jacket and a cheerful painted-on smile; "He had become, quite perfectly, a garden gnome."

# PART THREE: THE GREAT GNOMING

## 11. Perfectly Gnomed
Synopsis: Everyone perfect in the Great Hall has turned into a garden gnome, only the Dropouts are untouched, and the clock starts ticking.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 11: If everyone suddenly turns into a gnome, stay calm and take notes.*
1. The Great Hall, a minute later: hundreds of gnomes in neat rows still mid-clap, still smiling, trays of tiny cakes sliding off tiny hands, the tuba now a very short gnome with a brass hat; the only people not gnomes are Marnie, Toby, Lulu and Bunt, a few confused parents at the door, and Gerald, who honks, pleased.
2. Toby works out the rule by taking notes: the gnomes are alive, they can walk in short waddles and talk, but they cannot cast any spell; Cassius, now a gnome with golden hair sticking out under his pointed red hat, stands alone on the stage staring at his wand which is far too big for his hand.
3. The squeaky Principal-gnome demands Marnie fix it at once, his moustache standing straight up, and she tries "Straighten!" on him in a panic; it turns his wand into a sausage; Toby tries a backwards "Gnome!" and for a moment a gnome grows two inches, then shrinks again.
4. Two grey-suited Ministry inspectors burst in on a flying carpet, assess the scene at a glance, and cast a flawless "Restore!"; the gold glitter ripples out and both become gnomes in grey hats; Toby deduces the hex feeds on perfectly cast spells.
5. A giant golden clock appears on the Hall wall with seventy-two hours; the gold wave spreads slowly out the doors across the lawn; Marnie, in a very small voice: "We need a plan." Toby: "We need a great deal of biscuits." Bunt, unseen, has already put the kettle on.

## 12. Gnomes Are Terrible at Taking Orders
Synopsis: The club relocates the gnomes to safety and finds Cassius's notebook, which names the missing piece of the spell.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 12: Gnomes are terrible at taking orders.*
1. Bunt carries the gnomes to safety in batches in his enormous vest pockets (dozens at a time: two fishmongers, a vice-principal, a choir), calling it "Gnome Relocation Services"; the gnomes complain about the draught, the pockets, the biscuits and each other.
2. In the Potting Shed Lulu makes sleeping bags from socks for the gnomes; the Principal-gnome demands tea in a squeaky voice and then asks where his hat is; Cassius-gnome folds his arms and refuses to speak to any dropout; Gerald simply sits on the Principal-gnome's head as if it were a nest.
3. Marnie sneaks back to the Great Hall stage to fetch Cassius's golden notebook, walking carefully among the sparkling floor where the hex shimmers; she uses Toby's trick of going backwards on purpose so no spell is cast; Gerald watches from the door, honking only once.
4. The notebook's last pages are scorched; Bunt's sneeze from the doorway cleans the gold dust off them; in Cassius's tidy handwriting: "The Perfectly Perfect Spell requires a Balance Anchor: use the Founder's Hat, see Principal." A note in the margin: "Principal says replacement hat is fine?? It is NOT enchanted!"; Toby reads it aloud twice.
5. "What happened to the Principal's old hat?" Toby asks; nobody answers; Marnie goes very quiet; Lulu looks at Marnie; Bunt looks at Gerald; Gerald, who has no idea, steals a sock; the last line of the chapter: Marnie says, "I turned it into a goose."

## 13. The Rules of the Hex
Synopsis: The club tests the hex and learns the cure lies in the Founding Charter in the Great Library.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 13: Test your theory on something that can't sue you.*
1. Toby sets up a laboratory in the shed on a flowerpot table with a chart: Marnie casts a wonky "Grow!" at a pea plant (it grows a sock), the hex ignores it; Toby casts "Shrink!" backwards (it grows) and the hex ignores that too; Lulu casts a perfect "Hello" at a gnome and, as every sock lands in a heap, the glitter wave jumps another ten feet.
2. The glitter wave is creeping toward Dimblewick; the Mayor in her tall pink hat panics because magic in the village stops: the bread won't rise, the lamppost won't announce, Mrs. Humphreys's broom sulks in a corner; the village asks the Dropout Club to fix it, "with respect, given nobody else can".
3. Guilt: Marnie, hands shaking, tells everyone the whole truth about the hat; Lulu apologizes on Marnie's behalf; Toby says "It wasn't your fault: you made a wrong spell, Cassius made a bad plan and the Principal told a lie about the hat"; Bunt says "Everybody did one thing"; Marnie is only half comforted.
4. Professor Quillfeather, the gnome librarian, owlish and squeaky and the only calm gnome in the shed, tells them that the Founding Charter is in the Great Library, third shelf of the Founders' Section, and that "there is an answer; there always is"; she begs them to hurry before nightfall, because by midnight the hex will cross the lawn.
5. The plan: slip into the Great Library by the back way (through the hedge, up the kitchen stairs, along the Longest Corridor); Bunt packs sandwiches, Lulu packs socks, Toby packs a map he's drawn from memory; Marnie packs Gerald; the Mayor salutes them; the four set out as the sun goes down and the shadows of the school stretch across the glittering lawn.

## 14. The Longest Corridor
Synopsis: The four sneak through a hexed school, passing a polite staircase and a sword-wielding suit of armor using their wonky skills.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 14: Never trust a staircase that is too polite.*
1. Inside Humbleton the school is eerily neat: the hex has made every cobweb straight and every inkpot full; the Dropouts tiptoe past the kitchens, a row of gnome cooks mid-stir with fixed smiles, and Lulu apologizes to each one as they pass.
2. The Polite Staircase: a grand marble staircase that moves aside at the sound of "After you," so politely that it keeps moving out of the way and no one can climb it; Toby says "Come back!" backwards and it stays perfectly still, and Lulu throws socks on the steps so they don't slip.
3. The Longest Corridor: a hallway so long its far end is out of sight, lined with portraits of past principals all turned into gnomes inside their frames, each shouting advice ("Left!" "No, right!"); Marnie finds a portrait of a stern woman with a tall purple hat, plaque reading Wilhelmina Humbleton, Founder, and the hat looks very familiar.
4. A suit of armor, perfectly trained to guard the Library, steps forward and raises a pike, "No unauthorized persons!" ; Bunt's sneeze, "ah... ah... AH-CHOO!" blasts its enchantment and it collapses in a clatter, apologizing; Lulu props a sock-stuffed cushion under its head and it says "Thank you, that's much better."
5. The Library door has a riddle lock: "I open only to those who cannot open"; Marnie tries three correct answers, nothing; Toby says "Lock!" and the door swings wide with a happy creak; Gerald honks and marches in first as if he owned the place, and the Dropouts follow into the dark.

## 15. The Founder's Hat
Synopsis: In the Great Library the Dropouts read the Founding Charter and learn what the hat really was and what it needs.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 15: Normal is a setting on the oven, not a way to live.*
1. The Great Library by lantern light: shelves taller than houses, ladders that slide by themselves, reading lamps hovering like fireflies, Lulu whispering "I'm so sorry" to a shelf of snoring books; Bunt tiptoes with a handkerchief over his nose to keep from sneezing on the old pages.
2. In the Founders' Section Toby finds the Charter, a huge vellum scroll in a glass case; Marnie reads aloud: "I, Wilhelmina Humbleton, in founding this school, enchant my hat to catch the overflow of perfect magic, for perfect magic has no room in it for wonder, and will turn on itself."
3. The last line of the Charter: "Only magic that is a little wrong can balance magic that is entirely right"; Toby reads it twice, Lulu once, Bunt slowly with his finger, and Marnie once, then goes very still, looks at Gerald, and says "He's not just a goose, is he?"
4. They realize the Principal's tall purple hat was Wilhelmina's Founder's Hat, worn for forty years, soaking up all the overflow of the school's correct magic without anyone knowing; Marnie turned it into Gerald, so when Cassius's Perfectly Perfect Spell went off there was nothing to catch it; Toby: "You didn't ruin the hat. You hid it."
5. Marnie says she can fix it: she'll turn Gerald back, properly, once and for all, with a perfect spell; Toby warns her that a perfect spell will start the gold wave again; she doesn't want to hear it; as the Dropouts leave at midnight the four are not speaking, and Gerald, between them, looks from one to the other and honks quietly, like a goose who knows he is in the middle of something.

# PART FOUR: WONKY MAGIC

## 16. The Normalizing Tonic
Synopsis: Desperate to be normal, Marnie drinks Cassius's Tonic and casts a perfect spell, with a very big consequence.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 16: When a friend falls, catch them. Then put socks under them.*
1. Dawn of the second day: Marnie argues with Toby at the shed; she says she just wants to be good at something the right way and fix it herself, once; Toby says "You don't need the right way, you need the wonky one"; Marnie says "Easy for you to say, you've got a way that works"; Toby's face closes; she grabs Gerald and storms out.
2. In the empty Principal's office (portraits asleep, a gnome-sized teacup on the desk), she finds Cassius's secret bottle in a drawer: the Normalizing Tonic, labelled in neat gold, "Makes any spell go exactly where it is pointed"; it tastes of plain porridge.
3. She points her wand at Gerald and says "Revert!" and for the first time in her life her spell does exactly what it should: Gerald glows, stretches, and becomes a tall purple hat with silver stars; she gasps, grinning, laughing, a girl in an empty office with a hat in her hands, perfectly right.
4. The perfect spell sets off the gold wave; it sweeps through the office; the hat rolls back to a goose with a startled honk; and Marnie, feeling her cloak stiffen, looks down to see a bright blue jacket, a long white beard (on an eleven-year-old) and a pointed red hat: "The one correct spell of her life had made her a garden gnome."
5. Her friends, who have followed her, find a tiny red-hatted gnome with copper-red hair buns sticking out from under the pointed hat, a wand too big for her hand, and the cheerfulest face in the world on the saddest girl; Lulu sobs and apologizes, Bunt lifts her as gently as an egg, and Toby sits on the floor with his glasses in his hands and says nothing.

## 17. Four Wonky Wizards
Synopsis: The friends combine their wrong magic to un-gnome Marnie, proving the plan that will save everyone.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 17: A plan is a wish with a schedule.*
1. In the shed the gnome-Marnie sits on a flowerpot refusing a biscuit; Gerald sits in her lap; she says it's all her fault and she's useless as a gnome and useless as a wizard; Toby, quietly, "I once thought I was useless too. I made a list. It was a very long list, and then I made another: things only I can do. It was longer."
2. Toby has an idea: a backwards "Gnome!" on its own only worked for a moment because the hex pushes back; but what if Bunt sneezes the hex's perfection off first and Lulu's socks soak up the glitter? It's a four-part wonky spell; nobody has ever tried a spell like that; Lulu: "I'm so sorry in advance if it goes wrong."
3. The test: Bunt winds up, "ah... ah... AH-CHOO!" and the hex shell cracks; Lulu throws a hundred socks that drink up the gold glitter; Toby says "Gnome!" backwards with great care; and Marnie, tiny, aims her gnome-sized wand and says "Straighten" at herself, so wrong it's a hundred percent right.
4. A loud pop: Marnie is herself again, in her mustard cardigan, a little singed, with a bright blue hem; Lulu hugs her so hard she squeaks, Toby cleans his glasses three times, and Bunt, overcome, blows his nose so loudly the shed rattles; Marnie says "I'm so sorry I said that, Toby" and Toby says "Rule 2? Never mind. I was just wrong. We're all just wrong. It's brilliant."
5. They write the plan on the shed wall in chalk: restore Gerald to the Hat, place it on the founder's statue in the Fountain courtyard before the clock runs out, in the middle of the hex wave; it will take all four wrongs together; the clock says fourteen hours; Gran arrives with a flask of cocoa and says only "Good. A shed is the proper place for important thinking."

## 18. The Fountain
Synopsis: At dawn the four set out across the glittering courtyard to the Founders' Fountain with a plan, a goose and three hundred socks.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 18: Everything is useful if you stand in the right place.*
1. Dawn of the third day: the gnomes line up in the shed to see them off, the Principal-gnome giving a speech nobody listens to, Professor Quillfeather squeaking advice, and the Mayor-gnome presenting Marnie with a tiny ceremonial spoon; Cassius-gnome, arms folded, says nothing; as they leave he mutters: "The hat's incantation needs a first line. 'To the Founder, a little wrong.' Say it as you place the hat."
2. Marching across the glittering lawn: the gold wave is thick as syrup around the courtyard and gnomes anyone who passes; Bunt walks first, sneezing a path through the glitter with each step; Lulu follows throwing socks to soak the sparkle; Toby tiptoes backwards, narrating, and Marnie carries Gerald in her arms like a baby swan.
3. Halfway: the clock on the Hall wall booms, "Twenty minutes!" and the wave shoves back, high as a house; Lulu runs out of socks and says "Oh dear"; then, impossibly, she conjures an enormous heap in one go (three hundred, mismatched, lavender) because for the first time she isn't apologizing; the wave bumps into the wool and sags like a pudding.
4. At the Founders' Fountain the statue of Wilhelmina Humbleton stands bareheaded in the spray, the water sparkling gold; Toby, shaking, says the backwards version of Marnie's goose spell on Gerald: "Goose!" and Gerald starts to stretch, shimmer and change; Marnie holds him, whispering "Thank you for being a goose" and means it.
5. The wave rears up for a last great surge, bigger than any, a golden wall to the sky; the clock says one minute; Gerald is halfway between goose and hat, honking; Marnie looks at the statue's bare head fifteen feet up and the wobbly ladder Lulu has made of knotted socks; the chapter ends with her taking the first step.

## 19. The Hat
Synopsis: The biggest sneeze in history, one crooked hat, and a happy ending that pops into place.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 19: Always bring a spare handkerchief to the end of the world.*
1. Gerald finishes becoming the Founder's Hat in Marnie's arms, tall, purple, silver-starred and slightly goose-shaped around the brim; the hex wave hits the edge of the wool dune and every sock soaks up a swallow of gold; the courtyard goes golden and silent.
2. Bunt, with the handkerchief raised like a flag, stands in front of the others and begins: "ah... ah... ah..."; the whole school seems to lean in; every gnome in every window gasps; the biggest sneeze in the history of sneezes, "AH-CHOOOOOOO!", cancels every spell for a mile around and blows his own hair straight up.
3. Marnie climbs the sock ladder with the hat in her teeth, up past the statue's shoulder; at the top Cassius-gnome has somehow scrambled up behind her and holds the ladder steady, saying stiffly, "Pennywhistle. Don't look down. I'd recommend not looking at me either."
4. She places the hat on Wilhelmina's head and says Cassius's line: "To the Founder, a little wrong"; and because she can't help it she adds, "Straighten!" and the hat, perfectly obedient to her wonkiness, tilts jauntily to one side and sticks there; the gold glitter spins into the hat in one great whirl, like water down a drain.
5. A soft "pop" like a cork; the wave goes out like a candle; and across the lawn, in the Great Hall and the village, a thousand pops go off one by one as every gnome becomes a person again: Mrs. Humphreys covered in cream, the Mayor in her pink hat, Professor Quillfeather with her glasses, the Principal's moustache springing out last of all.

## 20. The Dropout Club
Synopsis: Everyone is restored, a new department is born, and the Dropout Club welcomes its next member.
Header: *From the Dropout Club Handbook, Rule 20: There are no dropouts. There are only students whose school hasn't been built yet.*
1. The morning after: the courtyard strewn with socks and confetti, the Principal in a blanket with a cup of tea and his moustache drooping, standing before the school: he publicly apologizes to Marnie ("I was wrong. About the hat, and about you.") and says the hat is on the statue where it belongs, "slightly crooked, as it turns out it prefers."
2. Cassius, hair flopped, approaches the Dropouts at the Fountain and says in a stiff voice that he is sorry; then, much to his own surprise, that he is truly sorry; Marnie says "It's okay to be wrong, Cassius; you'll get used to it", he tries to cast one wonky spell on purpose, gets a small soggy sock, and is so delighted he nearly cries.
3. The Ministry offers Marnie reinstatement; she says she'll come back only if the others come with her; the Principal clears his throat, strokes his moustache, then announces the new Department of Wonky Magic with Bunt as Assistant Professor of Sneezing, Lulu in charge of Applied Socks, Toby as Professor of Reversal, and Marnie as Head of Sideways Studies.
4. The village celebration in Dimblewick: Gran does a victory dance with a broom, Mrs. Humphreys's buns, a banner Lulu painted with a lot of apologies, Gerald in his old silver ribbon, who now seems to be a goose on odd days and a hat on even ones and makes a point of being a goose at lunchtime.
5. Evening in the shed, the handbook open, Marnie writes Rule 20 in ink; there is a knock; a small, nervous, new kid with a bent wand stands in the doorway and says "I heard you take people who can't"; Marnie, who had once been expelled at fourteen minutes past nine on a Tuesday, hands over a biscuit and says "We take everyone. Come in."
