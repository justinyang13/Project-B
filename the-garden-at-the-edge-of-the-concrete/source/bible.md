# THE GARDEN AT THE EDGE OF THE CONCRETE — story bible

Magic-garden adventure for ages 10-12: quiet wonder, real feeling and humor, set in a present-day concrete housing estate (Lowell Court) with a secret garden behind a loading bay. Nothing spooky, no violence, no romance. The magic is gentle and rule-bound: the garden only responds to patience and true words. Screens exist but are unimportant; the story is about hands, dirt, water and time.

## Voice
FIRST PERSON, PAST TENSE, narrated by Sana Haddad, 11, looking back over one year, in a dry, observant, quietly funny voice: an impatient girl who is learning to wait, who notices everything (cracks, weeds, the sound of a lift) and says less than she thinks. She addresses the reader now and then ("I want to say I was patient. I wasn't."). She lists things, times things, complains accurately, and gets plain and short when she is moved. Each chapter opens with a one-line italic entry from her Garden Log, on its own line, then a blank line, in this exact form: "*Garden Log, Day 1: Growing: nothing. Waiting: everything.*" The entries are terse, funny and specific, with the day number rising through the year (Day 1 up to about Day 365) and the last entry a kind of answer to the first.
Voice sample:
"*Garden Log, Day 1: Growing: nothing. Waiting: everything.*

I would like to say I found the garden because I am curious and brave. What actually happened is that I was hiding from a stack of moving boxes.

Lowell Court is eleven concrete blocks around a car park, and I had been living in Block C for nine days, which is enough time to know exactly how long the lift takes (forty seconds, if it feels like it) and how many steps lead to the fourth floor (ninety-two), and not nearly enough time to know a single person."

## The premise
Sana Haddad, 11, has just moved with her family to Lowell Court, a tired estate of concrete blocks at the edge of the city, where the paving ends at a chain-link fence and a rail cutting. Sana is impatient with everything: she checks a seed she planted in a yogurt cup every hour, she wants friends by Friday, she wants to be good at things by tomorrow. Hiding from unpacking, she squeezes through a gap behind the loading bay and finds a walled garden nobody has entered for years, with a rusted seed tin, a gate in a hedge, and a very old tortoise. The garden is magic, but only slowly: seeds sprout only if planted with a true thing said aloud, and if she rushes or lies, the garden answers with comic disasters. Then a notice on the block door announces that the land is to be sold and paved as a car park in one year.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Sana Haddad**, 11. Thin, brown skin, round glasses, thick black hair in a low bun with a green pencil through it, an oversized denim jacket with patched pockets full of seed packets and a tape measure. Impatient, precise, secretly shy about speaking in front of people. Habit: measures things and counts steps.
- **Gherkin**, the garden's tortoise: old (over three hundred years), the size of a hat, with a mossy shell, an unhurried voice and a habit of finishing sentences much later. Wise but unhelpful on purpose; loves lettuce and being right. Refrain: "Mm. Not yet."
- **Mr. Osei**, the estate's caretaker, 60s: very tall, soft-spoken, grey moustache, blue overalls, a ring of keys. He made the garden thirty years ago and lost his nerve to keep it after his wife died; he cannot bring himself to speak up for it.
- **Mama (Dalia Haddad)**, a night-shift nurse; tired, funny, fierce. **Baba (Karim Haddad)**, a baker who leaves at 3 a.m. and brings home warm bread; **Yusuf Haddad**, 6, missing front teeth, loud, cannot keep a secret.
- **Theo Kowalski**, 12, Block C skater in a beanie and a hoodie with the sleeves pushed up; sarcastic, lonely, secretly good with plants. **Mrs. Ilves**, 80s, Block C fourth floor, watches everything from her window, knits, remembers the garden.
- **Ms. Vandermeer**, the planner from Greenfield Homes: neat grey suit, brisk, not wicked; she believes the estate needs parking and jobs, and she has never seen the garden.
- Places: Lowell Court (eleven blocks, lift, ninety-two steps), the loading bay, the gap in the fence, the walled garden (Spring Room by the pear tree, Summer Room behind the hedge door, Autumn Room with the orchard wall, Winter Room under the glass), the bench that hums, the rail cutting, the Community Hall.

## The garden's rules (the seed tin's lid)
1. One seed a day. 2. Plant it with one true thing, said aloud. 3. Water in the morning and again at dusk. 4. Never hurry a root. 5. Ask before you pick. If you break a rule the garden does not punish you; it exaggerates: a rushed seed grows fast and wrong (a pumpkin vine that swallows a bicycle), a lie makes thorns.

## The plan of the story
Ch1-4: moving day, impatience, the gap, the garden, the tin, Gherkin. Ch5-9: the first plantings and the rules; comic disasters when she rushes; Yusuf finds out; Theo follows her; Mr. Osei's history. Ch10-14: the four seasons of rooms; her first big failure of responsibility (she skips two dawn waterings for Theo's skate meet-up and the Summer Room wilts); the notice about the car park; a Community Hall meeting where she freezes and cannot speak. Ch15-19: Winter: the long wait, seeds under glass that show nothing for weeks; Gherkin's story; the neighbors' true words; she learns to speak by practicing on plants; the Council meeting: she and Mr. Osei speak; the decision is postponed rather than won; Ms. Vandermeer visits and stays. Ch20: spring: the garden is saved as a community allotment for a trial year; the first seeds sprout; Log Day 365.

## Motifs
The Garden Log entries; counting (steps, seconds, days); "Not yet" (Gherkin); the tin's four rules; Sana's tape measure (plants grow 2 cm a week and that is a lot); bread from Baba; Mrs. Ilves' knitting (a scarf that grows one row a day, finished at the end); the bench that hums when someone tells the truth.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Responsibility and patience: good things grow slowly, and what you tend needs you every day, not only when you feel like it. Speaking up: caring about something means saying so out loud, even shaking, even if it may not work. Never moralize except one plain line near the end. Do not make the planner a villain. The ending is honest: the garden gets a one-year trial, not a victory lap.

## Things to avoid
No spooky or scary content; no violence; no romance; no villain; keep the magic small and consistent with the rules. Don't let Sana be fixed by one lesson: she relapses into impatience more than once. Avoid tech-heavy plot.
