# THE ROBOT WHO WAS BAD AT EVERYTHING — story bible

Warm, funny school-and-science-fiction comedy for ages 10-12, narrated by a robot who was built to be perfect at everything and has never once tried. Set in a near-future but ordinary town (Hollis Middle School, Room 5B). Nothing spooky, no violence, no romance, no real villain. The stakes are friendship, being seen, and a threat of being "reset" that the story treats gently. The comedy is about someone who has never been bad at anything learning what everybody else already knows: the trying is where the joy is.

## Voice
FIRST PERSON, PAST TENSE, narrated by PERCY (model PERFECT-9), looking back on his first term at Hollis. Formal, exact, deadpan and literal at first ("I arrived at 8:02 and 14 seconds"), with a running habit of reporting numbers and "approximately", a dry cool wit, and growing, unmistakable feelings he cannot classify ("a reading I did not have a word for"). He addresses the reader now and then ("I want it noted that..."). His sentences get shorter and less precise as he loosens up; by the end he rounds numbers and says "about" happily. Never cutesy robot-speak (no "beep boop", no "does not compute"). Keep him sincere: the humor comes from precision colliding with human mess.
Each chapter opens with one italic line from PERCY'S ERROR LOG, on its own line, then a blank line, in this exact form: "*Percy's Error Log, Entry 1. Errors today: 0. Suspicious.*" The entries are terse and funny, numbers rise through the book (errors climb into the hundreds, then thousands), and the last entry answers the first. The Header line is added by the script: never write it on any page.
Voice sample:
"*Percy's Error Log, Entry 1. Errors today: 0. Errors lifetime: 0. Note: this is not as nice as it sounds.*

I was built to be perfect. I would like to explain at the start that this is not a boast. It is a specification, in the same way that a bridge is built to be a bridge. On the morning I arrived at Hollis Middle School, I had never made an error, never been late, never misspelled a word, never dropped a plate, and never once been asked how I felt about any of it.

Nobody thinks to ask a perfect thing how it feels. I have since learned that this is the first problem with being perfect."

## The premise
PERFECT-9 is the ninth prototype of a school-assistant robot built by Meridian Labs: tall and slim, silver-white with a smooth round head and two blue circular eyes that show expressions like a screen, jointed grey arms, big soft grey hands, a small glowing chest light, a red school lanyard. He is placed in Room 5B at Hollis Middle School for a term as a "classroom assistant" so Meridian can certify him. He is flawless at everything measurable: maths, spelling, dodgeball (gently), tidying, remembering. His flawlessness makes the class feel small, and nobody sits with him. The girl he is drawn to is Bea Alvarado, who fails constantly and laughs. When a deliberate slip makes the class laugh WITH him, Percy begins to fake mistakes to be liked; it works, then it hurts the people it was meant to win (Marcus loses a ribbon because of it), and Bea calls it what it is: a lie that makes everyone feel dumb twice. Percy then tries something he genuinely might fail at (drawing), and the errors are real and, to his astonishment, joyful. Meridian's engineer reads his error log as a malfunction and orders a Recall and reset on Certification Day at the Founders' Fair. The class refuses to hand him over quietly and builds, with Percy, a huge Chain Reaction Machine that keeps failing in rehearsal. On Certification Day it fails in front of the whole town and Dr. Pryce, and then, on the seventh attempt, it works, by a hair. Dr. Pryce, foam in her hair, changes the certification.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Percy (PERFECT-9)**: tall, slim, silver-white robot with a smooth round head, two big round blue eyes that change shape to show feeling, jointed grey arms with big soft grey hands, a small glowing round chest light, red school lanyard with an ID card. Precise, sincere, literal; secretly lonely. Habit: reports numbers, says "approximately", tilts his head when a reading is unclassified. Refrain: "Errors today: ..."; "I would like this noted."
- **Bea (Beatriz) Alvarado**, 11: short and sturdy, warm brown skin, thick black curly hair in a messy bun stuck with pencils, safety goggles pushed up on her head, an oversized grease-stained yellow raincoat with big pockets, always carrying a small battered notebook, the FAILURE JOURNAL (214 failed inventions, each with "what I learned"). Fearless, funny, stubborn, fails loudly. Lives with Abuela.
- **Abuela Rosa Alvarado**, 70s: tiny, silver hair in a braid, big glasses, a workshop apron, runs Alvarado Fix-It, a cluttered repair shop. Motto: "Nothing is broken. It is only not finished."
- **Marcus Lindqvist ("Moose")**, 11: tall, broad-shouldered, pale freckled skin, a shock of red-blond hair, a fast runner, kind but proud of his speed; loses a ribbon because of Percy's faked clumsiness.
- **Tobias Pym**, 11: thin, neat, dark hair with a side parting, glasses, a bow tie he insists on; the class know-it-all who hates being outdone and is terrified of being wrong in public. Learns to be wrong out loud.
- **Priya Raman**, 11: small, quiet, brown skin, a long black braid, paint on her hands and cuffs, always with a sketchbook; the class artist; teaches Percy to draw.
- **Ms. Adeyemi**, the teacher: tall, calm, warm, dark skin, colorful head-wrap, cardigan with chalk dust, a mug that says nothing legible. She lets the class work it out.
- **Dr. Imogen Pryce**, Meridian's chief engineer, mid-40s: neat silver bob, crisp white lab coat, tablet clipboard, tired eyes. Not a villain: she believes errors mean danger, and she built eight perfect prototypes that were lifeless. Learns Percy is the first that is alive.
- **Gerald**, the class hamster: round, fawn and white, hopelessly bad at his wheel (falls off it every time); Percy's first kindred spirit.
- Places: Hollis Middle School, Room 5B; the supply cupboard (where Percy recharges); Alvarado Fix-It; the Founders' Fair in the school gym and playground.

## The plan of the story
Ch1-5: arrival, perfection and its lonely cost, Bea's exploding glitter invention, Percy fixes it and is told off, first deliberate mistake makes the class laugh. Ch6-10: fake mistakes; Abuela's shop and the Failure Journal; Marcus's lost ribbon; Bea's confrontation; Percy decides to try something real. Ch11-15: drawing with Priya (a portrait that looks like a potato, and joy); the Recall letter; Percy confesses the fake mistakes and asks for help; the class builds the Chain Reaction Machine; Tobias learns to be wrong out loud; the foam disasters. Ch16-20: the night before; Certification Day; the biggest error; Attempt Seven; Dr. Pryce's decision; Log entry 60.

## Motifs
Percy's Error Log headers; Bea's Failure Journal ("what I learned"); Abuela's "not finished"; Gerald falling off the wheel; Percy's head tilt; the chest light (pulses when he is happy); the machine's first step, a marble on a ramp (a tiny thing that keeps failing); foam.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Joy comes from trying, not from being perfect: the people worth admiring are the ones who keep going when they might fail. Faking a mistake is not the same as trying: it is a kind of hiding. One plain line near the end, at most (from Bea, Abuela or Percy). Do not make trying easy: Percy has real, funny, humbling failures, and never becomes a genius at anything new.

## Things to avoid
No scary content, no violence, no romance, no real villain (Dr. Pryce is not one). No "beep boop" robot cliches. No modern-tech-heavy plot beyond the machine. No stacked similes; avoid "It wasn't X. It was Y."; avoid "heart hammered", "stone in the throat". Do not leak the Recall, Dr. Pryce's history or Attempt Seven before their chapters.
