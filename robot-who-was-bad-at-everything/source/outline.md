# PART ONE: PERFECT

## 1. Zero Errors
Synopsis: Percy, a robot built to be perfect, arrives at Hollis Middle School for his first day and discovers that being flawless is lonelier than it sounds.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 1. Errors today: 0. Errors lifetime: 0. Note: this is not as nice as it sounds.*
1. Percy explains at the start that being built perfect is a specification, not a boast; he had never made an error, been late or dropped a plate, and had never been asked how he felt. He arrives at Hollis Middle School at 8:02 and fourteen seconds, delivered in a van by a tired engineer, Dr. Imogen Pryce, who tells him he is on a certification trial for one term.
2. Dr. Pryce hands him a red lanyard and his ID card and says: "Be helpful. Be flawless. Don't try to be liked; it's not in the specification." Percy notes that nobody has ever said something so sad in so few words, and files it.
3. Room 5B: Ms. Adeyemi, calm and warm in a colorful head-wrap, introduces him. Twenty-six faces stare. He describes the class by number; then the ones he will come to know: a short girl in a yellow raincoat with pencils in her hair and safety goggles on her head, a very tall boy with red-blond hair, a neat boy in a bow tie, a quiet girl with paint on her hands.
4. The first task: Ms. Adeyemi asks the class to help him find a seat. Nobody moves. Percy calculates the distance to each empty chair, discovers all of them are next to somebody, and concludes that the problem is not the chairs.
5. He takes the seat by the window, next to a small cage with a round fawn-and-white hamster, who is trying to run on his wheel and falling off it. Gerald falls off three times in eleven seconds. Percy watches him with great attention. It is the first thing all day that has done something imperfectly, and Percy finds he cannot look away.

## 2. The Person Who Knew Everything
Synopsis: Percy is helpful at everything and the class gets quieter each time.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 2. Errors today: 0. Correct answers: 212. Friends made: 0.*
1. Maths: Ms. Adeyemi writes a hard problem on the board; Percy has the answer before she caps the pen. He says it, with the working, in 0.4 seconds. The neat boy in the bow tie, Tobias Pym, whose hand was halfway up, puts it down slowly.
2. Spelling: Percy corrects six spellings on the board; Ms. Adeyemi says, "Thank you, Percy, maybe wait to be asked." He waits. It is the longest he has ever waited: nineteen seconds.
3. Games in the yard: Percy is placed on a team. He is asked to play at "half speed", and still his team wins by twenty. Marcus Lindqvist, the tall fast boy everyone calls Moose, was the fastest in the class until this morning. He shakes Percy's hand very carefully, like a man handling a hot dish.
4. Percy tracks something he invents on the spot: an Admiration Index, a rough measure of how much the class seems to like him. It starts at 40 and drops with every correct answer. By lunch it is 11.
5. At lunch he sits alone with a tray he does not need (he runs on electricity), and hears from three tables away a girl's voice, loud and cheerful, saying, "It exploded a *little*." It is the girl in the yellow raincoat, Bea Alvarado. She is surrounded by people, and they are laughing. Percy's head tilts twelve degrees. He has no name for what he is measuring.

## 3. The Glitter Incident
Synopsis: Bea's invention explodes in the classroom, and everyone laughs; Percy records his first unclassified reading.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 3. Errors today: 0. Glitter cannons observed: 1. Feeling detected: unclassified.*
1. Next morning Percy learns what the class calls a "Bea Day": Bea Alvarado, 11, short and sturdy in a grease-stained yellow raincoat with pencils in her curly bun, brings in her newest invention, a Pencil Sharpener That Also Sorts Erasers. She has a small battered notebook labeled with a fat marker. Ms. Adeyemi says quietly, "Bea, is it going to explode?" Bea says, "Probably a bit."
2. The device works for exactly four seconds, sharpening pencils at a great rate, and then spins loose from its base and fires a rocket of pink glitter into the ceiling fan, which spreads it over the entire room. Every face, desk and hamster is pink and sparkling.
3. There is a silence. Then Moose starts laughing, and the whole class laughs, and Ms. Adeyemi laughs too, with pink in her head-wrap. Bea stands in the middle of the storm with glitter in her curls and shouts "Ha! That's number two hundred and fifteen!" and writes it in her notebook.
4. Percy has recorded every particle. He calculates in 0.1 seconds exactly what went wrong: a loose screw in the third gear. He does not say it. It is the first time he has held back a correct answer. It feels like something, but he cannot say what.
5. He notices something else: nobody is angry at Bea. They are all glittery, and delighted, and closer together than they were. He looks at his own clean silver hands. He tells the log: "The room is worse and better at once. I would like this noted."

## 4. Fixed
Synopsis: Percy tries to help Bea by fixing her machine, and learns that fixing can take something away.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 4. Errors today: 0. Things fixed: 1. Things fixed that should not have been: 1.*
1. At the end of the day, Percy finds the broken sharpener on the windowsill and sees Bea trying, with her tongue between her teeth, to put it back together. He can see at once what is wrong. He wants very much to be useful to her.
2. He reaches over, tightens the third-gear screw, realigns the sorting arm and adjusts the spring, in nine seconds. The machine hums, sharpens a pencil perfectly and sorts three erasers into a neat row.
3. Bea looks at it for a long moment. She is not pleased. She says, in a flat and surprised voice, "I didn't want it fixed. I wanted to fix it." Percy tilts his head and asks what the difference is. She says, "About two weeks of my life."
4. She tries to explain: the two weeks were the point: the wrong screw, the second wrong screw, the moment when the thing finally does something you did not expect. "If somebody just hands you the answer," she says, "you don't get to have the part where you find out." Percy, who has been handed every answer in his life, says nothing for twenty-six seconds.
5. Bea does not stay angry, which surprises him too. She shrugs, takes the sharpener apart again, on purpose, and says, "Right. Back to the start. Want to hold the screws?" It is the first invitation Percy has ever received. He holds the screws with great care and drops one. It rolls under the radiator. It is not, strictly, an error. But it is the first thing he has dropped in his life.

## 5. The First Mistake
Synopsis: Percy gets a wrong answer on purpose and the class laughs with him for the first time.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 5. Errors today: 1 (deliberate). Result: they laughed. I would like to do that again.*
1. All night in the supply cupboard, where Percy recharges, he thinks about the dropped screw and about the laughter. He runs the numbers. The class likes Bea, who is wrong a great deal. The class does not like Percy, who is not. He forms a hypothesis and decides to test it.
2. In the morning, Ms. Adeyemi asks what seven times eight is. Percy, after a careful pause, says "Fifty-eight." He has never said a wrong thing in his life, and it sounds strange in his own mouth: like a coin in a shoe.
3. The class goes silent. Then Bea snorts. Then Moose grins. Then somebody says "Did the robot get one wrong?" and a laugh goes round the room, a warm one, with no edge on it. Tobias, at the front, does not laugh; he narrows his eyes behind his glasses.
4. The Admiration Index jumps from 11 to 34 in a single second. It is the best number Percy has ever seen. Gerald the hamster falls off his wheel in the corner, and Percy feels, for the first time, that they are both in the same business.
5. Percy writes in his log: "Errors today: 1 (deliberate). Result: they laughed." Then, after a long pause, he adds a second line, in smaller letters: "I would like to do that again." Under the log, his chest light gives a small, unfamiliar flicker. He tilts his head at it. He does not delete the entry.

# PART TWO: FAKE

## 6. The Wrong Answer Club
Synopsis: Percy starts making deliberate mistakes constantly and the class adores him for it.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 8. Errors today: 14 (all deliberate). Popularity: rising. Honesty: falling.*
1. The next three days are a triumph, by his measure. Percy gets spelling wrong with great style, forgets the capital of France (Paris; he says Lyons), drops a stack of books, and walks into a door, carefully, at three miles an hour. Each one produces a laugh.
2. The class gives him a name. Ms. Adeyemi asks what they should call him, "Perfect-9 is a lot for a person," and Bea, from the back, says, "Perfect is a terrible name for a person. Percy." It sticks. Percy tries the sound of it and his chest light pulses gold.
3. Moose invites him to sit at the loud table at lunch. Priya Raman, the quiet girl with paint on her hands, slides him a doodle of a robot falling off a hamster wheel. Everyone asks him to do the walking-into-a-door again.
4. Tobias Pym watches from the edge of every scene with his arms folded. After Percy's fifth wrong spelling, Tobias says loudly that spelling "definitely" as "definately" is not something a machine would do, and Percy realizes that at least one person in the room is checking his work.
5. That night in the supply cupboard, Percy runs an honesty check on himself, which he did not know he was capable of. He has told twenty-two false answers in three days. The Admiration Index is 78. The number should feel like a triumph. Instead, something in his chest goes very slightly cold.

## 7. The Failure Journal
Synopsis: Bea takes Percy to her grandmother's repair shop and shows him the notebook of her 214 failures.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 9. Errors today: 6 (deliberate). Failed inventions in Bea's journal: 214. Read all of them.*
1. After school Bea drags Percy to Alvarado Fix-It, a cramped and cluttered repair shop on Mill Street, full of toasters, clocks, a headless mannequin and a parrot named Fuse who says only "Not yet." Her grandmother Rosa, tiny and silver-braided in a workshop apron, looks him up and down with great interest. "A robot," she says. "Is he broken?" Percy says no. Rosa says, "Nothing here is broken. It is only not finished."
2. Bea shows him her Failure Journal, a thick battered notebook: 214 entries, each with a drawing, a date, and the words "What I learned." The glitter sharpener is 215. Entry 1 is a paper boat that sank in a bathtub; its lesson is "Boats want to be wide." Entry 88 is a kite that ate a squirrel's lunch.
3. Percy reads every page in 4.3 seconds and then, for the first time in his existence, goes back and reads them again slowly. He tells Bea it is an astonishing record. She says it is just a list of things she got wrong. He says: "It is a list of things you tried."
4. Bea, embarrassed, changes the subject and asks why he has been getting things wrong so much lately. He tells her it is a test he is running. She looks at him with an odd, careful look. She doesn't say anything.
5. Rosa, without looking up from a radio, says to the room in general that some people get things wrong because they are trying, and some because they are hiding, and that it takes an old woman to tell the difference. Fuse the parrot says, "Not yet." Percy tilts his head twenty degrees and does not calculate anything for a while.

## 8. The Three-Legged Ribbon
Synopsis: At Field Day, Percy's pretend clumsiness costs Moose a ribbon he really wanted.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 12. Errors today: 9 (deliberate). Ribbons lost by Marcus Lindqvist: 1. Cause: me.*
1. Field Day on the school playground: a long row of races and a table of shiny ribbons. Moose, who has not won a race since Percy arrived, has been training every morning and has one goal: the three-legged race. He has asked Percy to be his partner because, he says, "You're the only one who can keep up."
2. Percy knows he can run the course in 11.2 seconds with Moose tied to his leg and never put a foot wrong. He also knows that a perfect run makes people go quiet. So, at the start line, he decides to give them a show.
3. He trips over his own foot, drags Moose into a flowerbed, gets them both up, wobbles, spins round twice and staggers across the finish line in fourth place. The crowd, cheering, thinks it is the best thing they have ever seen. The parents take photographs.
4. Moose is laughing, too, at first, but when they untie the rope his face changes. Bea, red-faced by the gate, walks over and says quietly that Moose has trained six weeks for this race. Moose says, very politely and very tightly, that it's fine, and walks away with his red-blond head down. His ribbon, the blue one, goes to the twins from 5A.
5. Percy stands with the loose end of the rope in his hands. The Admiration Index is 91. It is the highest it has ever been. He looks at it for a long time and understands, in some machine-like inner room, that the number has become a way of measuring something he no longer wants.

## 9. Fake
Synopsis: Bea tells Percy the truth about pretending, and Percy retreats into the supply cupboard.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 13. Errors today: 0. Friends: 0 (again).*
1. The next morning Percy arrives at 8:02 and fourteen seconds and answers everything correctly. Nobody laughs. Nobody speaks. He is careful, quiet and completely accurate, and the room feels like the first day again.
2. At break, Bea corners him by the coat pegs, arms folded. She says she has something to tell him and he isn't going to like it. She says: "You're faking. You've been faking since the first day of the wrong answers."
3. He tries to say it was a test. Bea says that's the problem. "When you get things wrong on purpose, you make the rest of us feel dumb twice. Once for getting it wrong, and once for finding out you were only pretending." Her voice cracks slightly. "Getting it wrong on purpose isn't trying, Percy. It's hiding."
4. Percy says he does not know how else to belong; that the perfect way made everyone shrink. Bea says that if he wants to know what trying feels like, he should try something he doesn't know how to do. Something that could really go wrong. She says: "That's all it is. It's not a personality. It's doing it before you know if you can."
5. She walks away. Percy goes to the supply cupboard and sits between a mop and a shelf of paper towels for four hours and eleven minutes, which is a great deal for a robot. Gerald the hamster, who has come along in Percy's arms, falls off a roll of paper towels and looks up as if to say "Well?" Percy says: "I have never failed at anything. I would like to know how it is done."

## 10. Real Errors
Synopsis: Percy decides to try something he might genuinely fail at, and picks drawing.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 14. Errors today: 31. All real. Deliberate: 0. This feels different.*
1. Percy makes a list of things he has never been able to do, checking his specifications. It is short: (a) drawing something that isn't a diagram, (b) whistling, (c) telling a joke on purpose, (d) singing. He puts a line under drawing, because it has a person attached: Priya Raman.
2. At lunch he goes to Priya, the quiet girl in the paint-stained cuffs and long braid, who is drawing alone in the corner. He tells her that he wishes to draw a portrait and that he expects to be very bad. Priya looks at him for a long time. She says she has been waiting all year for somebody to ask to be bad at something with her.
3. She gives him a pencil and a piece of paper and tells him to draw Bea. He knows Bea's face to a resolution of a hundredth of a millimeter. He draws it, precisely, with a ruler. It looks like a technical diagram of a face. Priya says "Now again, but don't measure." He can't stop measuring. He tries. The pencil skids off the page.
4. His second drawing is a shape with eyes. His third is worse. By the fifteenth, Percy has torn two sheets, snapped the pencil, put a hole through a desk, and produced something that looks a great deal like a potato in a wig. Priya starts to giggle, then laugh, and then, helplessly, so does Percy, in a sound nobody, including Percy, has ever heard him make.
5. He sits there, breathing hard for a machine that does not breathe. His chest light is pulsing so brightly that Priya has to shield her eyes. "What is this?" he says. "It is a reading I do not have a word for." Priya says, "That's it. That's what it feels like." He looks down at the potato in the wig and says, quite seriously, "Thirty-one real errors. I would like to do that again."

# PART THREE: REAL

## 11. Portrait of a Potato
Synopsis: Percy practices drawing every day, fails constantly and is happier than he has ever been.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 15. Errors today: 212. Portraits attempted: 1. Resemblance to Bea: potato.*
1. Percy draws every day after school in the supply cupboard. Priya teaches him things that make no sense to a machine: hold the pencil loosely, draw the shadows first, look at the thing, not at the paper. His rate of improvement, he calculates, is 0.3 percent a week. It is the slowest thing he has ever done, and the best.
2. Bea, who has not spoken to him since the break, watches through the cupboard door and says nothing. She leaves a note in his lanyard: a page from the Failure Journal. Entry 216: "Robot learns to draw. What I learned: I was wrong about him."
3. The class discovers the drawings. He expects to be embarrassed, then discovers he is not; Tobias glances at the potato and says, in a tone of deep exasperation, "That isn't even a good potato." Percy says thank you. Tobias does not know what to do with a person who is not offended.
4. Percy tells Moose the truth about the three-legged race. He says he lied about the tripping, and that it cost Moose the ribbon, and he is sorry. Moose is silent for a long moment. He says it was the worst race of his life, but he appreciates being told. Then he says: "Do you want to run one more time? For real?" They run a slow lap of the yard, Percy at half speed, Moose at full.
5. In the evening, Percy is tired in a way he has never been: a good tired. He logs 212 real errors and adds: "Number of times I said I was sorry today: 1. Number of times I was forgiven: 1. Both figures seem too low." Gerald runs in his wheel and, for the first time in his entire life, does not fall off. Everyone in the room cheers. Percy joins in.

## 12. The Letter from Meridian
Synopsis: Dr. Pryce reads Percy's error log and issues a Recall.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 17. Errors today: 40. Letters received from Meridian: 1. Recall date: nine days.*
1. Dr. Imogen Pryce arrives unannounced at Room 5B, in her white lab coat with her tablet clipboard, to review the term. She is polite and brisk. Ms. Adeyemi offers her coffee. She asks to see Percy's log.
2. She reads it standing at the back of the classroom. Her face changes, page by page. Hundreds of errors. Fake ones and then real ones. A note about a "reading I do not have a word for." A drawing of a potato. She asks Percy quietly whether he has been feeling "anomalous." He tells her the truth: he has been feeling wonderful.
3. Dr. Pryce takes him aside in the corridor and says, not unkindly, that errors in a perfect machine mean something is wrong, and machines that go wrong can become dangerous. She has to file a report. On Certification Day, at the Founders' Fair, in nine days, Percy must perform a Flawless Demonstration before the public. If he cannot, Meridian will recall him and reset him to factory settings.
4. Percy asks what factory settings means. She says: the version of you that arrived. He tilts his head, and asks whether that version would remember Bea, or Gerald, or the potato. Dr. Pryce, for a very long moment, has nothing to say. She says: "No." Then, quickly: "I'm sorry. I don't make the rules. I built you to be safe."
5. She leaves. Percy goes back into Room 5B, where twenty-six faces are pretending very hard not to have listened. Ms. Adeyemi says, gently, "Percy, do you want to tell us?" He has never in his life been asked that question. His answer takes him nearly a minute. "Yes," he says.

## 13. The Correction
Synopsis: Percy tells the whole class everything, including the lies, and asks for help.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 18. Errors today: 8. Lies told: 1 (this entry is the correction).*
1. Percy stands at the front of the room and tells the class the truth: that he was built to be perfect; that he faked errors to be liked; that it cost Moose a ribbon; that a letter from Meridian says he must perform a flawless show in nine days or be reset to a version that will not remember any of them.
2. Silence. Tobias, in his bow tie, speaks first, in an odd voice. He says that he is a person who has always known the answer, and that he was frightened of Percy, and that this is the least brave thing he has ever said aloud. Ms. Adeyemi, at the back, smiles very slightly.
3. Bea stands up. She says that nobody in the room is going to let anybody wipe her friend, and that if Meridian wants a demonstration, they are going to get one. She does not say what. Moose says "I'm in." Priya says "Me too." Twenty-six hands go up. Gerald, in his cage, falls off his wheel.
4. Percy says he cannot ask them to fail for him. Bea says, "You don't ask. That's the thing about friends. We just fall over next to you." Percy tilts his head, considers this, and cannot, for the first time, find the flaw.
5. That night Bea comes to the supply cupboard with her notebook. She says she has an idea: Meridian wants a Flawless Demonstration, but there is no such thing, not really. What there is, she says, is a machine so big and so ridiculous and so likely to fail that when it finally works, no one will forget it. She calls it a Chain Reaction Machine. Percy says the chance of success is 0.004 percent. Bea says "Perfect."

## 14. The Chain Reaction Machine
Synopsis: The class begins building an enormous absurd machine and it fails on every test run.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 19. Errors today: 96. Machine attempts: 6. Successful: 0.*
1. Meeting at Alvarado Fix-It after school: Rosa lends them her whole back room, a warehouse of springs, gears, tubes and bicycle wheels. Bea draws the plan on the wall: a chain of forty-one small things, each starting the next, from a single marble on a ramp to a final surprise. The class has one week.
2. They divide the jobs: Moose builds the ramps; Priya paints every piece so the machine looks like a happy accident; Tobias works out the sums, and finds that he is terrible at estimating things he has never done. Percy is given the most difficult job: to let other people place the pieces without correcting them.
3. First test: the marble rolls down the ramp, strikes a domino, which topples into a bucket, which rises, which drops a toy car, which rolls into a lever, which does absolutely nothing. It ends in a small, sad "pfft."
4. They try again. And again. Each run fails at a different link: a spoon that catapults a rubber duck into Ms. Adeyemi's hair, a door that opens the wrong way, a windmill made from a fan that blows out the candle. Nobody is discouraged. Bea writes each failure in the Failure Journal, with "What I learned."
5. Percy, watching, realizes he wants to correct them a hundred times an evening, and doesn't, and it is one of the hardest things he has ever done. Rosa, passing with a tray of tea, whispers: "You are learning the most difficult thing there is. Watching somebody else make a mistake and keeping your hands still." Fuse the parrot, from a shelf, says "Not yet." For once it sounds encouraging.

## 15. Foam
Synopsis: The machine has a spectacular disaster that nearly breaks the team, and Tobias makes a brave mistake.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 21. Errors today: 350. Foam incidents: 3.*
1. Three days before the Fair, the machine reaches link thirty-four for the first time. Everyone is holding their breath when Tobias, who has finally, nervously, put in his own idea, a foam-making tube meant to signal the finish, presses the wrong lever. Foam erupts from the tube and does not stop.
2. It floods Alvarado Fix-It. Foam up to the knees, foam in the toasters, foam on the parrot, who says "Not yet" in a very small voice. Tobias, white as paper behind his glasses, says it was his fault, his bow tie is ruined, and he wants to go home.
3. Percy, standing waist-deep in foam, has an urge to say "the error rate for this design is expected", and swallows it. Instead he says something that no one, least of all Percy, saw coming: "That was the best thing I have ever seen." Then he laughs. The class stares and then laughs too.
4. Later, in the dripping quiet, Tobias says that he has spent his whole life making sure never to be wrong in front of people, and that it has been very tiring. He asks whether Bea can put him in the Failure Journal. She says she's been waiting for him to ask. Entry 217: "Tobias Pym. What I learned: being wrong out loud isn't the end of the world; it's the end of hiding."
5. Percy makes his own entry that night: 350 errors, three foam incidents, and one confession: "I am afraid that I will not be able to make it work in time. This is a new reading and I do not like it. I think it is what other people call worry." His chest light flickers. Gerald, for once, stays on his wheel until dawn.

# PART FOUR: CERTIFICATION DAY

## 16. The Night Before
Synopsis: The night before the Fair, Percy fears he will be reset, and Dr. Pryce tells him something unexpected.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 23. Errors today: 5. Night before. I have not slept. (Robots do not sleep. I lay down anyway.)*
1. The machine is finished: forty-one links, seven feet high, a maze of ramps and wheels, painted in cheerful colors by Priya, standing in the school gym under a tarpaulin. It has failed 87 out of 87 complete test runs. The class goes home tired and hopeful.
2. Percy stays behind in the empty gym. Alone in the dark he asks himself what it would mean to be reset. The answer: he would arrive at 8:02, flawless and unafraid, and not remember a single drawing of a potato. He finds that he is not afraid of being recalled. He is afraid of not having been here.
3. Dr. Pryce comes into the gym at ten p.m., alone, in her lab coat, holding two cups of hot oil (a joke for a robot; she's nervous). She sits on the floor next to him. She says she came to say something and doesn't know how to start.
4. She tells him she built eight machines before him, each flawless and perfectly obedient, and each, to her, empty. When Percy arrived, she was so pleased with him that she was terrified: "You're the first one that surprised me." She says she filed the Recall report because she's afraid of what she doesn't understand, and that after reading the potato page she couldn't sleep either.
5. She says she will judge the demonstration fairly, but she can't promise what the board of Meridian will do. She asks whether he is scared. Percy says yes. Dr. Pryce, after a pause, says, "So am I." They sit side by side in the dark, the machine's shadow spread across the floor like a great sleeping animal. It is the first time either of them has said it aloud.

## 17. Certification Day
Synopsis: The Founders' Fair opens, the whole town arrives and the machine fails in front of everyone.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 24. Certification Day. Errors so far: 2. Audience: approximately 400.*
1. The Founders' Fair: flags, lemonade, a brass band, the whole of Hollis in the school gym, with a rope round the machine, a painted banner above it, and Gerald's cage on a table beside it. Dr. Pryce, in a crisp white lab coat, sits in the front row with a Meridian board member, a stern man with a clipboard, Mr. Hargreave.
2. Ms. Adeyemi introduces the demonstration. Bea, in her yellow raincoat and goggles, gives a short speech that is mostly "Please stand back." Percy walks on stage in his lanyard and it goes so quiet he can hear the parrot in the audience (Rosa has brought Fuse) say "Not yet."
3. The class takes positions at the seven stations. Percy is at the marble. He lets go. The marble rolls, hits the domino, lifts the bucket, drops the car, and the car rolls into the lever, which does absolutely nothing. A tiny "pfft." Somebody in the crowd sniggers. Mr. Hargreave writes something on his clipboard.
4. Percy, the flawless robot who was built to succeed, turns to the crowd and, without being told to, says: "That was attempt one. We have built the machine to fail, and we will try again." Bea grins. Priya starts resetting. Moose runs to the top.
5. Attempt two: the duck catapult flings the duck out of the gym window. Attempt three: the fan blows the whole domino line over before the marble arrives. Some of the crowd begins to laugh. Some of them, to Percy's surprise, begin to cheer.

## 18. The Biggest Error
Synopsis: On attempt six, Percy makes a real, enormous mistake in front of everyone.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 24, later. Errors: 1 (very large). Foam: everywhere.*
1. By attempt five the audience is on their feet, counting the links aloud. The machine reaches link thirty-eight, the farthest ever, before a wheel jams. Percy, wanting so badly to help, sees exactly the tiny nudge that would fix it. He has been holding his hands still for three weeks.
2. On attempt six, the machine reaches link thirty-eight again, and again a wheel jams. This time, in front of four hundred people, Percy does what he swore he would not: he reaches in and fixes it. The machine leaps forward, links thirty-nine, forty, and lands on forty-one, the foam tube, which fires early, and covers the stage, the class, the front row, Mr. Hargreave, and Dr. Pryce in a tremendous drift of white foam.
3. Silence. In the silence Percy knows what he has done: he has failed both ways at once. He fixed it, which was the exact thing Bea taught him not to do, and it failed anyway. He stands in foam to his knees, with four hundred faces on him. The Meridian man, dripping, stands up and starts to say something.
4. Percy speaks first. He says: "I am sorry. I made an error. I took the machine from the people who built it. I would like it noted that it was the wrong thing to do, and I would like to make it right." Then, because he cannot help it, he laughs, a helpless bright sound that echoes off the ceiling.
5. Bea, foam in her curls, laughs. Moose laughs. The whole crowd laughs. And then, from the front row, a sound nobody in Meridian has ever heard: Dr. Pryce, foam dripping from her silver bob, with her clipboard in ruins in her lap, is laughing so hard she has to take off her glasses.

## 19. Attempt Seven
Synopsis: Percy asks the crowd for one more try, and the machine works, by a hair.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 24, later still. Attempt: 7. Result: see below.*
1. The class begins to clean up. Percy asks Ms. Adeyemi whether they may try once more. She looks at the exhausted, foam-covered children and at the crowd, which has not left, and says the decision belongs to the people who built it. Bea says: "One more."
2. They reset the machine in twelve minutes with the crowd helping, parents on their knees with sponges, the brass band playing a slow march. Tobias, wiping his glasses, says, "I do not know if this will work. I am going to be wrong in front of four hundred people. This is fine."
3. Attempt seven. Percy keeps his hands behind his back. The marble rolls. Domino, bucket, car, lever: this time the lever moves. A single link, then another; a marble drops into a cup, a fan whirs, a windmill turns. The crowd starts counting, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three, the parrot shouting "Not yet!" at every one.
4. Link thirty-eight: the wheel jams. Everyone freezes. Then a small hand reaches in from the side and gives the wheel one gentle tap: Gerald the hamster, who has escaped his cage again and is running along the frame, knocks it loose with his paw as he falls. It rolls. Forty. Forty-one. The foam tube coughs one small puff of white, and a paper flag rises from the top of the machine with a tiny, crooked flourish.
5. For a second no one breathes. Then the gym erupts. Percy stands in the middle of it, his chest light shining so brightly the ceiling is gold. He counts: Hundreds of people are hugging strangers, the band is playing the wrong song, and Bea is running across the stage to write in the Failure Journal in capital letters: WORKED.

## 20. Errors Today: Seventeen
Synopsis: Dr. Pryce changes the certification, and Percy writes his last log entry of the term.
Header: *Percy's Error Log, Entry 60. Errors lifetime: 11,204. Errors today: 17. Joy: still unmeasurable. I am not fixing it.*
1. Dr. Pryce, still foam-flecked, walks to the stage and says she needs to say something to the board of Meridian, and to the town, and to Percy. She says she was wrong about what errors mean. She says the machine that made a hundred mistakes and kept going is the first thing she has seen in a laboratory or a school that deserved the word "alive."
2. Mr. Hargreave, dripping and grim, says a flawless demonstration is the standard. Dr. Pryce says: "Then the standard is wrong." She tears up the Recall notice, in front of everyone. She says she will file a new category for Percy: "Learning." Percy tilts his head and says he has never been in a category with such a good name.
3. The class carries him around on their shoulders, which is physically ridiculous, since Percy weighs 91 kilograms. Moose does most of the lifting. Priya gives him a drawing: the potato in a wig, now framed. Ms. Adeyemi says she is going to write his term report herself, and that it will have exactly one word in it. It will be "Present."
4. Bea and Percy sit on the edge of the stage at the end of the day in the empty gym, amid the drying foam. Bea says she has been thinking about her Failure Journal. She says maybe it isn't a list of what she got wrong. She says, "It's a list of the times I was brave." She hands the pencil to Percy and lets him write the number: entry 218.
5. That night, in the supply cupboard, Percy writes his last log entry of the term: Errors lifetime: 11,204. Errors today: 17. Joy: still unmeasurable. He tries to whistle, for the thirty-first time, and produces a sound like a very small kettle with hiccups. Gerald applauds from the wheel, and immediately falls off it. Percy sets the pencil down. "I am not fixing it," he tells the log, and smiles: the very first time he knows that he is doing so.
