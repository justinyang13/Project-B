# THE DAY THAT WOULDN'T END — story bible

Funny middle-grade time-loop novel (ages 10-12). Warm, fast, laugh-out-loud, with a real ache underneath. No gore, no romance, nothing spooky.

## Voice (this is the most important part)
FIRST PERSON, past tense, narrated by Max Bello, age 11, telling the story afterwards. Wry, quick, observant, a little defensive, secretly soft. He talks to the reader directly ("You should know...", "Here is the thing about my brother."). He loves precise numbers and lists ("Things that were wrong with Saturday: 1..."). He undercuts big feelings with a joke, then, at the truest moments, drops the jokes and gets very plain and quiet. Short sentences mixed with the occasional long runaway one. No adult vocabulary, no purple prose, no similes about angels or oceans. His comparisons come from an 11-year-old's world: cereal, video games, school, siblings, the dentist, gym class, lost socks.
Voice sample:
"Here is what you need to know about my little brother Gus: he had not taken off his T. rex costume in forty-one days. Not for baths (he sat in the tub in it, like a wet green sofa). Not for picture day. Not for the dentist, who said 'open wide' and got a roar in the face. So when I tell you that at 6:47 on the morning of the worst day of my life a dinosaur climbed onto my chest and screamed, you should not be surprised. You should be asking a different question, which is: why did I wake up already feeling like a person who had lost?"

## The premise
Max Bello's family is moving from Dunmore, Ohio to Tucson, Arizona (1,700 miles) because Dad got a job at a solar-panel company. The moving truck comes at 7:00 Sunday morning. Today, Saturday in late June, is Max's last day in the only home and town he has ever known. He decides it will be the worst day of his life, and he works hard at it: sulks, snaps at everyone, refuses to say goodbye. At 11:58 that night, alone in the hall in front of the cuckoo clock his Grandma Bea gave him, he wishes: "I wish today would never end." The cuckoo clock's bird, Gerald, pops out and gives one crooked cuckoo. At 6:47 the next morning Max wakes up... on the same Saturday. The day loops. Everyone resets; only Max remembers. (Objects reset too. What Max learns and who he becomes are the only things that carry over.)

## Loop rules (Max works these out in chapters 5-6)
- Every loop starts at 6:47 a.m. Saturday with Gus roaring on his chest, and ends when Max falls asleep or at midnight. Falling asleep, running away, staying awake, all lead back to 6:47.
- Everyone else resets fully and remembers nothing. Max remembers everything. Cuts, bruises, sunburn, and pancakes eaten all reset. Nothing he does has lasting outside consequences.
- The day is the same every time unless Max changes it. A precise SCHEDULE exists (below) and Max memorizes it. Small variations appear when he interferes.
- Max never learns why. The clock never speaks. The ending does not explain the magic; it only shows Max stops asking the loop for anything.
- Total: he lives Saturday number 1 (the real one) to Saturday number 100. Chapter 20 is the Sunday after Saturday #100.

## The Saturday SCHEDULE (the loop's fixed events; Max knows them by heart)
- 6:47 Gus, in his T. rex costume, climbs on Max's chest and roars.
- 7:13 Waffles steals Max's left slipper and hides under the dining table.
- 7:22 Dad burns the toast. Smoke alarm. Dad's first pun: "I'm reading a book about anti-gravity. It's impossible to put down."
- 8:05 Mrs. Abernathy from next door arrives with a hot dish (tuna noodle casserole, potato chips on top). She says, "It's a hot dish, so be careful, you'll want a trivet."
- 8:30 Jonah texts: "I HAVE A PLAN. Be at my house at 2. Wear shoes you can get wet."
- 9:15 A moving-box crisis: the box marked FRAGILE/KITCHEN gets dropped; Mom saves it. Mom is always holding a clipboard with a 6-page checklist.
- 10:30 Max and Tessa fight in the garage over the last box (her drum practice pad and old band posters); she slams the door; she goes quiet.
- 11:00 Jonah shows up early in his bike helmet with a laminated itinerary for "the Last Day Extravaganza" (creek, minigolf at Putt-Putt Palace, ice cream at Scoops). Max says he might not come.
- 12:30 Lunch: peanut butter sandwiches on paper towels because the plates are packed.
- 1:15 Grandma Bea arrives in her huge old green Buick (the horn plays a tune) carrying the cuckoo clock in a laundry basket. She hangs it on the last hook in the front hall.
- 2:00 Jonah's Extravaganza starts. On the first Saturday Max hides on the garage roof instead and ignores Jonah's calls.
- 4:10 Mr. Grady's lawn sprinkler next door soaks the mailman.
- 5:30 Pizza on the floor of the empty living room, paper plates. Family goodbye toasts. On the first Saturday Max is horrible about it.
- 7:00 Dusk. 9:00 Gus's last bath in the costume. 10:30 Everyone's asleep on air mattresses. 11:58 Max, alone in the hall with the clock: the wish. 11:59 Gerald.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Max Bello**, 11. Narrator. Skinny, tan skin, messy dark-brown hair with a cowlick at the back, wears a faded red hoodie even in June ("my armor"), big grey sneakers with the left one double-knotted. Grumpy, clever, funny, stubborn, secretly terrified of starting over. Believes: happiness is something that happens to you when things go right.
- **Dad (Sal Bello)**, 40s. Big, round belly, thick dark mustache, an apron with a cartoon fish on it. Cheerful, constantly makes terrible puns (they are his nerves). Burns toast every day. Hugs like a bear.
- **Mom (Mira Bello)**, 40s. Curly black hair in a clip, pencil behind her ear, always carrying a clipboard with a 6-page checklist. Organized, brisk, funny when tired. Her lists are her panic.
- **Tessa Bello**, 15. Max's sister. A purple streak in her dark hair, headphones around her neck, black boots, drumsticks in her back pocket. Sarcastic, dramatic, very loyal. Secret: terrified of starting at a new high school with no friends, having left her band (the Kettle Drums) behind.
- **Gus Bello**, 6. Wears a green T. rex costume with a hood and a spiky tail; missing one front tooth; has worn it for 41 days. Roars for hello. Secret: is worried that the new house won't have a room where a T. rex can live.
- **Waffles**, the family dog, a chubby corgi with a white front paw, steals slippers.
- **Jonah Park**, 11. Max's best friend since kindergarten. Tall, skinny, buzz cut, huge grin, always wears a bike helmet (safety first). Loyal, enthusiastic, makes laminated plans. His texts are always in ALL CAPS. Cries at dog movies (a private fact Max uses to prove things to him). Loves Max without saying so.
- **Grandma Bea (Beatrice Bello)**, Dad's mother, 78. Tiny, big round sunglasses, red lipstick, gold bangles that jingle, drives an enormous green Buick. Born in Naples, Italy, and moved to Ohio at 11 (the same age as Max). Sprinkles Italian words (caro, mamma mia). Max calls her Nonna. Blunt, warm, tells stories sideways. Owns the family's cuckoo clock (bought in 1961).
- **Gerald**, the cuckoo. A small painted wooden bird with a crooked beak and angry eyebrows. Pops out with a fussy "cuck-oo." Never speaks. Sometimes seems to pop out at exactly the wrong (or right) time; never explained.
- **Mrs. Abernathy**, 80s, neighbor. Tiny, floral gardening hat, prize tomato plants, brings a tuna noodle casserole every Saturday at 8:05 and says the same line about the hot dish and the trivet.
- **Mr. Grady**, neighbor, whose sprinkler soaks the mailman at 4:10.
- Setting: 22 Larkspur Lane, Dunmore, Ohio. A yellow two-storey house with a garage roof Max climbs onto. Scoops (ice cream), Putt-Putt Palace, Weller Creek.

## Running gags (plant early, pay off later)
Gus's roar (Max eventually roars back); Waffles's slipper; the 7:22 burnt toast (the toast turns out to be good with peanut butter and Max stops minding); Dad's puns (Max learns them all and eventually tells Dad's pun before he can); Mrs. Abernathy's hot dish line (Max says it along with her, then finally asks her something about herself and learns she lost her husband and cooks for people because it's how she says I care); Jonah's helmet and laminated itinerary; the tally marks Max scratches in pencil on the inside of his closet wall; Tessa's drumsticks tapping on everything; Gerald appearing at odd times.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Happiness is a choice, not a result. Max believes he'll be happy when things go right: when the move is cancelled, when the day is perfect, when everyone else is happy. The loop keeps proving that wrong. He chases FUN (empty), then a PERFECT DAY (impossible), then making OTHERS happy (exhausting, and it undoes itself every reset). At his lowest he shouts terrible things at his family. Grandma Bea's story of her own worst day (a peach at an airport) shows him the trick: you decide, first thing, on purpose, before the day proves anything. Choosing happiness is not pretending; he can be sad about leaving AND choose to be glad in the day he's in. The loop ends only when Max stops bargaining with it and lets the day end.
Grandma Bea's peach story: when she was 11, moving from Naples to Ohio, she stood in a bleak airport furious at everything. A stranger handed her half a peach. It was the best peach she ever tasted. She decided then that she'd eat the peach: "The day was going to be terrible either way, caro. I got to pick whether I was terrible in it."

## Things to avoid
No ghosts, no darkness, no romance. The magic is never explained. Do not let the narrator state the lesson outright until (at most) one plain sentence in chapter 19 or 20. Do not repeat jokes. Vary each retold Saturday: when the same day is revisited, only show what is new, faster and funnier.
