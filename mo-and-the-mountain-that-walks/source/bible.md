# MO AND THE MOUNTAIN THAT WALKS — story bible

Epic-cozy fantasy adventure for ages 10-12. A village of two hundred and twelve people lives on the back of a mountain-sized, living creature named Tor, who walks a great circle of the land every year. One spring morning she turns the wrong way. Warm, wry, wondrous, funny; a slow-walking road story with a big heart. Nothing spooky, no violence, no villain, no romance. Danger is weather, rivers, cliffs and fear of change.

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Mo, with a warm storyteller narrator who is allowed a dry aside now and then (like a grandmother telling a tale she loves): specific, funny, concrete, big landscapes seen at a child's height. Plain words, short scenes, real feeling when it matters. Mo can hear a mountain but not always his best friend: keep that irony alive. Avoid stacked similes, avoid "It wasn't X. It was Y.", avoid "heart hammered", "stone in the throat", and one-word emphatic paragraphs.
Every chapter opens with one italic line from GRAN'S WALK-BOOK, on its own line, then a blank line, in this exact form: "*From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 1 of the Long Walk. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: (an ink blot).*" The entries are terse, funny and specific; day numbers rise; the last entry answers the first. The Header line is added by the script: never write it on any page.
Voice sample:
"Most mornings on the mountain began with a bell, and this one did too, but it was the wrong sort of bell: a single cracked clang from the rope tower, the kind you ring when a goat is in the cheese.

Mo Ferreira did not need the bell. Mo was already awake, because Mo was always awake for the first step of the Long Walk. He stood on the flat stone of the Footpost in the middle of Highstep, barefoot, with his eyes shut and his toes spread, waiting for the whole village to lean the way it had leaned every spring of his life."

## The premise
Highstep, a village of two hundred and twelve people, goats, cheese and rope bridges, sits among the pines on the shoulders of Tor, an enormous, shaggy, moss-backed creature the size of a mountain, who has four great stone-grey legs, a long low head with two grey stone horns and small kind eyes. Nobody knows what Tor is; she has always walked. Every spring she wakes and follows the same Old Road for forty days: Spring Meadows, Summer Lake, Autumn Orchards, then the Winter Hollows, at about three miles a day, and the villagers plant, herd, mend and trade as they go. Highstep's Stepkeeper is Mo's Gran, who writes the Walk-Book. Mo, eleven, is her apprentice and has the Footsense: through his bare soles he can feel Tor's steps, her mood, even a little of what she means (two long steps = yes; a pause = wait; a shiver like a shrug = itch).
On Day 1 of the Long Walk this year, Tor turns left. She keeps going left. She is walking away from the Old Road toward wild, empty country. The grown-ups panic and decide that the mountain is lost, or ill, or that a mountain has no opinions. Mo is sure she knows exactly where she is going. Nobody listens to an eleven-year-old.

## The truth (revealed late; do NOT leak early)
Tor is very old, and this is her last Long Walk. When a walking mountain grows old, she walks to the sea to the Gathering, where all the walking mountains come together for a single dawn every thousand years, and lies down to rest, and becomes an island. The villages on her back become seaside villages that never move again. It has happened before: Gran's great-great-grandmothers knew the story and tore the page out of the Walk-Book because they could not bear it. Gran found the page forty years ago and hid it in her walking staff, afraid of what it meant for the Walk and for Highstep.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Mo Ferreira**, 11. Small, wiry, warm brown skin, untidy black hair, always BAREFOOT with dusty feet, a patched green wool poncho, a rope belt with a small brass plumb-bob. Stubborn, funny, honest; he hears the mountain but often talks over the people he loves. Habit: presses one bare heel to the ground when he is thinking.
- **Gran (Ada Ferreira)**, the Stepkeeper, 70s: tiny, upright, a long white braid, huge round spectacles, a tall walking staff with a small brass bell, an ink-stained thumb. Dry, brisk, secretly frightened. Keeps the Walk-Book. Refrain: "Write it down, then worry about it."
- **Junie Achebe**, 11, Mo's best friend: tall, dark brown skin, a big round puff of black curls tied up with a yellow scarf, a leather tool-belt full of nails and pegs, freckle-free, sharp-tongued, kind. Wants one thing: a house whose floor never tilts. Keeps a scrapbook of drawings of "still towns." Her family: Mama Achebe (weaver), Papa Achebe (carpenter), baby brother Obi.
- **Admiral**, a very large goat: brown and white with one crooked horn and a tin bell, who behaves as if he commands the village. Follows Mo everywhere, eats maps, stubborn, loyal, comic. Never talks.
- **Mum (Marisol Ferreira)**, the cheese-maker: strong forearms, apron, flour-dust laugh. **Dad (Tomás Ferreira)**, rope-mender: big, gentle, always whistling through his teeth, coils of rope over one shoulder. They disagree about leaving.
- **Mayor Delphine Marsh**: round, brisk, a tall hat with a green sash of office, a clipboard, loves order. Not a villain: she wants everyone safe. Learns to trust.
- **Old Hale**, the Ear-Keeper, 80s: a lean hermit with a wild white beard who lives in caves in Tor's ear and hears her hum in words; cheerful, cryptic, wears a knitted cap with earflaps.
- **Mrs. Cobb (Wilhelmina Cobb)**, a farmer from Lowbrook on the plain below: stout, straw hat, wheelbarrow, blunt and warm; calls Tor "that big lump."
- **Tor**: colossal, mossy, pine-covered back, stone-grey legs, low head with two grey stone horns, small kind eyes; slow, patient, curious; hums a note you feel in your teeth. She answers only through her steps, her hum and her body (a kneel, a turn, a long breath). Never speaks in words.
- Places: Highstep (village on her shoulders: the Green, the rope tower, the Footpost, the cheese caves, the bell library), the Spine Ridge, the Ear caves, the Crown (her highest point), the Tail (waterfall of a stream that runs down her tail), the Fold under the shoulder (moss gardens), the Old Road, Stony Pass and the Last Ladders (rope ladders down to the plain at the pass), Lowbrook (a farm town on the plain), the Slow River and its marsh, the sea, the Gathering.

## The plan of the story
Ch1-5: the morning Tor turns; the village meeting; Mo's Footsense; the torn page in the library; the Ear-Keeper. Ch6-10: the vote to leave at the Last Ladder in fifteen days, the family splits; river crossing and goat stampede; a storm where Tor shelters them; the Fold; Mo and Junie's big quarrel (he cannot hear her fear). Ch11-15: Hale's story; Gran's confession and the torn page; nobody believes Mo; the Crown at night and the sight of other mountains' lights; the Last Ladder: Junie's family and sixty others leave. Ch16-20: half-empty Highstep; the flood on the plain: Tor kneels and carries Lowbrook and the descended to safety; everyone together; the Gathering at the sea at dawn, seven mountains; Tor lies down; Highstep stays: the last Walk-Book entry.

## Motifs
The Walk-Book entries (Distance and Direction lines); the Footpost and Mo's bare feet; Tor's step-language (two long = yes, a pause = wait); Admiral eating paper; Junie's still-town scrapbook and the yellow scarf; the private lantern signal (two long, one short = "I'm here"); cheese; the brass bell on Gran's staff; the low hum you feel in your teeth.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Home is the people you are with, not the place you stand on: the mountain can move, or lie down for ever, and Highstep is still Highstep. Also: listening (Mo hears a mountain but not his best friend) and the courage to change. One plain line of it near the end, from Gran or Mo, at most. Keep grief gentle: Tor is resting, not gone; her hum lingers.

## Things to avoid
No scary content, no villain, no violence, no romance. Tor never speaks. Don't leak the Gathering, the sea, the ending or the old story before Ch11 (Hale's story) except a torn page and a hint. Nothing modern (no phones, cars, electricity); a lantern, rope, wood and wool world.
