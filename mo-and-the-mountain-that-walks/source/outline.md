# PART ONE: THE TURN

## 1. The Morning the Mountain Turned
Synopsis: On the first morning of the Long Walk, Mo feels Tor take the wrong step, and the whole village of Highstep finds out the mountain is walking somewhere new.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 1 of the Long Walk. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: (an ink blot).*
1. Introduce the village on the back of a walking mountain with a dry storyteller's voice: Highstep, two hundred and twelve people, forty goats, one bell tower, and Tor, who has walked the same great circle every year for as long as anyone has kept a Walk-Book. Mo Ferreira, barefoot on the flat stone of the Footpost in the middle of the village, waits with his eyes shut for the first step of the Long Walk, as he does every spring.
2. Mo's Footsense: through his soles he feels Tor wake, the deep slow roll of her shoulders, the hum in his teeth. His best friend Junie Achebe, tall, tool-belt full of pegs, yellow scarf, arrives and complains that the floor of her house has tipped again; she says one day she will live in a house that never leans. Admiral the goat eats a corner of somebody's washing.
3. The village gets ready the way it does every spring: Dad whistling on the rope tower, Mum carrying cheese wheels down to the cellar, Mayor Marsh with her clipboard ringing the Starting Bell, and Gran with her staff and her Walk-Book, writing down the weather.
4. Tor takes her first step. It is a good, big, ordinary step, and then the second step comes down a little to the left. Mo's eyes fly open. The old Road goes right, through the birch gap toward the Spring Meadows. Tor is going left.
5. By the third step everybody feels it. The cheese wheels roll, the bell clangs by itself, the Mayor's hat slips. Gran, pen in the air, stops writing, and where the word for the direction should go, she lets a drop of ink fall. Ahead of the village, for the first time in a thousand years, lies a road nobody has walked.

## 2. The Walk-Book Says Right
Synopsis: The village argues on the Green about whether the mountain is lost, sick or simply wrong, and Mo tries to tell them she is not.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 1, evening. Weather: fair, then loud. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. The Mayor has asked me not to write "left."*
1. By noon Tor has kept walking left, and the whole village gathers on the Green, where the Mayor stands on a cheese crate. Mayor Marsh loves an agenda: item one, the mountain is off the Road.
2. The argument: Mrs. Pell says Tor must be ill, the baker says it is a tremor of the ground, somebody says mountains do not have opinions. Gran reads from the Walk-Book: eleven hundred and four years of Long Walks, all with the same route. The Winter Hollows are where the hay and firewood are; nothing is planted anywhere else.
3. Mo, small and barefoot, says from the back that Tor is not lost, she is going somewhere. Everybody turns to look at him. The Mayor says kindly that she is sure he feels things, but a mountain cannot decide. Mo says she can. He gets laughed at, gently, which is worse.
4. Junie backs him up for exactly one sentence, then wonders aloud what happens if the mountain does not come back to the Road: could people leave, could there be a house on ground that stays still? A few heads nod. Mo stares at her; she looks away.
5. That night Mo lies on the flat stone of the Footpost instead of in bed, one heel pressed to the rock, listening. Tor steps, steps, steps, a slow patient rhythm that says, very plainly to him, I know where I am going. Admiral chews his poncho. Mo whispers: "Where?" A pause, then two long steps. It is the first answer she has ever given.

## 3. Two Long Steps
Synopsis: Gran tests Mo's Footsense and teaches him what Tor's steps can say, while a torn page in the old book hints that Highstep has forgotten something.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 3. Weather: mist. Distance: three miles. Direction: still left. The goats have stopped believing in the Road.*
1. Morning on the rope tower with Gran, who has pretended to be busy for two days and now admits she believes Mo. She takes him to the Footpost and quizzes him: what is Tor feeling? He answers with his feet: content, curious, a little tired.
2. Gran teaches him the step-words the Stepkeepers have kept for centuries: two long steps for yes, a pause for wait, a shiver like a shrug for itch, three light steps for she is thinking. Mo has always felt these but never had names for them. It is like being handed a dictionary for a language he already speaks.
3. Mo tries to ask a question the way you would ask a friend: are you all right? Two long steps. Are you sick? A pause. Are we lost? A shiver, then a tiny quick step that makes Mo laugh out loud. Admiral butts him because he is standing on the goat's favorite rock.
4. Gran shows him the Walk-Book shelf in the bell library: eleven hundred and four years in fat leather volumes. Mo notices, in the oldest one, a place where a page has been cut out with a knife, very neatly, long ago. Gran says quickly that books lose pages, and turns away a little too fast.
5. Mo says he will find out where Tor is going, if he has to ask every rock on her back. Gran says "Write it down, then worry about it," gives him a small blank notebook of his own, and looks at the horizon, where the birch gap has shrunk to a smudge behind them.

## 4. The Missing Page
Synopsis: Mo and Junie search the bell library for clues and quarrel a little over what Highstep should do.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 4. Weather: drizzle. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. Admiral has eaten Tuesday.*
1. Mo and Junie in the dusty bell library among the old Walk-Books, a rainy afternoon; Admiral has followed them in and is eating the corner of an eleven-hundred-year-old map, which they rescue too late. The librarian, Mr. Bittle, snores under a book.
2. Junie shows Mo her secret scrapbook: drawings of still towns, houses with floors like tabletops, a garden that stays where you planted it, a school with no ropes. She says she has never told anybody. Mo says it is a good book. She is quietly pleased.
3. They search the eldest Walk-Books for any other year when the mountain went left. Nothing. Then Junie finds a loose sheet tucked into a binding: a faded drawing of a mountain-shaped thing lying down beside a wide flat glitter, with a village at its feet. There are no words. She is thrilled; Mo is thoughtful; the drawing is torn at the edge.
4. Junie reads it as proof that people once settled somewhere and it worked, so Highstep could too. Mo reads it as proof that Tor has somewhere to go. Both are right and neither hears the other. They squabble in whispers over the library table until Mr. Bittle wakes.
5. They part on the library steps a little cross with each other. Mo puts the drawing in his notebook and does not tell Junie that he has decided to climb to the Ear-Keeper, who is said to hear Tor's hum in words.

## 5. The Ear-Keeper
Synopsis: Mo climbs the Spine Ridge in the dark to Old Hale, who hears Tor sing, and learns that the song has changed.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 6. Weather: clear, cold. Distance: three miles. Direction: left, and confident. Mo is missing from supper. Again.*
1. Mo sets out at dusk along the Spine Ridge, the long ridge of grey rock and pine that runs down Tor's back, with Admiral trotting after him, unasked, bell clinking. The Ridge is quiet and enormous; far below the village lights glitter like a spilled jar of sugar.
2. The Ear caves at the head end, where Tor's great ear lifts out of the pines: a hollow, lit by a single lantern. Old Hale, the Ear-Keeper, lean and cheerful, in a knitted cap with earflaps and a beard like a snowdrift, is sitting on a stool with his eyes closed, humming back at the mountain.
3. Hale says he has heard Tor's hum in words all his life, but that he will not talk to anybody who fidgets. Mo has to sit in silence for the whole of a long slow hum, not moving, not asking, only listening. It is the hardest thing he has done all year. Admiral falls asleep on his feet.
4. When Mo has finally been still, Hale tells him the song has changed. For fifty years it was a walking song: forward, forward, and around. Since this spring it has been a different sort, slow, rounder, with a long low note at the end of each line. "It sounds," says Hale, "like a song about going home."
5. Mo asks whose home. Hale opens one eye and says that is the question, isn't it, and he will say no more tonight: some things you have to hear for yourself. Below them, from the back of the mountain, a very small bell rings: someone in the village has started to pack.

# PART TWO: THE WILDS

## 6. The Vote
Synopsis: The village decides that anyone who wants to leave the mountain may climb down at the Last Ladder, and Mo's own family splits over it.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 8. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. There will be a vote. There is always a vote.*
1. The village sees the peaks of the wild country, a long grey range with no road. Mayor Marsh calls a Vote on the Green: the Old Road is lost; the winter stores will not last; at Stony Pass, fifteen days ahead, Tor must squeeze through a gap and the Last Ladders will hang down to the plain, where the farm town of Lowbrook stands. Anyone who wants to leave Highstep for solid ground may climb down there.
2. Gran refuses to vote and says only that the Stepkeeper keeps the book, she does not decide the walk. Mo watches the hands go up: not everybody, but a great many. Junie's family are among the first, and Junie looks at her boots.
3. Dinner at the Ferreiras'. Mum, who has kept goats and cheese on a moving hill all her life, says a house that stays still would let her rest her back. Dad, who mends the ropes that keep the village up, says Highstep is people who walk together and he cannot picture a Highstep on the ground. They talk in low, careful voices. Mo says they should ask Tor. Both parents say, at the same time, "Mo."
4. Mo goes to bed furious and lies awake counting: one hundred and ten hands up, ninety-eight down, four people who could not decide. It is the first time in his life the village has not agreed about anything.
5. In the dark, Admiral climbs onto the bed and Mo tells him that a mountain could not possibly be wrong all by herself. Admiral, who has eaten the vote (a slip of paper from the Mayor's cheese crate), looks unrepentant. Mo laughs in spite of himself, and then he does not.

## 7. The Goat Stampede
Synopsis: Tor wades a river and the village's whole herd of goats bolts down her tail; Mo and Junie must work together to save them.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 10. Weather: wet, then wetter. Distance: two miles and a swim. Direction: left, across a river nobody has named.*
1. A wide brown river lies across the path, and Tor walks into it as if it were a bath. Water pours off her flanks in silver sheets; the village cheers or shrieks, depending on how near the edge they are standing. A rope bridge along her side sways like a hammock.
2. The herd of forty goats, spooked by the water, bolts along the Tail toward the roaring waterfall where the mountain's stream tumbles from her hindquarters. Admiral leads them, of course. Mo and Junie, who are meant to be mending a fence, chase them along the swaying bridge.
3. Junie, without a word, tosses Mo a peg and points to a loose rope; she has worked out in a second where the goats will run. Mo uses his Footsense to feel exactly when Tor lifts her tail, and they time their move. It works like a pair of hands clapping, and they do not have to speak to make it work.
4. They wrangle the herd into the safe hollow of the Fold, a mossy alcove under the shoulder, dripping and laughing, goats piled up like a giant woolly cushion. Admiral is missing; then he is found sitting on the very top with a piece of rope in his mouth and a look that says it was all his plan.
5. For a whole minute the quarrel about leaving is forgotten. Junie says quietly that this is what she will miss, this, and Mo says "Miss?" and she says she is not going to talk about it, and they both look at their wet boots. He feels something sink in him like a stone in a pond.

## 8. The Storm on Her Back
Synopsis: A great storm hits the mountain, and Tor does something remarkable to protect the village.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 12. Weather: a storm of the old sort. Distance: one mile. Direction: down, mostly.*
1. A wind comes down from the grey range and the sky goes the color of a bruise. Mayor Marsh orders everyone to the storm cellars; Dad and the rope crew race along the rope bridges tying down roofs. Mo's feet tell him something before the thunder does: Tor is stopping.
2. The storm hits like a hand. Rain sideways, pines bending, the bell tower swaying, the rope bridges cracking like whips. A roof goes off the cheese caves and sails away with the Mayor's hat under it. Mo, ordered to shelter with Gran, runs to the Footpost instead.
3. Mo puts his heel to the stone and feels Tor do something she has never done in Gran's lifetime: she turns. Very slowly, with a groan like a hundred doors, she swings her great shoulder round into the wind so the whole village sits in the lee of her, like a house behind a wall. The winds still scream, but the village barely moves.
4. In the storm cellar, families hear it and go quiet. Gran, who is holding the Walk-Book under her cloak, says she has never seen a mountain shelter her people in the whole eleven hundred and four years of records. She asks Mo, not the Mayor, what Tor is saying. Mo says: I'm here.
5. After the storm the sky is clear and scrubbed and the village comes out into a world of dripping pines and cheese wheels lodged in trees. Somebody laughs. Mayor Marsh finds her hat, wet, on Admiral's horn, and looks at it for a long time. She says nothing, but she stops calling the mountain "the problem."

## 9. What Tor Carries
Synopsis: Mo and Junie explore the Fold and find the carved marks of the people who lived on Tor before Highstep.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 14. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. The goats are unpacked. The Mayor is not speaking to the goats.*
1. Mo and Junie, on an errand to bring Mrs. Pell a sack of moss for her sore knees, climb down into the Fold under Tor's shoulder: a place of dripping ferns, glowing lichen and warm damp air, where nobody goes because it is far from any rope bridge. It smells of rain and old stone.
2. On the walls of the Fold, half-hidden by ferns, they find carvings: tally marks, handprints, tiny stick figures on the mountain's back, and boats. Junie reads the marks like a builder; Mo reads them by touch, running his bare hand along the grooves. Some of the handprints are very small.
3. The carvings run in a long row, one hand for every year of the Walk. Then, at the end of the row, one broken picture: a mountain lying down under a wide curve of water, and a crowd of tiny figures standing on the shore holding hands. Junie stares at it. This is the drawing from the library.
4. Junie says maybe this is what happens to a village when it stops: it gets a shore, a house that stays. She says it flatly, to see how Mo takes it. Mo says he does not want a shore, he wants Highstep. Junie says Highstep is just a word for a group of houses, and Mo hears her, but not the shake underneath.
5. They climb out together in silence. At the top, Admiral is waiting on a rock with the missing library map hanging out of his mouth in tatters. Mo takes it and tucks the scraps in his notebook. It is, unexpectedly, the map of the ground ahead: a river, a plain, and far away a line of blue.

## 10. Halfway to Nowhere
Synopsis: Food runs low, the grownups snap at each other, and Mo and Junie have their worst quarrel.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 16. Weather: bright and hard. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. Supplies: I have counted them twice. Once was enough.*
1. Rationing begins. Mayor Marsh stands on her cheese crate reading out figures: hay for the goats, barley, salt. There is no hay in the wild country. The goats grumble, the children look serious, people who had all been friends start saying "Well, if we had gone right." A fight breaks out over a sack of oats, ended by Dad with a rope.
2. Junie's family begins packing in earnest: Mama Achebe folding her looms, Papa Achebe sharpening his saw. Mo watches from the rope tower and feels his stomach drop. When Junie comes up to fetch a mallet he asks how long she has known she was going.
3. It comes out in a rush: she has known since the day the mountain turned. She wants a floor that does not tilt, a house that is still there in the morning, a place she can find again. Her mother is scared, and so is she, and she thinks the vote is right. She asks him to come with them.
4. Mo, who has never been afraid of anything he could feel through the ground, says she is giving up on Tor, on Highstep, on him. He does not hear that she is frightened; he only hears that she is leaving. Junie says he can hear a mountain but not one single word she says. It lands like a slap. She takes her mallet and goes.
5. Mo sits alone on the rope tower until the lanterns come out, knowing she was right, and not ready to admit it. Far below, Admiral bleats; the bell on his neck rings once. It is the loneliest sound Mo has ever heard.

# PART THREE: WHAT THE MOUNTAIN KNEW

## 11. Hale's Story
Synopsis: Mo returns to the Ear-Keeper, who at last tells him the old story of what happens when a mountain grows old.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 17. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. I have not slept. The book is heavier than it looks.*
1. Mo climbs the Ridge again before dawn, alone but for Admiral, and this time he sits without fidgeting from the very first moment. He tells Hale about the vote and Junie and everything. Hale nods and pours him a mug of something hot that tastes of pine.
2. Hale says it is the oldest story on the mountain and the oldest secret: Tor is not the only walking mountain. There were nine once, and there are seven now, and each walks her own circle for a thousand years. When one grows old she walks to the sea, where the others meet her for a single dawn, the Gathering, and there she lies down.
3. "Lies down?" says Mo. Hale says she does not die. She rests. She becomes an island and the villages on her back become shore villages, and they stay, for ever, on a mountain that does not walk any more. The people of her back have always had a choice: go or stay. Some old villagers leave; some stay. It has been a thousand years since anyone in Highstep has heard the story, because a Stepkeeper long ago tore out the page.
4. Mo can hardly speak. He says Tor is old? Hale says very old, and that her steps have grown slower each spring, and Mo has felt it, has he not? And he has: the long pauses, the tiredness, the way she turned into the storm. It was her last strength, and she gave it away to keep them dry.
5. Mo walks back down along the Ridge with his heel pressed to the rock at every step, feeling all of it: the huge, slow, loving heart under him. Tor is not lost. She is going home, and she is bringing them with her, and nobody knows it but him and one old man in a cap.

## 12. What Gran Knew
Synopsis: Mo confronts his grandmother, who admits she found the torn page years ago and hid it.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 18. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. I have been a coward in ink for forty years.*
1. Mo finds Gran at her desk in the rope tower writing nothing at all. He asks her, straight out, about the sea and the torn page. Her pen stops. She says, "Who told you?" and looks, for the first time in his life, very old.
2. Gran unscrews the top of her walking staff and takes out a rolled and brittle page, the one missing from the oldest Walk-Book. It is in the hand of a Stepkeeper from a thousand years ago: a plain, calm account of the Gathering and the Lying Down, with a note at the bottom: "Do not fear it. It is a good ending." She found it forty years ago in the back of a drawer.
3. She says she kept quiet because she was afraid: of what the village would do, of what it meant for the Walk, of losing the only life she had ever known. A Stepkeeper who keeps a book of a thousand years of walking does not want to be the one to write the last page.
4. Mo sits beside her on the floor and says nothing for a long time, which for him is a good deal of work. Then he says: "You could have told me." She says she should have. She says she is sorry, in a small dry voice, and puts her hand on his head the way she did when he was three.
5. Gran gives him the page to keep and says the village should be told the whole truth, but Mo will have to be the one to speak, because no one would believe an old woman who hid it. Mo, who was ignored all week, discovers that being listened to is a much more frightening thing.

## 13. Nobody Believes a Barefoot Boy
Synopsis: Mo tells the village the truth, and nobody believes him.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 20. Weather: cloud. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. The village has been told. The village has views.*
1. Mo climbs onto the Mayor's cheese crate in front of the whole village and, with the old page held up in both hands, tells them what he has learned: Tor is old, she is walking to a place called the Gathering, she will rest there, and Highstep can stay. His voice wobbles. He does not do it well.
2. The reaction is a mix: some laugh; some cry; some say it is a children's tale; Mama Achebe says kindly that she has no room in her heart today for one more story. Mayor Marsh says she is grateful, and that they cannot plan a village around a page that could have been written by anyone.
3. Gran stands and says the page is real, and the village goes quiet, and then argues about whether the Stepkeeper is too old to be believed. Mo's own father says nothing, which hurts most of all.
4. The count of families planning to descend at the Last Ladder actually goes up. Junie does not come to the meeting. Mo finds her scrapbook on the Footpost stone with a note inside: "I'm not leaving you. I'm leaving the tilt."
5. Mo realizes that saying the truth is not enough: people must see it. There is only one way to prove it to everyone, and it is a climb: to the Crown, Tor's highest point, where on a clear night you can see all the way to the horizon. He packs a rope, a lantern and a heel of bread, and Admiral follows without being asked.

## 14. The Crown
Synopsis: Mo climbs to the highest point of the mountain at night and sees where Tor is going.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 21. Weather: clear, and very large. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. Mo is up the mountain. So is the goat.*
1. The climb: past the last pines, up bare grey stone that is warm from Tor's breath, over a saddle of wind-flattened grass. Mo climbs in his bare feet because he trusts them, and he can feel Tor's pulse coming up through the rock the higher he goes: slow and strong, like an enormous drum.
2. At the Crown there is a hollow of rock, a cairn of old stones and a view of the whole world. The night is cold, clear and immense; stars from edge to edge. Admiral lies down on Mo's feet to keep them warm.
3. And far to the east, low on the black horizon, Mo sees a light. Then another to the north. Then a scatter of lights, small and steady, coming slowly closer from every side, like lanterns on a long procession. Each one is a village on the back of a mountain, walking toward a place he cannot see yet.
4. He counts: five, six, seven lights, plus the one he is standing on. Tor's hum thickens under his heels, a warm huge rising note, and there is an answer from the dark, seven notes in a chord that he feels in his ribs and teeth. It is the sound of a family calling to one another across a long distance.
5. Mo cries a little and does not mind. Then he grins, because he knows how to prove it. If Highstep cannot believe a barefoot boy, they can believe their own eyes. He wakes Admiral, takes the lantern and starts down in the dark, and Tor, very gently, steadies each step beneath his feet.

## 15. The Last Ladder
Synopsis: Tor squeezes through Stony Pass, the Last Ladders drop, and half the village makes the hardest choice.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 23. Weather: fair. Distance: two miles, through a gap. Direction: left. The ladders are down. I am not writing the rest yet.*
1. Mo reaches the village at dawn with his news, but the pass is upon them: two great cliffs and a narrow gap. Tor slows, breathes in and squeezes through with a long groan of stone on stone, the whole village clinging to ropes. Every child holds a goat.
2. The Last Ladders drop. Long rope ladders unroll down Tor's side to the plain, where the smoke of Lowbrook rises. Mayor Marsh gives the order calmly, but her hands are shaking: families with bundles queue at the ropes. Mo runs along the line shouting, showing the lights, the page; a few look up at the horizon and see nothing, because it is daylight.
3. The Achebes are near the front of the line. Junie stands with her scrapbook and her yellow scarf. Mo says he is sorry for what he said, that he heard her, that he will hear anything now if she will stay. She says she believes him; she is going anyway. A floor that does not tilt is what she needs. It is not the same thing as not loving him.
4. She hugs him, hard, with a mallet handle sticking into his ribs, and goes down the ladder with her family. Sixty-three villagers descend. Mo watches until they are toy figures on the green plain, and then until they are nothing.
5. The ladders are pulled up. Tor walks on, and Mo feels her take one careful step after another, as if she knows exactly what has just been lost. Gran writes in the Walk-Book: Day 23, sixty-three left. She blots the page with her sleeve, and this time it is not ink.

# PART FOUR: THE GATHERING

## 16. The Half-Empty Mountain
Synopsis: Highstep carries on with fewer people, and Mo learns what he hears is not only the mountain.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 24. Weather: fair. Distance: three miles. Direction: left, downhill. The Green looks very big.*
1. The morning after: fewer bells, fewer voices, empty doorways, the Achebes' loom lying quiet in the sun. The Mayor's clipboard has a lot fewer names. Children play a game in the space where Junie's house stood, and Mo cannot watch.
2. Mo does his chores badly. He hears Tor plainly now, but he starts listening to the village too: to Mum humming while she stirs, to Dad's rope whistle, to the Mayor talking to herself in the dark about whether she has made a terrible mistake. He begins to pay attention to the words that people do not say.
3. The Mayor, on the cheese crate late at night, says quietly to Mo that she has been listening to a child because she has run out of adults. She asks him what Tor needs. He answers plainly: she needs everyone to trust her. She needs the village to still be a village when they arrive.
4. Tor comes down from the wild country onto the great plain, and from her flank Mo can see Lowbrook's lights, a small gold cluster by a river, with Junie's family somewhere among them. He and Gran go to the rope tower and light a lantern: two long flashes, one short: the old signal, I'm here. Miles away, after an agonizing wait, a light answers: two long, one short.
5. They keep it up every night. Junie, across a few miles of dark plain, is still his best friend. Mo starts to understand that a person can be with you and far away at the same time.

## 17. The Flood
Synopsis: Rain in the hills sends the Slow River over its banks and Lowbrook is in danger; Tor does something only she can do.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 27. Weather: rain, for three days. Distance: one mile, and I will not say how. Direction: toward the lanterns.*
1. Three days of steady rain in the grey hills. Tor slogs across the plain with her feet churning brown mud, and the Slow River at the edge of the plain begins to rise. The village fills buckets and gets on with it; nobody thinks of the ground people until, at dusk, Mo sees a lantern signal from Lowbrook: two long, three short, fast and frantic. It is not "I'm here," it is "help."
2. Lowbrook lies in the bend of the river, and the river is coming over its banks: a wall of brown water, fields gone under, farm carts floating. Through Mo's feet he can feel the plain shudder as the water rises. Mrs. Cobb and the Lowbrook folk are climbing on their roofs, with Junie's family among them.
3. Mo runs to the Footpost, and without asking permission, before the Mayor can even say a word, he puts both heels to the stone and asks Tor for what has never been asked of a mountain: to kneel, to turn toward Lowbrook and lower her side to the flood so people can climb aboard. He feels her hesitate. Then he thinks of Junie's scarf, and asks her not as a Stepkeeper's apprentice but as a friend. Two long steps.
4. Tor wades into the flood, deeper than she has ever gone, and kneels with a long deep groan until the water laps at her side and the rope ladders fall into the flood. Highstep throws lines, lanterns, blankets. The Mayor shouts orders and for once everyone follows them. Lowbrook climbs: families, chickens, a cow, Mrs. Cobb's wheelbarrow.
5. Junie arrives near the end, soaked, with Obi on her back and her scrapbook in a sack, and Mo hauls her up over the rail. She says, "You came." Mo says, "She came." Behind them Tor rises, streaming, a mountain carrying two villages instead of one.

## 18. Everyone on Board
Synopsis: Two villages share one mountain for the last days of the Walk, and Mo and Junie make peace.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 28. Weather: fair, and steaming. Distance: three miles. Direction: left. Population: three hundred and ninety-one. Goats: forty-one, including one from Lowbrook.*
1. The morning after the flood: drying clothes on every rope, farm animals wandering through the pines, Lowbrook folk staring up at a mountain with their mouths open. Mrs. Cobb calls Tor "that big lump" and pats her, in front of everybody. Tor gives a little shiver that is unmistakably a laugh.
2. Mayor Marsh and Mrs. Cobb, both very large voices in small bodies, have a meeting on the cheese crate about who is in charge of rationing. It ends in a truce and a shared pot of soup. The people of Highstep and the people of Lowbrook discover that they need the same things: salt, warmth and a good story.
3. Mo finds Junie on the Footpost, drawing. She says she did not know a mountain could be brave. Mo says he did not know he could be so bad at listening. They both start to apologize at once, then both stop, then laugh. Junie shows him a new page in her scrapbook: a still town, but with a very large mountain in the background.
4. Mo tells her everything, slowly and in order this time, with pauses for questions. He hands her the torn page. Junie reads it twice, aloud, and says that a house that stays still might not be the same as a home that stays still, and she is not sure yet which she wants. She says she is glad he told it properly.
5. On the Green that night, with three hundred and ninety-one people crowded around a great communal fire, Gran reads out the old page in her clear dry voice, and this time nobody laughs. It is Mrs. Cobb who says, in the long quiet afterward, "Well. I've lived in one place all my life. I would like to see the sea."

## 19. The Gathering
Synopsis: Tor reaches the sea at dawn, where six other walking mountains arrive at once, each carrying a village.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 33. Weather: gold. Distance: three miles. Direction: forward. At last I can write it.*
1. The last approach: a long grey day of sand and marsh grass, gulls, a smell of salt. Tor's steps grow slower and gentler; the village lines the rope bridges in silence, lantern in hand. That night nobody sleeps. At the first paling of the sky Mo stands on the Footpost, heel to stone, and feels the whole of the ground shiver with a huge chord.
2. Dawn on the sea, wide and silver-gold: six other mountains coming in from north and south and east, out of the mist, each huge and shaggy and different: one snow-capped, one covered in red autumn trees, one with a lake on her back and fishing boats moored to her horns. Each carries a village, and the bells begin to ring one after another, mountain to mountain, over the water.
3. There is a festival on the shore like nothing anyone has ever seen: seven villages from seven mountains, tents and banners and long tables on the sand, pipes, drums, and dancing. Children who have never met run together. Highstep's cheese is a great success. Mrs. Cobb tries oysters and declares them "not for her."
4. Gran finds the Stepkeeper of the oldest mountain, a very small very old man with a beard to his knees, and they sit on a rock and read each other's Walk-Books for hours. They laugh, they cry, they trade a thousand years. Mo hears a fragment: the last entry in every book, for every mountain, is the same: "Weather: fair. Distance: none. Direction: home."
5. At evening the mountains sing, seven voices in a low chord that rolls across the water and lifts every hair on every head. Mo, on the Footpost, feels Tor's answer through his heels: enormous, calm, a farewell and a welcome all at once. He knows what comes next.

## 20. Distance: None
Synopsis: Tor lies down to rest and becomes an island, and Highstep stays, and stays Highstep.
Header: *From the Walk-Book of Highstep, Day 34. Weather: fair. Distance: none. Direction: home.*
1. The next dawn Tor takes her last steps, three long slow ones out into the shallows, and stops. Mo tells the village to sit down wherever they are, because that is what she asked for. She lowers herself with a long, long breath, rolling gently into the warm water, and the whole mountain settles like a great animal going to sleep.
2. It takes the whole day for the mountain to be still. The hum stays. Mo, sitting with his heel to the Footpost, feels her pulse slow, slow, slow, to a rhythm so wide he can only catch one beat in a hundred, and then he feels it settle into something he thinks he will feel for the rest of his life: a warm quiet under everything. She is not gone. She is resting.
3. Mo says goodbye the only way he knows: two long steps, one for each foot, on the flat stone. Two long steps come back, very small, from very deep. He is crying and laughing at once. Admiral, who has been sitting quietly beside him, puts his head under Mo's hand.
4. The village wakes to a new morning: a shore, a wharf, boats, gulls. Highstep is on solid ground for the first time in a thousand years. Papa Achebe and Mrs. Cobb build a bridge to the mainland; Junie's family return; Lowbrook's farm people plant a field; Mama Achebe sets up a loom facing the sea. There is a bakery, and a school with no ropes. Junie's floor does not tilt.
5. On the last page of the Walk-Book, Gran writes the entry for Day 35. She hands the pen to Mo, and he writes the first line of a new book: "This is the first day of Highstep by the sea." Under his bare feet the stone hums, very faintly, in his teeth. Home, Mo thinks, is not the mountain. It is everyone standing on it.
