# THE MAPMAKER'S APPRENTICE — story bible

Historical-style adventure quest for ages 10-12, set in an invented old-world kingdom (roughly the 1400s: no guns, no machines, horses, ferries, quills and lanterns). Warm, funny, wondrous and truthful, with real weather, real effort and real feelings. No gore, no romance, nothing spooky, no magic (the wonder is in the land itself). Peril is gentle: mud, floods, falls, getting lost.

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, in the manner of a warm, wry old storyteller who loves the world she describes. Rich, concrete, slightly old-fashioned sensory detail (the smell of gall-ink, the creak of a ferry rope, the taste of cold porridge), long rolling sentences balanced with short dry ones, and now and then a small aside to the reader ("Now, a compass is a peculiar thing."). The humor is gentle and observational. Never modern slang. Every chapter opens with an italic epigraph in this exact form on its own line, then a blank line: "*The Fourth Rule of Mapmaking: Draw what is there, not what you wish were there.*" The rules are Master Finch's sayings, numbered in order (First, Second, Third... up to the Twentieth), one per chapter, each a real piece of craft wisdom that also fits the chapter's events.
Voice sample:
"*The First Rule of Mapmaking: A map is a promise.*

Nobody in the city of Verrin was awake yet, except the gulls, the baker, and Tam Marrow, who was grinding ink and hating it.

There is a way of grinding oak-gall ink that Master Finch called 'listening,' and a way that Tam called 'trying not to throw the pestle at the wall.' It involved a stone bowl, a lump of something that looked like a burnt raisin, and a great deal of patience, and Tam had exactly the amount of patience an eleven-year-old has at half past five in the morning, which is to say, none."

## The premise
The kingdom is Aldermere: river valleys, salt marshes, high moors and sea cliffs. Its capital is Verrin, a crowded river-city of bridges, ferries and steep lanes, ruled by an old, distant king. The Mapmakers' Guild is powerful and proud. Once every ten years the Guild holds the Grand Survey: apprentices are sent to chart the Uncharted Marches (a wild region of thornwood, marsh, moor and cliff that no honest map has ever covered). The Guild will judge every map. The winner earns a place at the Royal Chartery, the finest map-house in the kingdom. Deadline: 21 days.
Tam Marrow, 11, an orphan from the Lowtide Docks who only learned to read at nine, is the ink-grinder for old Master Odalys Finch, whose shop is at the end of Ink Lane. Everyone else in the Survey is a rich, well-taught Academy apprentice. Tam draws crooked lines, blots everything, and secretly believes she began too far behind ever to catch up. Master Finch quietly enters her in the Survey. Her only rule, which she makes Tam follow every night, is the Yesterday Book: each evening Tam draws the same simple test line (a river, a coast, or a path) and compares it ONLY with the line she drew yesterday, never with anyone else's. "Be better than yesterday. That is the whole of the trade."

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Tam (Tamsin) Marrow**, 11. Short, sturdy, sunburnt, freckled, a thick black braid tied with a bit of blue string, ink-stained fingers, a patched brown coat two sizes too big, a leather satchel. Blunt, curious, quick to laugh, quick to sulk. Secret belief: she started too far behind to matter. Grew up on the Lowtide Docks, sorting fish and running errands; knows how ropes, knots, tides and boats work (a hidden skill that saves the day).
- **Master Odalys Finch**, 70s. Tall, thin, white hair in a bun stuck with pencils, round spectacles, a lavender scarf, a hawthorn cane; her hands tremble with age. Gravelly, kind, dry-witted, sharp. Has mapped half the kingdom. Her first map, sixty years ago, was a disaster (she'll tell Tam so). Owner of the shop and of the mule Dumpling.
- **Lucan Ravel**, 12. Neat fair hair, a green coat with silver buttons, a gold compass on a chain, always clean. The best Academy apprentice, gifted and trained since age six, proud, used to being first, afraid of being ordinary. Not evil; his father, Lord Ravel, is the Guild treasurer and expects victory. He comes to respect Tam.
- **Odd Brannock**, 13. Ferryman's son. Tall, gangly, red hair that sticks up, huge gap-toothed grin, laughs at everything, knows every river and current, terrified of heights. Loyal.
- **Dumpling**, Finch's old grey mule: stubborn, greedy for apples, wise about mud. **Sixpence**, a grey homing pigeon with a white patch on her chest, who carries notes between Tam and Finch.
- **Guildmaster Aurelio Quint**, 60s, tall, stately, deep voice, a chain of office with a silver compass. Fair; secretly agrees with Finch that improvement matters most.
- Lucan's two assistants: Bram (big and gentle) and Ines (small and quick).
- Places: Verrin, Ink Lane, the Lowtide Docks, the Guild Hall of Maps; in the Marches: the Thornwood, the River Aln crossing, the Greywater marsh, the abandoned village of Saltmere, the Tallow Pass, the Hollow Cliffs and its old lighthouse.

## The 21-day plan of the story
Ch1-4: the shop, the announcement, Tam's worst day, packing and departure. Ch5-9: first week: the Thornwood, the river, Finch's fall (Tam continues with Odd and Sixpence), Lucan on the ridge, the marsh. Ch10-14: low ground: Saltmere and an old map that lies, rain ruins a week's work, Tam starts over, Lucan's team sinks in the marsh (Tam and Odd rescue them, losing a day), the dark night and Finch's letter. Ch15-20: the cliffs, the race home over a broken bridge with Lucan's team helping, the Guild Hall, the judging, the Finch Prize for improvement, and a quiet ending in the shop.

## Motifs
The Yesterday Book (a small stitched notebook: each page has a date and one test line; Tam writes tiny notes: "Straighter by a whisker." "Blot at the corner. Less blot."); Finch's numbered Rules of Mapmaking (the epigraphs); the blue string in Tam's braid (a fisherman's gift); Dumpling's greed for apples; Sixpence's white patch; Odd's laughter and his fear of heights; Lucan's gold compass (he lends it to Tam in the last act; Tam gives him her knotted rope).

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Be a little better than you were yesterday. Growth speed matters more than where you began, and comparing yourself to others steals the only measurement that matters. Tam's fear that she began too far behind is answered by the pages of the Yesterday Book (visible proof), by starting over after her ruined map (the second draft is far better), and by the Guildmaster's new prize. Never let the narrator state the moral except, at most, one plain line near the end. Lucan should not be humiliated; he grows too.

## Things to avoid
No magic. No villains. No guns. No romance. Keep the peril gentle. Do not skip the Rules epigraph at the start of each chapter. Do not use modern slang or "okay".
