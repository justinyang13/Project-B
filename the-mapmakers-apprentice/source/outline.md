# PART ONE: THE SHOP AT THE END OF INK LANE

## 1. The Burnt Raisin
Synopsis: At dawn in Master Finch's shop, Tam grinds ink and hates it, meets Finch and the Yesterday Book, and we learn where she came from and what she secretly believes.
Header: *The First Rule of Mapmaking: A map is a promise.*
1. The stone bowl, the lump like a burnt raisin, her impatience at half past five in the morning.
2. Master Odalys Finch descends the crooked stairs in her lavender scarf with pencils stuck in her bun, cane tapping, and inspects Tam's ink with one trembling finger. Their dry, affectionate banter. Finch says the ink is "better than yesterday's, which was better than the day before's, which is how one gets to a good ink." The shop described: rolled maps like sleeping cats, the great slanted drawing table, a brass globe with a dent.
3. Tam's back story in brief, told with wry warmth: raised on the Lowtide Docks, sorted fish, ran errands, could tie forty knots by age seven, learned her letters at nine from a one-eyed net mender, then mended a cracked compass for a customer and caught Finch's attention. Finch hired her for her hands and "her refusal to say 'it can't be done.'"
4. The Yesterday Book: a small stitched notebook. Every evening Tam draws the same simple test line, a wavering river of ink with two bends, and writes one honest sentence. Finch flips the pages and shows a long row of rivers, each a whisker straighter. Tam sees only how crooked they still are. Finch says nothing about it. Tam mutters that the Academy children could draw this in their sleep.
5. A rattle at the door: the town crier's bell in the street outside, and his voice booming over the roofs. Tam looks up from the burnt raisin. Hook: "By order of the Mapmakers' Guild," bellows the crier, "the Grand Survey of the Uncharted Marches is proclaimed!" Finch's pencil stops mid-air, and for the first time Tam sees her smile like a woman who has been waiting a very long time.

## 2. The Grand Survey
Synopsis: The crier announces the Survey. Lucan Ravel swaggers into the shop, and Master Finch quietly enters Tam.
Header: *The Second Rule of Mapmaking: Start where you stand.*
1. Tam and Finch push out into Ink Lane among the crowd reading the proclamation nailed to a post: the Uncharted Marches, twenty-one days, every apprentice in the kingdom may enter, and the winning map earns a place at the Royal Chartery. Tam reads it slowly with her finger, moving her lips, and is aware of a girl behind her noticing.
2. Explain the Marches with relish: a wild land of thornwood, marsh, moor and cliff where no honest map had ever been drawn, and the last blank in the kingdom. The Academy apprentices, in their crisp coats, are already planning. Tam says loudly that it is not for her, that she is not even a proper apprentice. Finch says nothing.
3. Back in the shop, the door bangs and in walks Lucan Ravel, twelve, fair hair neat, green coat with silver buttons, gold compass swinging on a chain, followed by two assistants, Bram and Ines. He is polite in the way that stings. He glances at Tam's ink-stained hands and says he supposes "the grinder" will be enjoying the holiday, since "of course there is no question of entering."
4. Finch peers at him over her spectacles, dips a quill, and writes something on a sheet of thick cream paper. She blows on it and hands it to Lucan to carry to the Guild clerks: the entry form for the Grand Survey. Under "Apprentice" she has written: TAMSIN MARROW. Lucan reads it, his face fighting itself, and puts it in his coat as if it were a hot potato.
5. Tam is furious and terrified. "You can't. I'm not... they'll laugh at me." Finch, gently: "Rule the Second. Begin where you stand. You are standing in my shop. You will do." Hook: Tam looks at her own ink-stained hands and thinks: I started from the docks. Nobody starts further behind than that. And she is very afraid that she is right.

## 3. The Worst Line in Verrin
Synopsis: Tam's practice day goes badly. Finch tells her the first great secret of the trade and shows her own first, terrible map.
Header: *The Third Rule of Mapmaking: Compare your line only with yesterday's.*
1. Practice day. Tam tries to draw the coast of a nearby island from Finch's model and everything goes wrong: the ink splashes, the pen scratches, a blot the size of a plum appears in the middle of the sea. She tears up three sheets and kicks a stool.
2. Lucan's sample map, delivered by a servant as a courtesy, arrives on the table: a beautiful, perfect coast drawn in three colors with a tiny sea serpent in the corner. Tam looks at it, then at her own blot, and something inside her goes quiet and flat. "I'll never draw like that," she says, in a whisper that scares Finch more than any shouting would have.
3. Finch does not lecture. She goes up the crooked stairs and returns with a curled, yellowed, mouse-nibbled map. It is dreadful: the coast looks like a spilled plate of noodles, the compass rose is crooked, a tree is labeled "Big Tree (?)". It is Finch's own first map, drawn at eleven. Tam laughs so hard she has to sit down. "Sixty years ago," Finch says, delighted. "Look how far a person can travel."
4. Finch explains the secret: never compare your line with someone else's, because you will always find someone better or someone worse and neither one helps you draw. Compare it with yesterday's. Is it a whisker better? Then you are winning. She shows Tam the Yesterday Book, page by page, and for the first time Tam sees the rivers as Finch does: a slow, stubborn staircase going upward.
5. That evening Tam draws her river again, the two-bend river. She holds it next to yesterday's. It is a hair straighter, with less blot. She writes: "Straighter by a whisker. Blot at the corner, but smaller." She looks at it for a long time. Hook: "It was the first time she had ever been proud of a line that was still crooked."

## 4. Dumpling and the Chain
Synopsis: Packing for the Marches: the mule Dumpling, the pigeon Sixpence, the surveying kit, Odd the ferry boy, and the rival team's grand departure.
Header: *The Fourth Rule of Mapmaking: Pack less than you fear you'll need, and more than you hope you will.*
1. Finch's old surveying kit is hauled out of a trunk: a folding table, a measuring chain, a plumb-bob, a brass compass, quills, jars of ink, good paper and a spyglass. Tam tests the chain and it clinks like a small orchestra. Dumpling, Finch's ancient grey mule, is loaded and expresses views about it.
2. Sixpence, the grey homing pigeon with a white patch, is put into a wicker basket with strict instructions to carry any note back to Ink Lane. Finch will travel with them for the first stage, on Dumpling's cart. She pats her pockets for apples. "One does not go into the wild without apples," she says. "For the mule. And for morale."
3. At the ferry landing they are met by Odd Brannock, the ferryman's son: tall, gangly, red hair sticking up, with a huge gap-toothed grin. He has been hired as their guide because no one alive knows the rivers of the Marches better. He shakes Tam's hand with both of his and laughs at nothing in particular. She likes him at once.
4. The road out of Verrin passes the Guild square, where the other apprentices are departing in style: banners, trumpets, matched horses, servants with trunks of instruments. Lucan's carriage is lacquered green, his assistants Bram and Ines riding behind. Lucan looks down from the window at Tam's small cart, mule and pigeon basket, and touches his gold compass to his forehead in a mock salute.
5. Tam's stomach lurches. She takes out her Yesterday Book and looks at the crooked little rivers. Finch, jolting beside her, says nothing but taps the notebook once. Hook: the city walls slide past, the last chimney smoke falls behind them, and ahead lies a blank green-and-grey land with no roads on it at all. "Well," says Tam, "there's the promise. Let's see if we can keep it."

# PART TWO: THE MARCHES

## 5. The Thornwood
Synopsis: Their first days in the Thornwood. Tam draws what she wishes were there, and Odd and the land correct her.
Header: *The Fifth Rule of Mapmaking: Draw what is there, not what you wish were there.*
1. The Thornwood: a green wall of brambles, twisted old trees, damp shadows, and a path that keeps deciding not to be a path. They leave the cart at a farm and continue on foot with Dumpling carrying the kit. Tam's first survey day: setting up the folding table, counting the chain. They leave the cart at a farm and continue on foot with Dumpling carrying the kit. Tam's first survey day: setting up the folding table, counting the chain, measuring the first bend.
2. Tam draws the path as she wishes it were: a neat curving track. When Odd looks over her shoulder, he says kindly that there isn't one there, that the path she has drawn is a place where a bramble hedge is. Tam is annoyed, then reddens, because he is right. She had drawn what was easy to draw.
3. Finch, sitting on a stump with her cane across her knees, gently says the line has "a lovely sweep and no truth in it." Tam crumples it. Then she walks back through the brambles with the chain, taking the scratches, and measures the real path. The real path is a mess of turns and dead ends. She draws it. It looks awful and correct.
4. Comic episode: Dumpling refuses to enter a particular thicket, sits down, and will not be moved by pulling, pleading or bribery until Odd notices the thicket is a hornet's nest. The mule was right. Finch says, "Mules are the finest surveyors of all. They will not lie to save face."
5. That night by the fire, Tam draws her river in the Yesterday Book: the two-bend river, bent well. She writes: "Drew what was there. Ugly. True." Hook: from the dark woods comes the sound of a distant trumpet, thin and proud. Somewhere in the Thornwood, Lucan's team is making camp.

## 6. The River Aln
Synopsis: Odd's river. Tam learns to measure with what she has, uses her dock knots, and gets a real win.
Header: *The Sixth Rule of Mapmaking: Measure with what you have.*
1. The River Aln, wide, brown and quick. Odd's face lights up: this is his country. Then Tam unpacks the brass chain and finds two links bent out of shape by Dumpling's unfortunate roll in a ditch. It can't measure a river. Odd's face lights up: this is his country. He tells the currents like a story and points out where the ferry used to cross. Then Tam unpacks the brass chain and finds two links have been bent out of shape by Dumpling's unfortunate roll in a ditch. It can't measure a river.
2. Panic. Finch, calmly: "What have you got?" Tam looks around. She has a length of good hemp rope, her patient hands, and forty knots she learned on the Lowtide Docks. She ties knots every arm's-length, tests them against the last good bit of chain, and makes a knotted measuring rope. It is a docker's trick and it works. Odd whoops. Finch's eyes crinkle.
3. They cross the shallows in a line, Odd leading, Tam calling the knots as they pass a shoal. Dumpling, encouraged by an apple held on a stick, crosses with the dignity of an admiral. Midway Odd tells his own worry: his father wants him on the ferry all his life. He doesn't know if he wants more. Tam says everybody starts somewhere, and it does not decide where you end.
4. On the far bank they set up and Tam measures the Aln's width with her rope and draws it with real care. It is the best line she has ever drawn. It's not beautiful, but it is exact. She compares it to her Yesterday Book's river: far, far better than the first page. She laughs out loud.
5. Finch wraps her scarf tighter and says that a person is at her best when she uses the tools she was born with. Tam asks, "You knew the chain would bend?" Finch: "I knew you'd know what to do." Hook: they hear a shout across the river, thin and far: someone's cry for help, and the clatter of a fallen thing.

## 7. Master Finch Falls
Synopsis: Finch slips on wet rock and sprains her ankle badly. She insists Tam go on without her.
Header: *The Seventh Rule of Mapmaking: A good map has more than one hand in it.*
1. The shout was only a heron and a rock fall, but as Finch hurries to look, her cane skids on wet stone and she goes down with a cry. Odd carries her to a shepherd's hut on the ridge. Odd carries her to a shepherd's hut on the ridge. Her ankle is swollen the size of a loaf. She is very white and very calm.
2. The shepherd's hut: a low stone room, a hearth, a bed of heather, a sleepy dog. Finch announces she cannot go on, that they must send Sixpence for help, and that the survey will go on. "Tam goes. Odd guides. I stay here, stubborn and comfortable, until the shepherd's wife comes home with her soup."
3. Tam refuses. She says the map belongs to Finch, that she is only the ink-grinder, that they should all turn back. Finch, hobbling upright with the cane, says the map is Tam's, that it always was; that she entered Tam in the Survey because she believed in her. "I did not enter you for my sake. I entered you for yours."
4. Finch gives Tam her spectacles to keep (Tam is horrified), her sextant, and her most precious tool: her own first map, sixty years old, folded in oilcloth. "Carry it. When you feel you started too far behind, look at what I drew at eleven, and look at what I draw now." Tam's eyes prickle. She nods.
5. Sixpence is released with a note tied to her leg, telling the Guild that Finch is safe and the survey continues. The pigeon spirals up and away. Tam, Odd and Dumpling set out along the ridge, leaving a small figure in the hut doorway waving her cane. Hook: "It was the first time in Tam's life that anyone had trusted her with the whole thing."

## 8. Lucan on the Ridge
Synopsis: Tam and Odd meet Lucan's grand team on a ridge. Tam feels small, then remembers the rule.
Header: *The Eighth Rule of Mapmaking: Fast is a fine speed, but true is a finer one.*
1. A misty morning on the moor ridge. Tam and Odd, with Dumpling, cross a stone wall and see a splendid camp: three striped tents, a folding brass table, a telescope on a tripod, servants stirring a cauldron. Lucan, in a spotless green coat, is dictating to Ines. Tam and Odd, with Dumpling, cross a stone wall and see, a hundred yards away, a splendid camp: three striped tents, a folding brass table, a telescope on a tripod, servants stirring a cauldron. Lucan, in a spotless green coat, is dictating to Ines.
2. Lucan greets them with cool courtesy. He is far ahead: he has already mapped the Thornwood and half the Aln with a fast triangulation method taught at the Academy. His map, unrolled, is exquisite: a hundred tiny trees, perfect coast, delicate hills. Tam's map, unrolled beside it, looks like a fence that lost a fight.
3. Tam feels the old flat cold feeling. Lucan says, not unkindly, that it is "very brave of you to have entered." Bram gives Tam a slab of bread, kind and wordless. Ines glances at Tam's knotted measuring rope with unexpected interest. Tam takes a breath, and instead of comparing, takes out the Yesterday Book and looks at last night's page, the two-bend river, and finds it better.
4. She says, in a steady voice: "It's not for me to be as fast as you. It's for me to be better than I was on the Thornwood." Lucan blinks. Something in his face flickers, not scorn but a small doubt: he doesn't have a book like that. Odd grins at Tam like a sunrise. Lucan turns away sharply and says his team must press on to the marsh.
5. As they pack, Ines slips Tam a small paper: a copy of her quick way of estimating slope. Tam hides it in her satchel and thanks her with a look. Hook: Lucan's team sets off at a brisk trot toward the silver-grey flats on the horizon. Odd, watching, whispers: "That's the Greywater. Nobody in my family will go in there. The map says meadow. My father says otherwise."

## 9. The Greywater
Synopsis: Tam and Odd enter the marsh and get lost. Tam learns to stop, look, and draw where she is.
Header: *The Ninth Rule of Mapmaking: When you are lost, stop, look, and draw where you are.*
1. The Greywater at dusk: silver reeds, black water, a smell like wet pennies, herons standing like question marks. Odd says they must skirt the north edge, but a shining channel tempts them south, and by nightfall they have no idea where the north edge is. Odd says they must skirt the north edge, but a shining channel tempts them south, and by nightfall they have no idea where the north edge is.
2. Panic rises. Odd is white, Dumpling snorts nervously, and Tam's throat tightens; she feels the pull to run, to shout, to give up. Then, in her head, Finch's voice: stop, look, and draw where you are. She sits on a tussock, breathes, and draws exactly what she sees: three willows, a black pool, a reed island with a dead tree.
3. Drawing calms her. The drawing tells her things: the willows lean the same direction (the wind's), the black pool is fed from the left, the reeds are thinner toward the right, where the ground must be firmer. She talks Odd through it. She isn't sure. She is only a little surer than yesterday. They edge toward the thin reeds.
4. Night on a reed island the size of a barn. No fire. Dumpling eats reeds with dignity. They tell stories. Odd tells hers a tale of his grandmother crossing the Greywater in a storm with an egg basket. Tam, warmed by a wet blanket and the pigeon's absent coo, writes in her Yesterday Book: "Was lost. Stopped. Drew. Was less lost."
5. Hook: through the dark comes the faint sound of splashing and distant shouting, far across the marsh, thin and frantic. A light bobs, flickers and goes out. Odd sits up. "Someone's in the water."

# PART THREE: LOW GROUND

## 10. The Village That Wasn't There
Synopsis: The next day they find the ruined village of Saltmere, which old maps show as a thriving town. Tam learns to distrust maps she has not walked.
Header: *The Tenth Rule of Mapmaking: Never trust a map you did not walk.*
1. Dawn. Odd and Tam follow the splashing sounds, but find only a floating boot and a broken pole. They decide it was a heron or a dream, and press on to dry ground, where they come upon a village: Saltmere, a ring of roofless stone houses, a fallen church bell overgrown with ivy.
2. Finch's old map (which Tam carries for luck) shows Saltmere as a busy town with a road leading straight to the coast. Tam and Odd walk the ground and find no road at all, only a peat bog. The map lied, or was made for a world that no longer existed. Tam sits on a broken wall, stunned: maps can be wrong and people can drown of it.
3. She thinks of what Finch said about promises. She pulls out her pen and draws Saltmere as it is: a ruin, a sunk road, a marsh creeping in. It is the most honest line she has ever drawn. She writes under it: "Old road drowned. Do not walk here." A small thing that might save a stranger's life.
4. Odd finds something in the ruined church: a wall scratched with names and dates, pilgrims' marks from a hundred years back. Someone had written, with a nail, "GOD KEEP THE GREYWATER OFF THE ROAD." He and Tam add their own names, then a tiny drawing of a compass. Tam feels connected to all the people who ever needed a true map.
5. Hook: from the ridge above the ruins, Sixpence flutters down out of the pale sky, landing on Tam's shoulder with a note tied to her leg. Finch's shaky handwriting: "Ankle mending. Shepherd's wife makes better soup than I do. How is the line today?" Tam grins. Then thunder rolls, low and slow, from the western sky.

## 11. Rain
Synopsis: Three days of rain. Dumpling slips in a stream and Tam's satchel is soaked, ruining a week of work.
Header: *The Eleventh Rule of Mapmaking: Ink runs. Memory doesn't, unless you let it.*
1. The rain arrives like a curtain: three days of grey, gusting, relentless water that turns the moor into a sponge. Tam and Odd tie oilcloth over the kit. Dumpling wears a sack like a cape and a look of deep betrayal.
2. On the third day, crossing a swollen stream by a slippery ford, Dumpling's hoof skids. The mule sits down in the current with a shocked grunt. The satchel, slung on her side, slips underwater. Tam plunges in after it, grabs it, hauls it out. She has it. The oilcloth has torn. Water pours out.
3. They shelter in a hollow under an overhang. Tam unfolds the map with hands that are shaking. Seven days of work are a smear of grey, blue and black. The coast is a river. The Thornwood is a bruise. She stares at it for so long Odd stops talking. Then she says, very quietly, "It's gone."
4. She takes it hard. The old flat cold feeling crawls back. Lucan is somewhere ahead with a dry map, a dry team and a fast method. She began too far behind and now she has fallen even further. She thinks of the Yesterday Book, but even that is damp and the ink has run on last night's river. She puts her head on her knees.
5. Odd, who never stops laughing, doesn't laugh. He sits beside her and says: "My grandfather lost his whole net in a storm once. He sat and looked at the water for one hour. Then he took out his knife and started a new one." He hands her his last bread crust. Hook: "You don't have to start today, Tam. But you do have to start again."

## 12. Starting Over
Synopsis: Tam redraws everything from memory and notes, and the second draft is far better. She has never been so aware of her own growth.
Header: *The Twelfth Rule of Mapmaking: The second try is not the first failing twice.*
1. Morning: the rain has thinned to mist. Tam stares at the ruined map, then at the blank sheet of good paper. She has one sheet left and six days. Tam stares at the ruined map, then at the blank sheet of good paper. She has one sheet left and six days. She takes a breath. She smooths the sheet. She dips her pen. She begins.
2. She draws from memory: the Thornwood path, the Aln crossing, the shoals, the marsh, the drowned road at Saltmere. To her surprise, she remembers it all. She had walked every step and looked hard at every bend. The map is in her, more than on the paper. Each line comes easier, quicker, straighter than it did the first time.
3. Odd watches, huge-eyed. "It's better," he says. "It's a lot better." Tam holds the ruined map beside the new one. The ruined map, the one she'd been so proud of, looks childish beside it. The new coast has proper curves. The new hills lean the right way. The measure of every bend is truer. She laughs in disbelief.
4. She takes the damp Yesterday Book and, with the blurred old river on one page and a fresh one on the next, sees it whole: the first river (page one), the river of a week ago, and today's. It is a staircase going up. She has come a long way. Even the ruin has taught her. She writes: "Lost a week. Gained a week. Better."
5. Hook: Sixpence circles overhead with a note, and Tam gives her a reply: a tiny drawing of the new map's coast. Then, as they pack, Odd spots something moving on the far edge of the marsh below: a line of small figures, a green flag, and someone waving frantically.

## 13. Lucan's Mistake
Synopsis: Lucan's team is stuck in the Greywater because his map shows a meadow where there is a marsh. Tam and Odd rescue them, which costs a day.
Header: *The Thirteenth Rule of Mapmaking: A map that drowns a traveler is no map at all.*
1. Down at the marsh edge they find Lucan's team in trouble: their cart and horses sunk to the axles, Bram waist-deep in black mud, Ines stranded on a tussock, and Lucan, coat ruined, on a single reed hummock, clutching his beautiful, perfect map. On it, a lovely green meadow. On it, a lovely green meadow. The land is water.
2. Lucan sees Tam and his face goes through fury, shame and relief in three seconds. He does not ask for help. Tam looks at the ground, at the marsh, at her own map, which shows the true path. She could leave him. He'd have left her. The reeds sway. Odd looks at her. She says, "We're going in."
3. The rescue is gritty and comic and tense: Odd's knowledge of ropes and currents, Tam's knotted rope thrown across the mud, Dumpling hauling on a harness with grim dignity, Ines catching the line, Bram pulled out with a squelch like a giant boot. The horses are freed last with a lot of wet cursing.
4. Lucan, covered in mud, sits on a rock and says nothing for a long time. He looks at his gorgeous map. Then at Tam's scruffy, true one. "It said meadow," he says. "It said meadow because I drew it from the ridge, at a distance. I never walked it." Tam, gently: "Rule the Tenth." Lucan: "I didn't know there was a rule." "There's twenty."
5. The rescue has cost them a whole day. There are six days left, and they are three days' walk from Verrin. Lucan's team can't map anything now; they must go home. Hook: Lucan, without looking up, says quietly: "Your map's better than mine." And Tam realizes it's the first thing he's ever said to her that isn't polite.

## 14. The Darkest Night
Synopsis: Alone at the cliff edge, Tam despairs. Finch's letter comes by pigeon.
Header: *The Fourteenth Rule of Mapmaking: On the darkest night, draw the smallest line.*
1. Evening at a cliff-top camp. Lucan's team has gone off to find a road; Tam and Odd must still chart the Hollow Cliffs, two days away, then hurry to Verrin in the final four. It cannot be done. She has done the sums twice. Odd is quiet, poking the fire.
2. Tam takes out both maps: her true one and, in her mind, Lucan's perfect one. She hears the old whisper: you began too far behind, you will never catch up, you might as well stop. Even after all that, it is still there. She says to Odd that she isn't going to make it. Odd doesn't argue. He just sits.
3. Sixpence lands on the fire-ring stones, exhausted, with a note. It is a long letter in Finch's shaky hand, and Tam reads it by firelight. Finch writes that she cannot draw a straight line any more; her hands tremble; she is losing the trade to age. She writes that this is not a tragedy but a season. She tells the story of her first map, and what she learned: "I was not trying to be the best mapmaker in Aldermere. I was trying to be a better one than I had been the day before. I have been doing that for sixty years. It has been enough."
4. The letter ends: "Tonight you cannot draw the whole map. Draw one small line. Tomorrow, draw another. That is all of it." Tam reads it three times. She puts the letter down and, by firelight, draws the two-bend river in the Yesterday Book. It is the straightest one yet. She writes: "Dark night. Small line. Better." Odd reads it over her shoulder and says nothing at all, which is the kindest thing he could do.
5. Hook: Tam takes a deep breath and says out loud, to the firelight, the stars, the sea below: "I'm not too far behind. I'm exactly where I am. And tomorrow I'll be one line further." The night wind blows. Far below, the surf roars against the Hollow Cliffs. She sleeps at last.

# PART FOUR: THE TRUE LINE

## 15. The Hollow Cliffs
Synopsis: Tam and Odd map the coast under time pressure. Odd faces his fear of heights.
Header: *The Fifteenth Rule of Mapmaking: Look up from the paper.*
1. Dawn on the Hollow Cliffs: a shattered white coast, sea stacks, screaming gulls, a path that narrows to a ledge. To map the coast exactly, Tam must see it from the cliff-edge, which means walking the narrowest part. Odd, terrified of heights, goes green.
2. Tam tries to do it alone, edging along on her belly with the chain. She can only see so far. The line she draws is wrong and she knows it. She scrambles back and asks Odd for help instead of pretending: "I need a second set of eyes on the ledge." Odd, sweat on his lip, nods. She ties him to herself with her knotted rope.
3. They go out together, one foot at a time. Odd calls out the drop and the distance to each stack; Tam measures and draws. His fear turns into focus. He finds that if he counts the waves he doesn't look down. He begins to laugh, a small, shaky laugh at the top of the world. "I'm on a cliff," he says. "I'm on a cliff and I'm fine." On the ledge's end they find the old lighthouse, a stubby tower half fallen. Tam climbs its broken stair and, from the top, sees the entire coast at once: the true shape of the Marches. She has never seen so much land in one piece. She draws it in a single long breath, and it is the best line of her life.
4. Odd, safely down, sits on the grass and cries a little with relief and pride. Tam sits beside him. "Nobody in my family's been on a cliff in a hundred years," he says. "My father won't believe me." "Then we'll draw it," says Tam. She adds a tiny figure on the ledge: Odd, in red hair, waving.
5. Hook: they count the days on their fingers. Three left. They are two days' walk from the city if all goes well. A dark line of cloud is building in the east. Odd frowns: "The Aln. If that rain's fallen upriver..." He doesn't have to finish. They both know what floods do to bridges.

## 16. The Broken Bridge
Synopsis: The river Aln is in flood. Tam, Odd, Lucan's team and others must cross together, building a rope bridge.
Header: *The Sixteenth Rule of Mapmaking: A bridge is a map you can stand on.*
1. Evening of day 19. The Aln is a brown roaring monster, twice as wide as before, and the old timber bridge at Marwick is half torn away. The Aln is a brown roaring monster, twice as wide as before. The old timber bridge at Marwick is half torn away. On the near bank a crowd has gathered: Lucan's team, three other Academy teams, a lost peddler and a cheese merchant with a cart. Everyone is stuck. The Guild hall is fourteen hours away.
2. Lucan, muddy but composed, holds a council. Nobody knows what to do. The Academy apprentices have brilliant plans on paper but nobody has ever tied a rope across a flood. Tam, looking at the water, at the remains of the bridge, at her own knotted rope, feels the old flat feeling tugging, and pushes it aside: this is a dock problem.
3. She steps forward: "I know how to tie a bridge line. On the Lowtide Docks we did it every storm." The Academy apprentices stare at the ink-stained girl in the too-big coat. Lucan says, quietly, "Tell us what to do." Tam directs, not shouting but sure: Odd swims a light line across (he knows the eddies), Bram and the merchants haul the heavy cable, Ines measures the sag, Lucan calculates tension with his Academy sums. She ties the knots.
4. It takes all night in the roar of water. It takes rope, planks from the cheese merchant's cart, and every hand. Teams that had been rivals hold lanterns for each other. Dumpling crosses first, on a harness, muttering. Then the peddler, the merchant, the apprentices, one at a time, clinging to the line, laughing and shaking.
5. Tam crosses last, with the Yesterday Book in her teeth. On the far side, Lucan holds out his hand and pulls her up the bank. "That was very good knotting," he says. He takes off his gold compass, looks at it, and hangs it round her neck. "You'll need it. Yours is bent." Hook: dawn is breaking. It is the last day of the Survey, and Verrin is seven miles away.

## 17. The Guild Hall
Synopsis: They race to Verrin and reach the Guild Hall just before the deadline. Tam's map is scruffy but true.
Header: *The Seventeenth Rule of Mapmaking: Sign your work only when it is true.*
1. The last seven miles: a mad dash by cart, on foot and on Dumpling's stubborn back. Odd runs alongside laughing and gasping. Tam clutches the map case, which is wrapped in oilcloth twice over. City walls appear. Church bells begin to toll noon.
2. The Guild Hall of Maps: a great domed chamber with a marble floor inlaid with a compass, tall shelves of ancient maps, and a long table where twenty maps lie unrolled, glowing in the light. The judges in dark robes sit in a row. The Guildmaster, tall Aurelio Quint with a silver compass on his chain, calls the last name: "Tamsin Marrow?" She stumbles in, mud to the knees, hair full of reeds, breathing like a bellows.
3. She unrolls her map. Silence. Next to the elegant, colored, ornate maps of the Academy, hers looks like a work of honest labor: no serpents, no gold leaf, one wobbling compass rose. But every mark on it is true. The judges lean in. One clears his throat. Lucan, standing at the back with his hands folded, watches without expression.
4. In the crowd she spots a familiar figure with a bandaged ankle, propped in a chair, lavender scarf, hawthorn cane. Master Finch has come by cart, against everyone's advice. She doesn't wave. She simply meets Tam's eye and nods, once, the nod of one craftsman to another. Tam's chest fills with something she has no word for.
5. Hook: the Guildmaster says gravely: "Each apprentice will now explain how they made their map." Then, with a hint of a smile: "And also, how they improved in the making of it." A murmur goes through the hall. Finch, in her chair, closes her eyes and smiles as if a very long argument were about to be won.

## 18. The Judging
Synopsis: The judges compare the maps. Lucan's marsh error is discussed honestly, and Tam shows her Yesterday Book.
Header: *The Eighteenth Rule of Mapmaking: Let the reader check your work.*
1. The judges send runners with Tam's map and Lucan's to the Guild's own survey shelves. The Guildmaster tests both against the Guild's oldest, faintest sketches of the Marches. Lucan's ornate map shows a meadow where the Greywater lies. Tam's shows a sunk road and a marsh, exactly.
2. Lucan steps forward before anyone can speak. His voice shakes, but carries. "My map is wrong. I drew the Greywater from the ridge and never walked it. My team nearly drowned. Tamsin Marrow pulled us out." A stir. His father, Lord Ravel, in the front row, goes pale, and then, slowly, something in his stern face loosens.
3. The Guildmaster asks each apprentice to show how they'd improved during the Survey. The Academy apprentices offer polished techniques. Then Tam steps up, takes out the little stitched Yesterday Book, and opens it on the judges' table: the first crooked river, then a week later, then the drowned week, then the two-bend river on the night of the darkest night.
4. She explains the rule, out loud, in her own words, blushing: that you must never compare your line with anyone else's; only with yesterday's. That she came from the Lowtide Docks and only learned to read at nine. That she started further behind than anyone in the room. That she is not a good mapmaker yet. "But I am a better one than I was on the first day. And I'll be a better one tomorrow." A silence in the great hall.
5. The Guildmaster picks up the Yesterday Book, turns the pages one by one, and for a long, long moment says nothing. Then he looks up, at the old woman in the chair with the bandaged ankle. Hook: "Odalys," he says quietly, "you were right."

## 19. The Finch Prize
Synopsis: The results: Lucan's map earns the craftsmanship award; a new prize honors Tam. The rivals become friends.
Header: *The Nineteenth Rule of Mapmaking: The best prize is the distance you have come.*
1. The Guildmaster stands and announces the judges' verdict. He announces the judges' verdict. The Royal Chartery place for the finest craft goes to Lucan Ravel, whose technique is dazzling; but the judges attach one condition: that he re-draw the Greywater and walk it first. Lucan bows, ears pink, and says he would be honored to.
2. Then the Guildmaster explains that fifty years ago, Odalys Finch argued before this Guild that the finest mapmaker is not the one with the best hands but the one who improves the fastest, and she was laughed out of the room. Today, the Guild creates a new honor: the Finch Prize, for the greatest distance traveled between the first day and the last. "It goes to Tamsin Marrow."
3. The hall erupts. Odd whoops so loudly a pigeon flies out of the rafters. Sixpence, in her basket, coos indignantly. Tam can't breathe. She is handed a silver pen. Then a scroll: the Guild will publish her map of the Marches as the official chart, with the names of Tamsin Marrow and Odd Brannock on it. Odd stares at the scroll: his name, on a map. He sits down suddenly on the marble floor.
4. Finch is brought forward on Lucan's arm. Tam runs to her. Finch takes both her hands, her own trembling around Tam's steady ones. "I cannot draw a straight line any more," she says. "But look. I have left one behind." Tam cries into the lavender scarf. Finch pats her hair with the cane hand, in a businesslike way.
5. Later on the steps, Lucan finds Tam. Awkwardly he asks if she would teach him the knotted rope and the Yesterday Book. She says only if he teaches her perspective and shading. They shake on it. He says, "I have never had a friend who didn't want something from my father." Tam says, "I only want your gold compass back." He grins, a real grin, and refuses.

## 20. A Better Line
Synopsis: Back at the shop on Ink Lane, life goes on. Tam draws the same river, one more time.
Header: *The Twentieth Rule of Mapmaking: Tomorrow, begin again.*
1. Two weeks later. Ink Lane at half past five in the morning. Ink Lane at half past five in the morning, gulls, the baker, and Tam Marrow, grinding ink. She hums. It is not a burnt raisin any more; it is a friend. Dumpling stands in the yard with an apple. Sixpence coos on the sill.
2. Master Finch comes down the crooked stairs, cane tapping, pencils in her bun. The Guild has sent letters: forty new apprentices have asked to study in the shop on Ink Lane, all wanting to learn "the Yesterday Book." Finch grumbles about it and looks delighted. She says they will begin with the ink.
3. Odd arrives with his father's blessing and, in his arms, a new gift for the shop: a hand-carved ferry model. His father, he says, saw the map with his son's name on it and cried into his beard for an hour. Odd will do both: the ferry in summer, mapmaking in winter. Tam laughs. Lucan appears with a bag of pastries, his hair slightly untidy for the first time.
4. Evening. The shop empties. Tam sits at the great slanted table with the Yesterday Book, a fresh page and a candle. She draws the two-bend river. She puts it beside yesterday's. It is a whisker straighter. She thinks about the first page, months of pages ago. She thinks about the Docks. She thinks about how far the river of her own line has run.
5. She writes her one honest sentence: "A whisker better. Tomorrow, another." Finch, on the stairs, watching in the candle's light, says nothing, and everything. Last lines: the lamp burns low, the gulls are asleep, the whole kingdom lies on the table as a map still waiting to be drawn, and somewhere in a small stitched book a girl who once thought she started too far behind sets down her pen, smiling, ready to begin again.
