# PART ONE: EIGHT GAMES, ZERO WINS

## 1. The Official Scoreboard
Synopsis: Dani introduces the Mud Hens and their record, and in game eight against the Hollis Hornets she scores an own goal that a boy from her school films.
Header: *Way to Lose #1: Show up.*
1. The official scoreboard: 7 games played, 0 won, 43 goals against, Priya the goalkeeper like a small brave umbrella in a hailstorm; Dani is captain because "nobody voted, it just kind of happened, the way a bruise happens"; the Sunnyvale Sprouts are the only team below them.
2. Marigold Park: the pitch that slants left, the duck pond behind the goal, the ducks with opinions; the whole team introduced by their worst habits: Priya, Tank the apologizer, the Cruz twins, Josie's non-stop commentary, Ahmed and his shoelaces, Ollie and his orange slices.
3. Game eight, Hollis Hornets; 3 to 0 by the ninth minute; the anatomy of a goal against the Mud Hens; Dani's inner announcer voice: "She's looking for a way out of this entire situation."
4. The sideline: Dad's foam finger and "Shake it off!", Abuela who thinks offside is rude behavior, Luz's crayon Deputy Loss Log with its sad ducks; 7 to 0 with four minutes left; Dani finds a gap, turns to pass back to Priya, and hits it a little too well.
5. The own goal. Even the ducks look up. The ref's kind voice; Tank apologizes; forty-three plus eight is fifty-one; Carter Boyle from her school is on the bleachers with his phone up and the red light on; Dani picks the ball out of the net and says to it, "I'm sorry."

## 2. Own Goal Dani
Synopsis: The video spreads through school by Monday; Dani's whole sense of herself starts to crumble, and she nearly quits.
Header: *Way to Lose #4: Kick the ball into your own net, then apologize to the ball.*
1. Sunday night: the video already has 400 views; it is set to a sad trombone; someone has slowed it down and zoomed in on her apologizing to the ball. Dani watches it eleven times "for research".
2. Monday at Harbor Valley Middle: hallway kids make a "whoops" gesture; a boy asks if she needs directions to the right net; Carter says "It's just a joke!" Dani's best friend Nia is kind about it and Dani cannot stand it.
3. In the red notebook Loss Log, Dani writes the ways they've lost, one per game: #1 Show up. She realizes she has never once written a way she played well, because in her mind a good game only counts when the scoreboard agrees.
4. Dinner: Dad's pep talk that goes wrong ("You're the best player out there, mija!" "We lost 8 to 0, Dad."), Abuela says she should have "kicked the boy with the phone", Luz gives her a crayon drawing of a duck in a captain's armband. Dani laughs and then goes very quiet.
5. Night: the league email says their coach, Mr. Dunphy, has resigned "to spend more time with his aquarium." Dani stares at her cleats with the green and orange laces and types a message to the team chat: "I think maybe I'm done." She doesn't send it. Yet.

## 3. The Bus in the Parking Lot
Synopsis: A new coach arrives in a very yellow retired school bus, and Dani decides in the first ten minutes that she hates everything about her.
Header: *Way to Lose #7: Meet a new coach and decide in ten seconds that she's not the answer.*
1. Tuesday practice: nobody expects anyone. Then a huge dented yellow school bus with SCHOOL BUS painted over and the words "GIN'S OTHER BUS" in chalk rolls slowly into the Marigold Park lot and stops with a sigh.
2. Coach Gin Petrakis climbs down: short, square, silver braid, sunglasses on top of her head, fluorescent yellow windbreaker, whistle and bus key on the same lanyard, steel thermos. She looks at the field, the pond, the ducks, and the team and says only, "Well. That's a lot of information."
3. She asks each player one question, and never asks about the score: Tank what he had for breakfast (three pancakes, apologized to them), Josie to stop talking for ten seconds (six), Ahmed what he does when he gets the ball ("run"), Priya what she sees (an answer so quiet nobody hears it).
4. Dani, as captain, informs her about the record, the standings, the video. Gin blows her whistle once to interrupt. "I didn't ask what the scoreboard says. I asked who's on my team."
5. Practice: no drills, no laps. Gin makes them all sit on the bus seats with the windows open and watch a game on the old bus TV: game eight, filmed by Luz. Dani sees for the first time that Priya closed her eyes at the second goal, that Ahmed left his position seven times, and that she herself never once looked up. Gin: "Now. That's data."

## 4. Losing Is Information
Synopsis: Coach Gin reframes losing as information; Dani is furious and tries to hand over her armband, and Gin gives her a week.
Header: *Way to Lose #10: Ask the scoreboard how you're doing.*
1. Dani confronts Gin after practice: "Losing isn't information. It's losing." Gin pours a cup of very strong tea and says: "The scoreboard tells you what happened. It doesn't tell you who you are."
2. Dani, angry, says that's easy for someone who doesn't care about winning. Gin, calm as a lake, points to the scoreboard on the old bus TV and says she cares plenty. She just doesn't think it's the only thing worth measuring. She asks Dani to tell her one thing she did well in game eight. Dani cannot think of one.
3. That night at the Dugout Diner (terrible milkshakes; the team is there in a lump), Tank quietly says he thinks he can stop the winger if he stops apologizing first. Josie says the coach is weird. Ahmed says the coach is weird and also wrong. The twins say the coach is weird in stereo.
4. Dani takes off the yellow armband and puts it on the table by Gin's thermos. "I'm not the right captain for a team that doesn't care about winning." Gin looks at the armband, then at Dani, and pushes it back with one finger. "Give me a week. If it's still no, we'll both quit."
5. On the way home, Abuela's car passes the pond; a duck is sitting on Dani's ball at the water's edge. It looks up. It doesn't give it back. Dani takes out the red notebook and, for the first time, writes down something the Mud Hens did right in game eight: Priya stayed in the goal.

# PART TWO: LOSING IS INFORMATION

## 5. The Autopsy
Synopsis: Coach Gin makes the team study all 51 goals against them and finds patterns nobody wanted to see; Ahmed's refusal to pass becomes the first real conflict.
Header: *Way to Lose #13: Have a plan that only works if nobody else is on the field.*
1. Saturday morning on the bus: Gin lays out fifty-one paper plates, one for every goal against them, and makes the team write each goal's cause on it in marker. Josie narrates it like a crime scene. Luz's Deputy Loss Log becomes evidence.
2. The plates sort into three piles: balls down the slanted left side (14), balls lost in midfield by the person who is trying to do everything (17), and goals scored while Priya has her eyes closed (11). The rest are "weird" (9) and one is "an actual duck" (1).
3. Ahmed hits the roof. His theory of soccer: get the ball, run faster than everyone, score. He points out he's scored four of the six goals the Mud Hens have ever scored. Gin agrees. Then she asks how many of the fifty-one he was near when they happened. He goes silent.
4. Dani realizes with a jolt that "the person who tries to do everything" in the second pile is often her. She defends herself so loudly that Josie stops narrating.
5. Gin doesn't argue. She writes one word on the last plate, hands it to Dani, and says, "Loss Log. Make a new page. Call it Strategy Log." The plate says FIXABLE. Dani puts it on the dashboard of the bus like a trophy she didn't want.

## 6. The Sock
Synopsis: Coach Gin gives each player a "pocket of confidence," one true thing that no scoreboard can touch; Dani struggles to find hers.
Header: *Way to Lose #16: Write down one true thing about yourself, then cross it out because it's not about soccer.*
1. Practice at the bus depot lot where Dad works, among idling school buses, because the field is flooded. Gin hands out index cards and pencils: "Write one true thing about yourself that a scoreboard can't change. Fold it. Put it in your sock."
2. The team's answers: Tank: "I can make a decent croissant." Josie: "I notice everything." Ollie: "I remember everybody's birthday." The twins write the same thing in different handwriting. Priya writes hers and hides it before anyone can see. Ahmed writes "I'm fast" and Gin says, "Fast is a fact. Try again."
3. Dani stares at her card for eleven minutes. Every true thing she thinks of is a soccer thing, and every soccer thing is only true if the team wins. She writes "captain" and immediately hates it because nobody voted.
4. Gin walks over, reads it, and says only: "Is that true when you lose?" Dani doesn't answer. Dad walks over from the depot to say hi and covers for her by hollering about her cleats. Dani realizes her dad has never once asked her how she felt after a game, only what the score was, and that she has never told him.
5. She tucks the card into her sock anyway, still blank on the back. It sits under her ankle bone all afternoon, small and scratchy, like a question.

## 7. The Silent Scrimmage
Synopsis: A practice game with no scoreboard, no score, no winners, and Dani cannot handle it; Ahmed makes his first accidental pass.
Header: *Way to Lose #19: Keep score of a game that has no score.*
1. Gin sets up a scrimmage on the slanted Marigold Park pitch with two rules: no keeping score, and every player has to touch the ball before anyone shoots. She takes down the little plastic scoreboard and puts it in the bus.
2. The first ten minutes are torture for Dani; she keeps counting in her head anyway and getting annoyed at herself for counting. Josie, forbidden to say the score, invents a language of whistles instead.
3. Something odd: without a scoreboard, Tank tackles without apologizing (and then wins the ball), Priya comes out of her goal and stays open-eyed on a cross, and the twins stop arguing about who's older long enough to pass to each other.
4. Ahmed gets the ball, gets blocked, and, out of pure panic, passes it back to Dani, who scores. He stares at the ball, then at her. "Was that... passing?" "Yeah." "It was faster than I thought." Dani laughs so hard she has to sit down on the grass.
5. Afterwards Gin asks: "Who won?" Nobody knows. Dani realizes it's the first time since she was seven that she finished a game and felt, not empty or full, just tired and good. She writes it in the Strategy Log and then hides the log in her backpack, embarrassed.

## 8. What Priya Sees
Synopsis: Priya's secret talent is revealed and Dani learns to admire something she had never counted: reading the game.
Header: *Way to Lose #22: Stand in front of a ball with your eyes closed.*
1. Before practice Dani finds Priya alone in the goal, throwing tennis balls at the crossbar and drawing lines in the mud with her toe. Priya has drawn the whole net as angles and arrows. She is horrified to be caught.
2. "Where do goals come from?" Dani asks. Priya, slowly, without looking up, explains: the shooter's hip tells you where it's going; the standing foot points; most shots go where the slant of the pitch pushes them. She knew the ball's path before it left the foot. She's just always been too scared to open her eyes.
3. Dani offers to shoot at her. Priya keeps her eyes shut for the first five. The next five she peeks. The sixth, she opens them and saves it with a yelp of shock. She saves four in a row. Then eight.
4. Gin, who has been watching from the bus steps with her thermos the whole time, blows her whistle once: "That's data." Dani realizes with a strange lurch that the best player she has ever seen read a game is eleven and afraid of the ball, and that she'd never have known if she hadn't been looking at anything except the score.
5. Priya asks quietly what Dani's true thing is. Dani lies and says "It's a secret." Priya, who is always terrified, says: "Mine is that I can see where things are going." She pulls it out of her sock and shows Dani the card. Dani feels the blank card in her own sock like a little rock.

## 9. Game Nine
Synopsis: Against the Sunnyvale Sprouts, the Mud Hens win their first game of the season; Dani feels the joy and does not notice where it comes from.
Header: *Way to Lose #25: Get so used to losing that winning feels like a mistake.*
1. Game nine, Sunnyvale Sprouts, the only team below them. Gin's pre-game speech is four words: "Look. Pass. Trust. Duck." (There is a duck. It is on the field.)
2. The Mud Hens play differently: Ahmed drops back to make a pass, the twins hold the left side and take turns, Tank tackles without saying sorry (mostly), and Priya's eyes stay open. It is 0 to 0 at half time and nobody knows what to do with it.
3. Second half: Ahmed's through-ball, Dani's shot, the ball crossing the line, the whole park erupting. 1 to 0. Then the Sprouts equalize; then Josie scores an ugly deflected goal off her own shoulder and nobody, including the ref, is sure it counts. It counts. 2 to 1.
4. The final whistle. Dani has never heard anything like it. Tank picks her up. Dad's foam finger is a helicopter. Abuela stands up and shouts "GOOOOL" in a voice that carries across three counties. Luz writes "WIN" in gold crayon and draws a duck with a crown.
5. In the car, Carter Boyle's new video is already circulating: "MUD HENS WIN!!" with the same trombone used happily. Everyone at school will know by Monday, and Dani, riding home with the window down, feels for the first time in her life that she is exactly who she thought she'd be. She does not stop to notice that the feeling is coming from everyone else.

# PART THREE: THE HIGH AND THE HOLE

## 10. Everybody's Favorite
Synopsis: The win changes everything at school and Dani starts to love the applause more than the game.
Header: *Way to Lose #28: Forget to pass and remember to celebrate.*
1. Monday: the hallway is a parade. Carter Boyle asks for a selfie. Kids Dani never spoke to say "captain!" A teacher mentions the game on the announcements. Nia says quietly, "It's nice to see you happy." Dani says, "I'm not happy, I'm winning." She thinks it's a joke. It isn't.
2. At practice, Gin has the team review game nine on the bus TV, and Dani, watching herself score, keeps rewinding to the moment the crowd cheers. Gin notices. Gin says, "What did you see in the pass before the shot?" Dani didn't see it. She'd been waiting for the cheer.
3. Dani skips the Strategy Log twice: no time; she's busy being famous. She takes the card from her sock and forgets to put it back.
4. At the Dugout Diner, the terrible milkshakes taste better now that everybody at the counter keeps looking at their table. Ahmed asks whether he can go back to not passing; Dani says "Depends who's watching," and hears how it sounds.
5. Dad hangs a hand-painted banner on the garage: "MUD HENS! 1 WIN!" Abuela draws a small "9-12" in the corner because she says next year they will need a bigger banner. Dani, grinning, tells the reporter from the free weekly paper: "We just decided to be winners." Somewhere behind her, Priya's face falls, and nobody sees it.

## 11. Hero Ball
Synopsis: Game ten against the Bayside Barracudas: Dani plays selfishly, the team wins, and everyone credits her.
Header: *Way to Lose #31: Score three goals and lose count of your team.*
1. Game ten. Dani decides the plan is simpler now: get the ball and take it herself. She tells Gin at the coin toss that she's got this. Gin lifts one eyebrow and one sunglass-lens, and says nothing.
2. First half: Dani dribbles through four players, scores a goal that would make a highlight reel, and hears the crowd. She doesn't look at Ahmed, who's open in the middle of the pitch with his hand up. Or Josie. Or Tank, on a corner.
3. Half-time: Gin asks, in the mildest possible voice, "How many passes did you make?" Dani says she doesn't count those. Ahmed, next to her, doesn't say a word, which is the loudest thing he's ever done. Priya sits with her head in her gloves.
4. Second half: hat-trick. Three goals, all hers. The Mud Hens win 4 to 3, but they almost lose it when Dani's turnover in their own half leaves Priya alone against two attackers. Priya saves the first, cries on the second, and only barely doesn't concede.
5. The crowd chants "DA-NI, DA-NI," and Dani is lifted onto Tank's shoulders. Gin doesn't clap. She gets on the bus, starts the engine, and just sits there. Later, Dani will remember that she didn't hear a single teammate's voice in the celebration, and won't be able to say when they stopped talking to her.

## 12. Game Eleven
Synopsis: Against the Harbor Valley Hawks the whole Mud Hens plan collapses; Dani makes her worst mistake yet and says something cruel to Priya.
Header: *Way to Lose #34: Tell your goalkeeper exactly what you think of her.*
1. Game eleven against the Hawks, the second-best team in the league, and Dani is still full of last week. She has told the newspaper they'll finish the season "undefeated from here." Coach Brandt of Northgate is in the crowd, scouting them for the final.
2. The Hawks are fast, patient, and remarkably good at exactly the thing the Mud Hens stopped doing: passing. By the tenth minute it's 3 to 0. Dani tries to take the game on herself, loses the ball four times, and every time the Hawks score off it.
3. Halfway through the second half the score is 7 to 0. Dani turns on Priya after the eighth goal and yells across the pitch, in front of everybody: "You're supposed to STOP them! That's your entire JOB!" Priya's face goes white. She doesn't answer. She takes off her gloves and sets them down very gently in the goalmouth.
4. Gin blows the whistle, subs Ollie into the goal (the first time all season, with a smile that breaks Dani's heart), and takes Dani off the field. On the sideline Dani watches Ollie face a penalty with the orange-slice bag still in his hand. He saves it. The bench is delirious, and nobody looks at her.
5. Final score: 9 to 1. The ducks, in the pond, are silent. The video will be online by dinnertime. In the car, Abuela says: "Even the duck felt bad for you." Dad, for the first time in his life, says nothing at all. Dani looks at the card in her sock. It's still blank. She has been playing with the wrong things written on it.

## 13. The Bench
Synopsis: Dani is benched for the first time in her life and finds out how much of her "confidence" was made of other people's cheering.
Header: *Way to Lose #37: Sit on the bench and count everyone else's mistakes but your own.*
1. Sunday: the new video is worse than the first. It has a title card, a slow-motion replay of the moment she shouted at Priya, and 2,000 views. Kids leave comments. Nia texts "call me?" Dani doesn't. She puts the phone under a pillow, and then the pillow under her bed.
2. Practice: Gin doesn't make a speech. She calmly lists the starting nine on the whiteboard and Dani's name isn't on it. "Take a week on the bench," Gin says. "Watch." Dani says the team needs her. Gin says: "That's the first thing I've heard you say all season that isn't true."
3. From the bench Dani watches everything for the first time from the outside: Josie's commentary has become useful (she calls the space before anyone else); Tank has learned to stop and stand tall instead of hiding; the twins have invented a signal; and Priya is standing very still in the goal with her gloves back on, looking at nothing.
4. Priya doesn't look at Dani once. Nobody says anything cruel. That's the worst part. Dani keeps waiting for someone to yell at her so she can be angry, and no one does. Ahmed, warming up beside her, says simply: "I passed four times today. Nobody yelled." "That's because you were good," Dani says. "That's because I was on a team," he says.
5. At home Dani opens the Loss Log. She reads every entry back to page one and, for the first time, sees a pattern in her own handwriting: every "way to lose" is about what other people thought. She writes: "Way to lose #37: ..." and can't finish the sentence. Luz, in the doorway, hands her a crayon. "Deputy says try again."

## 14. The Armband
Synopsis: Dani hits bottom, tries to quit, and Coach Gin finally tells her one plain piece of her own story.
Header: *Way to Lose #40: Take off the armband and hope somebody asks you to put it back on.*
1. Thursday. Dani leaves the yellow armband on the bench without a word, bikes to the bus depot at dusk and sits in the doorway of Gin's old yellow bus. She's decided: she isn't a captain, she isn't a player, she is a girl with an own goal.
2. Gin appears with two mugs of tea, one too strong for a twelve-year-old. She sits on the bus step. She says she drove a school bus for thirty-eight years and had forty-one children on it most mornings; nobody ever asked her the score. She says she lost a lot of things she loved along the way, including her husband, and none of them were games. She says it plainly. She doesn't dwell on it.
3. Dani says she doesn't know who she is without a win. Gin says: "That's the most honest sentence you've said all season. Now we know what we're working on." She takes the blank card out of Dani's cleat (Dani's been carrying it, of course) and reads the front: "captain." She asks what Dani did well against the Hawks. "Nothing." "Try again."
4. Dani sits with it for so long the streetlights come on. Then, very slowly, she says: "I saw where Tank was going to be before he got there. I was already on it. I just didn't pass." Gin says "Mm." Dani says: "I see the field before it happens. I always have. I just never counted it because it doesn't show up on the board."
5. She takes a pencil and writes it on the card: "I see where the play is going." She folds it small and puts it in her sock. Gin taps the armband, still on the bench three miles away, and says: "It'll be there. But first you owe somebody an apology, and it isn't me."

# PART FOUR: THE OTHER KIND OF CONFIDENCE

## 15. Sorry Is Not a Text
Synopsis: Dani apologizes to Priya properly, in person, and learns what Priya has been carrying.
Header: *Way to Lose #43: Say you're sorry in a text.*
1. Friday: Dani writes an apology text to Priya, deletes it, writes it again shorter, deletes it, and finally bikes to Priya's house with a bag of orange slices from Ollie (she's told him what it's for; he gave her two bags).
2. Priya's mother opens the door. Priya, in the backyard, is throwing a tennis ball against a garage door and catching it, over and over, with her eyes open. Dani says she's sorry; not a speech, just the plain version: "I said something mean because I was scared, and it was about me, not you."
3. Priya says she's known for a while that she was scared. She says Dani wasn't wrong about the goals; she was wrong about who they belonged to. She says her true thing wasn't only about angles. It was: "Even when I let one in, I stay." She says she stayed because of the team, and because of Dani, and Dani has to sit down on the grass.
4. They start throwing the ball. Priya explains the whole pitch as a map of angles and slope, and Dani starts sketching arrows in the red notebook. For the first time the Loss Log page turns into the Strategy Log page and it's Priya's handwriting in the corner.
5. In the driveway Dad has waited in his truck for an hour. Dani climbs in. She tells him something true for the first time: that she has been afraid for a long time that people only like her when she wins. Dad grips the wheel. He says, roughly: "I liked you when you were three and couldn't kick a ball. I still do." He doesn't say anything else, and it is exactly enough.

## 16. The Hens' Own Plan
Synopsis: The team designs its own strategy from what each player actually does well, and Dani returns as a teammate, not the star.
Header: *Way to Lose #45: Try to be somebody else's team.*
1. Sunday at the bus depot: the whole team in the yellow bus, windows down, plates and markers. Dani returns without the armband and says she'll do whatever Gin asks. Gin says the armband isn't hers to hand out. The team votes. Nobody knows how, so they vote by throwing socks. The armband goes back to Dani, but this time on a vote.
2. Gin asks each player the question she's been asking since the first day: what are you good at? Tank: "Standing in the right place." Josie: "Seeing what's happening and saying it." The twins: "Making noise on the left." Ahmed: "Going fast and, apparently, passing." Priya: "Angles." Ollie: "Everybody else." Dani: "Seeing where it's going." The board fills up.
3. The plan they build is very simple and very Mud Hens: use the slope of the field instead of fighting it. Ahmed stays high and fast. The twins hold the slanted left with a signal. Priya calls the angles. Josie's commentary becomes an official job title: "Voice." Ollie will come on for the last ten minutes with the orange slices and a secret weapon.
4. They practice it until the sun drops. It's not glamorous. The first attempt goes very badly, which Gin calls "excellent data." The third attempt gets a pass through four players that ends in a goal off the post. Nobody cheers loudly. Everybody looks at each other.
5. Dani realizes, walking home, that for the first time she has a whole afternoon behind her and no idea who won anything. She also realizes she isn't afraid of Saturday. Whatever the score is, it can't take the card out of her sock.

## 17. Northgate Week
Synopsis: The team prepares for the undefeated Northgate Wolves; Dani meets Kai Brandt, who is not what she expected.
Header: *Way to Lose #47: Meet the other team's star and decide to hate him.*
1. The last game of the season: the Northgate Wolves, twelve wins, no losses, matching uniforms, a team bus that has a bathroom. Josie reads their stats aloud until Tank asks if she could read something a little less loud. Gin, unbothered, says: "Nice bus."
2. Dani goes to the Dugout Diner alone to think, and Kai Brandt is at the next booth, in his spotless white-and-blue warm-up, alone, eating a plain hamburger like a person who's been told exactly what to eat. He knows exactly who she is. He recognizes her from the video. He says: "I watched the own goal. I've done that. In fifth grade. Nobody filmed it. Lucky."
3. They talk for twenty minutes. Kai's dad is Coach Brandt. He isn't allowed to lose. He hasn't had a game in two years he didn't have to explain at dinner. He says: "You guys look like you're having fun." Dani says they are. He looks at her like she's said something about another planet.
4. Coach Brandt appears at the counter, sees his son with the enemy, and barks that it's time to go: a short, hard voice that makes the whole diner go quiet. Kai stands up so fast he knocks over his water. He mouths "Sorry." Dani sees, plainly, that Kai's confidence is on loan too.
5. Friday night: the Mud Hens' last practice. Gin doesn't give a speech. She hands out a single sheet with three words on it: "Play the game." Under it, in tiny writing: "The scoreboard isn't invited." Dani reads it and puts the paper in her sock, next to the card.

## 18. Game Twelve, First Half
Synopsis: The Mud Hens play the best half of their season; the Wolves respond; Dani watches the field and the score both and holds onto herself.
Header: *Way to Lose #49: Let somebody else's dad tell you who you are.*
1. Saturday, Marigold Park. The whole town seems to have turned out: Dad's foam finger, Abuela with a folding chair and a fan, Luz with a fresh crayon notebook labelled DEPUTY WIN LOG (she's optimistic), Carter Boyle filming, but this time he puts the phone down and just watches. Nia has come. The ducks are at the edge of the pond, expectant.
2. The Wolves score on the third possession, a perfect strike from Kai that Priya reads and touches but can't stop. 1 to 0. Coach Brandt on the sideline is shouting in a voice like a slammed door. Dani feels the old drop in her stomach. Then she feels the card in her sock and, oddly, it holds.
3. The Hens' plan comes together: Ahmed's fast lane, the twins' left-side signal, Josie's running commentary calling the space ("Ahmed left! Ahmed LEFT!"), and Dani, seeing the pass before it happens, threading the ball between two defenders. Ahmed scores. 1 to 1. The park loses its mind and, for the first time all season, Dani does not.
4. Tank stops Kai in the box with a perfectly clean tackle and does not say sorry. Kai, on the ground, grins up at him. Tank offers a hand, apologizes for the apology he didn't make, and everybody laughs. Coach Brandt shouts at the ref. Coach Gin doesn't say anything. She sips her tea.
5. Half-time: 1 to 1. Northgate hasn't been level at half-time all season. Coach Brandt is lecturing his team in the far corner. On the Mud Hens' side, Gin passes out the orange slices (Ollie has brought hundreds) and says: "You're doing it. Now the hard part: don't do anything different because of the score." Dani realizes the scoreboard has been showing 1-1 for ten minutes and her hands are steady.

## 19. Game Twelve, Second Half
Synopsis: A hard, honest second half with a heartbreaking final minute; Dani loses the game and keeps herself.
Header: *Way to Lose #51: Miss the last shot.*
1. Second half. The Wolves score early, 2 to 1, off a rebound Priya should have caught (and maybe could have, if she hadn't made three saves in a row before it). She looks at the ground. Dani jogs back and taps her gloves: "Angles." Priya takes a breath. Then she makes a save that makes the whole bench stand up.
2. The Mud Hens push. Josie, Voice, keeps up the running commentary: "Ruiz on the ball, and she's looking, and she's looking..." Dani hears herself, in Josie's voice, being the person she had once narrated in her own head, and for the first time it isn't sarcasm. It's a description.
3. Minute 47 of 50. Josie's cross, Ollie (subbed on at last, orange-slice-free) wins a header, the ball drops to Ahmed, who could shoot, and hands it back to Dani in the box, deliberately, with a "yours." 2 to 2. The park does something hard to describe. Kai, on the pitch, applauds.
4. Minute 49. A long ball down the slanted left, the one place the Hens have never defended, and this time they do, all three of them, and it goes out for a throw-in. Then the Wolves' corner, an ugly bouncing ball in a crowd, and a deflection off Tank's knee that drops to Kai, who finishes. 3 to 2. There is time for one more kick. The ball comes to Dani thirty yards out. She takes it up the middle and strikes it as cleanly as she has ever hit anything. It hits the crossbar. The whistle blows.
5. Dani stands there with her hands on her head, exactly where she stood after the own goal, and waits for the drop. It doesn't come. She finds Priya. Priya finds Tank. Tank hugs the twins, and both of them stop arguing about who is older, and Josie, for the first time in recorded history, says nothing. On the sideline Gin turns to Dani and says only: "Information." Dani laughs so hard she cries. In the red notebook that night she writes Way to Lose #51 and then, underneath, in her own handwriting: "Also: the best game we've ever played."

## 20. Way to Win
Synopsis: The season ends; the team celebrates a loss; Dani writes the first Way to Win and understands what she has learned.
Header: *Way to Win #1: Show up.*
1. Final standings: Mud Hens 2 wins, 10 losses, ninth out of ten; the Sprouts, who never won, finish tenth. Nobody at the Dugout Diner cares. Dad orders the terrible milkshakes for the whole table. Abuela finally learns what offside is (a boy standing where he shouldn't), and is scandalized, and wants to talk to the league.
2. Carter Boyle posts a new video: the last minute, the shot, the crossbar, the whole team hugging. It has the same trombone, but he's slowed down the hugging. The comments are kind. Dani watches it once and doesn't watch it again. Nia sends a message: "Best own goal ever." Dani writes back: "Best team ever."
3. Coach Gin, at the depot, is packing her thermos and her lanyard; she'll be leaving the team after the season. She has a grandson's swim team to look after. She doesn't say goodbye; she says, "You did the work." Dani gives her the yellow armband to hold. She says: "It's not the armband. You know." Dani says: "I know."
4. Dani opens the red notebook to the last page. She has written fifty-one ways to lose, and she reads them over: a list of every way her worth had depended on someone else. She adds one plain sentence to the last page, the only sentence in the book that tells you what it all meant: "The score is what happened. I am who I am." She closes the notebook and, in the dusk, puts the armband back on herself.
5. The final entry of the book, in her own handwriting: "Way to Win #1: Show up." She walks out onto the empty field, the ducks at the pond, the slanted left side, the last of the light, and for one long, quiet moment she is simply herself, alone, exactly enough. A ball rolls toward her from nowhere. She picks it up. She says, out loud: "Okay. Next game."
