# FIFTY-ONE WAYS TO LOSE A SOCCER GAME — story bible

Realistic sports comedy for ages 10-12. Loud, hilarious, warm, and true about how it feels to try hard and lose. Set in an ordinary American suburb in the present day. No magic, nothing spooky, no romance, no bullying that goes unresolved. The soccer is described accurately and simply enough for non-players.

## Voice
FIRST PERSON, PRESENT TENSE, narrated by Dani Ruiz, 12, brash, quick, funny and competitive, who talks like a sports announcer in her own head ("And Ruiz takes the ball, she's looking left, she's looking right, she's looking for a way out of this entire sentence...") and keeps lists, stats and ratings for everything. She exaggerates, confides in the reader, and is bluntly honest about her own worst moments. Her humor is fast and specific; when something truly hurts she gets quiet, plain and short. Every chapter opens with an italic entry from her Loss Log on its own line, then a blank line, in this exact form: "*Way to Lose #4: Kick the ball into your own net, then apologize to the ball.*" The numbers run up through the book (first chapters cover several numbers: pick the next ones in order, at least one entry per chapter; the last entries are Way to Lose #51 and then, in the final chapter, "*Way to Win #1: ...*"). The entries are funny and specific, and each one is secretly a lesson she has yet to learn.
Voice sample:
"*Way to Lose #1: Show up.*

Here is the official scoreboard of my soccer career so far: Games played, 8. Games won, 0. Goals we have let in, 51. That's an average of six and a bit goals per game, which sounds like a lot until you see our goalkeeper, Priya, who is terrified of the ball, standing in front of the net like a small brave umbrella in a hailstorm.

I am the captain. Nobody voted. It just kind of happened, the way a bruise happens."

## The premise
Dani Ruiz, 12, captain and central midfielder of the Marigold Park Mud Hens, the worst under-13 team in the Harbor Valley Youth League. Eight games into the twelve-game season the Mud Hens have lost every single game (51 goals against, 6 scored). Dani has always believed her worth as a player and a person goes up and down with the scoreboard and with what people say after the game: if they win and everyone cheers, she's great; if they lose, she's nothing. She keeps a "Loss Log" in a red notebook, where she lists the ways they've lost, half as a joke, half as a way of punishing herself. After a very public own goal that was filmed and passed around school, she is close to quitting.
Then the team gets a new coach: Coach Gin Petrakis, 60s, a retired school-bus driver with a whistle, a thermos and no interest whatsoever in the scoreboard. She changes how the team thinks about losing. Four games remain: Dani has to learn to find her confidence from inside, so that a loss changes her plan but not her worth.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Dani (Daniela) Ruiz**, 12. Wiry, brown skin, dark hair in a high ponytail with a yellow elastic, bright yellow captain's armband, scraped knees, cleats with one green lace and one orange. Fast, stubborn, competitive, funny. Secret: her confidence is on loan from other people; without applause she doesn't know who she is.
- **Coach Gin (Virginia) Petrakis**, 60s. Short, square, silver braid, sunglasses on her head, a fluorescent yellow windbreaker, a steel thermos of very strong tea, a lanyard with a whistle and a bus key. Blunt, funny, calm as a lake. Has been through much; she says almost nothing about it. Her lines: "Losing is information." "The scoreboard tells you what happened, not who you are."
- **Priya Nair**, 11, goalkeeper. Tiny, glasses strapped on with a sports band, oversized gloves; afraid of the ball and of shouting; secretly a brilliant reader of angles.
- **Tank (Marcus Bell)**, 12. Very large, very gentle, plays defender; apologizes to opponents when he tackles them; wants to be a baker.
- **Josie Kaplan**, 11. Never stops talking; plays winger; narrates the game aloud.
- **Ahmed Suleiman**, 12. The fastest kid in the league; refuses to pass ("Passing is slow").
- **The twins, Leo and Mateo Cruz**, 10. Defenders, identical, finish each other's sentences, argue about who is older.
- **Ollie (Oliver) Pratt**, 11. Substitute, always brings orange slices, always cheerful, seldom plays.
- **Dad (Rafael Ruiz)**, a school-bus-depot mechanic, loud, proud, wears a foam finger; **Abuela (Carmen)**, sharp-tongued, doesn't understand offside; **Luz**, 7, little sister, keeps a "Deputy Loss Log" in crayon.
- **The Northgate Wolves**: rich, polished, undefeated; star striker **Kai Brandt**, 12, confident, not a villain; **Coach Brandt**, his father, intense, yells.
- Places: Marigold Park (muddy field with a slanted left side and a duck pond behind the goal), Harbor Valley League, the Dugout Diner, Dani's kitchen, the bus depot.

## The season
Ch1-4: the losing streak, the own goal video, Dani nearly quits, the new coach arrives. Ch5-9: Coach Gin's odd methods (losing is data; the Loss Log turned into a Strategy Log; playing a game with no scoreboard; each player's pocket of confidence), the first tiny wins, and the first win (game 9). Ch10-14: the good stretch: overconfidence, a crushing defeat, Dani's confidence collapses because it was still borrowed; she is benched after a huge mistake; the Dark Night. Ch15-19: rebuilding from the inside; Priya's moment; the team's own strategy; the final game vs the undefeated Northgate Wolves; the result. Ch20: aftermath and Way to Win #1.

## Running gags / motifs
The Loss Log (a red notebook; Luz's crayon Deputy Log); Josie's non-stop commentary; Tank's apologies; Ahmed's refusal to pass (he learns); Priya's angle-reading; Coach Gin's thermos and "Losing is information"; Abuela's confusion about offside; the duck pond behind the goal (balls end up in it); the Dugout Diner's terrible milkshakes; the pocket: Coach Gin makes each player write one true thing about themselves and keep it in their sock ("pocket of confidence") that no scoreboard can change.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
If your confidence comes from within, a loss doesn't hurt your value; it just changes your strategy. Dani begins with all her worth on the scoreboard. Losing repeatedly proves to her that it can't stay there. Coach Gin reframes each loss as information. The book must show three stages: borrowed confidence (dependent on applause and wins), the crash when the win streak ends, and confidence that comes from inside (knowing what she does well and what she's working on, regardless of the score). Never let the narrator moralize except one plain line near the end. Never make winning the point: the last game must be decided honestly and Dani's confidence must survive it whichever way it goes.

## Things to avoid
No cruelty that isn't resolved. No adults yelling at kids in a way that's celebrated. No romance. No profanity. Keep the soccer accurate: 11-a-side is not needed; the league plays 9-a-side on a small field, 2 halves of 25 minutes. Use plain descriptions for non-fans.
