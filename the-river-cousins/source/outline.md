# PART ONE: TWO WAYS OF KNOWING

## 1. Fourteen Floors Down
Synopsis: (POV Jun) Jun is packed off from his Seoul apartment to spend three weeks in the village, and arrives by bus to find a girl with a wheelbarrow.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 32 degrees, sunny, arrival 2:10. Nari's river says: not yet.*
1. Fourteenth floor, Seoul: Jun has never carried anything heavier than a backpack, and now his mother is zipping a suitcase the size of a small refrigerator. Three weeks at his grandparents': clean air, ice-cold watermelon. Jun protests: he has hagwon. Hagwon is closed for the summer break, and she checked.
2. Jun's list of what he will miss and what he is bringing: charger, backup charger, notebook, vacation homework, a jar of his mother's kimchi she insists on. He is twelve, thin, in round glasses and brand-new white sneakers. His father calls from work: "Be nice to your cousin." Jun says he is always nice. Nobody laughs.
3. The intercity bus: the buildings fall away; rice paddies and hills appear; his phone drops from four bars to three, two, one. He checks the weather: sunny, ten percent chance of rain. He feels a small cold worry: he has never been anywhere he could not search.
4. He steps off at a rusty bus shelter on a dusty road in the hot afternoon, and cicadas scream from every tree. No grandparents. There is a heap of green mud-splashed something: a wheelbarrow, and behind it a girl with a braid and a straw hat hanging on her back.
5. The girl looks at his suitcase, at his sneakers, at his phone. "You're four minutes late," says Nari, "and you brought all that?" Jun opens his mouth to explain the bus schedule. She has already picked up his suitcase and dropped it in the wheelbarrow.

## 2. The Wheelbarrow
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Nari wheels Jun's suitcase home, forms an opinion of city cousins, and meets him at her grandmother's table.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 1 bar of signal, 91% battery. Nari's river says: warm and slow.*
1. Nari's view: the cousin from Seoul walks like the road might bite him. His suitcase has small wheels for a floor that does not exist. She wheels it and hums so she does not say what she thinks. He talks a great deal and points at things his phone shows him.
2. Village: a hundred houses in a green valley, rice paddies, corn, sweet potatoes, red peppers ripening. Nari names things as they pass and Jun says, "I know, I've seen it on a map." She squints at the sky and sniffs the air. "Rain by the end of the week," she says. "The app says ten percent," says Jun.
3. Home: the low farmhouse, the wooden porch, the pump, the yard with straw mats where the first red peppers are drying like a red carpet. Halmoni comes out wiping her hands on her apron, small and round with short curly grey hair and a purple sun visor, and says "Aigoo!" three times and hugs Jun until his glasses fall off.
4. Shoes off at the door; Jun's white sneakers already have a brown line on them. Halmoni's rule: "Eat first. Talk later." She sets out cold noodles in icy broth, kimchi, a bowl of rice, and a watermelon the size of a bowling ball cut into red half-moons. Jun takes a photograph of the noodles before he eats them. Nari rolls her eyes so hard it hurts.
5. Harabeoji comes in from the yard in his faded green cap and tall black boots, says almost nothing, looks at Jun for a long time, and then nods once. At the end of the meal he says one sentence to both of them: "Rules of the river. Vests on. Never alone. Stay with the boat." Nari knows it by heart. Jun writes it in his notebook and underlines it twice.

## 3. Shoes at the Door
Synopsis: (POV Jun) Jun tries to do everything right in a house where every rule is unwritten, and loses his first argument.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 2 bars at the window, 88%. Nari's river says: the frogs are loud tonight.*
1. Jun's first evening: there is no chair; you sit on the floor at a low table. Everybody is served in an order he does not understand. He bows to Harabeoji too late and too low and knocks his glasses off again. Halmoni laughs and pinches his cheek. "Aigoo, so polite."
2. Dinner: many small dishes and his phone will not tell him which to eat first. Nari says, "Just eat." He says, "I'm working out a method." Nari says a method is what you do when you are afraid. He says she is afraid of anything without a screen. She says, "I'm not afraid of anything." A pause. "Except tall buildings. But that's not fear. That's common sense."
3. Their first quarrel: over which is faster, the bus or the truck. Jun has the numbers; Nari has a truck. Neither is really talking about buses. Halmoni raises both hands: "Eat, eat." Uncle, Nari's father, broad and quiet in a straw hat, says "Both are right" and takes more rice, which makes them both angry.
4. Night: thin mats are unrolled on the floor in the small room next to the kitchen. The floor is cool (in winter it would be warm; Halmoni explains the old heated floor and Jun looks it up). The cicadas are so loud he cannot think. He holds his phone up to the window to get the one bar and sends his mother one word: "Arrived."
5. Through the wall he hears Nari say, to nobody, "He'll last two days." He puts his phone under his pillow and lies awake trying to decide whether to prove her right or wrong.

## 4. The Long River
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Nari shows Jun the river, the boat called the Pear and its rules, and he shows her something she did not know.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 33 degrees, sunny, 85%. Nari's river says: brown-green, easy, low.*
1. Morning: Nari takes Jun down the path through the corn to the river. He is wearing the white sneakers again. She says nothing about them, loudly. The river is wide and slow and shining, with sandbars like sleeping animals and stepping stones at the ford. She has spent every summer of her life beside it.
2. She shows him how to read water: brown-green means calm; a smooth sliding "tongue" means a channel; wrinkles mean stones; floating sticks mean it has rained upstream. Jun listens, unimpressed, then asks how she knows. "I looked," says Nari. "For eleven years."
3. The boat, the Pear: a flat-bottomed wooden boat in faded pear-green paint tied at the near bank with two oars and two orange life vests under the seat. Harabeoji's rule again: vests on, never alone, stay with the boat if you get lost. Jun is delighted by a rule that is clear; Nari is annoyed at how delighted he is.
4. Jun opens his phone and shows her the river from above, a satellite picture: the bends, the far field, a long sandbar she has never seen the shape of, a bridge and an old stone weir far downstream. Nari looks, and for a full minute does not say a word. She has lived here all her life and never seen it from the sky.
5. She says, "That's not the river." "It is," says Jun. "It's the same river." "The river's not a picture." The river slides past the stepping stones, brown-green and patient. She does not tell him she is going to look at that picture again tonight.

## 5. The Score
Synopsis: (POV Jun) The cousins start a competition, Jun keeps score, and the sky turns strange.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 10% chance of rain. Nari's river says: something is coming.*
1. The scoreboard begins in Jun's notebook: two columns, NARI and JUN, and a tick for each time one of them is right. Nari rolls her eyes but insists on checking it.
2. The contest: thumping a watermelon to find the ripe one (Nari right, Jun's app confused); knowing which way is north in the yard (Jun right, Nari looks at the moss); feeding the chickens (Nari, of course); fixing the wobbly table with a folded napkin (Jun). By supper it is Nari 3, Jun 3.
3. Halmoni, patching a straw mat, watches them count and says "Aigoo" softly, in a way that is neither happy nor sad. She turns to Harabeoji. "These two." He says, "They are alike." Both cousins say "We are not" at the same time and glare.
4. Evening: the air goes thick and hot, the cicadas stop suddenly, and the sky in the west turns the color of a bruise. Nari sniffs, squints, and says, flatly, "Tomorrow. Rain. A lot."
5. Jun opens the weather app: ten percent, sunny. He shows her the screen. She shows him the sky. He writes a tick under his name, then hesitates and looks at the sky again. The first fat raindrop hits the notebook and blurs his ink.

# PART TWO: RAIN

## 6. Ten Percent
Synopsis: (POV Nari) The rains arrive three days early, the app is wrong, and Halmoni fries scallion pancakes to the sound of the rain.
Header: *Jun's phone says: sunny, 10% chance of rain. Nari's river says: rising a finger.*
1. Rain: the first big drops on the tin roof of the shed, then a roar. The peppers on their mats must be covered fast; everybody runs, even Jun, who carries the tarp upside down and is laughed at, even by Halmoni. It is the funniest thing Nari has seen all year. She refuses to tell him.
2. When the peppers are safe and everyone is soaked, Jun holds out his phone with the weather still saying ten percent. "It's wrong," he says. He looks stunned. Nari wants to gloat and, for one second, finds she does not.
3. Halmoni in the kitchen, frying pajeon: scallions and batter in a hot pan, the sizzle exactly like the rain on the roof. "When it rains, pajeon," she says. "The sound matches. It has always been so." Jun tastes one and forgets to photograph it.
4. The village meeting is announced: the rains have come early and long, and the levee at the lower field is old. Uncle says little, but Nari sees his jaw.
5. That night the river sounds different, louder and browner, and she stands on the porch and listens with her mouth open. Jun comes out and stands beside her. He does not say the app was wrong, and she does not say she was right, and for a while they simply listen to the river together.

## 7. Three Days of Rain
Synopsis: (POV Jun) Three days of rain, a dead radio, and something Jun is good at.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 64% battery, no signal. Nari's river says: brown and fast.*
1. Rain: three days of it. The house smells of wet earth and frying oil. The tin roof roars. Jun's phone has no signal and 64 percent battery, and he rations it like water. He does his vacation homework at the low table and Nari's sighs from across the room have a rhythm.
2. Harabeoji's old radio in the shed has died and he has been listening to the rain instead. Jun opens it with a spoon handle and Nari's screwdriver, sees a loose wire, and mends it with a paper clip and a bit of foil. Nari watches and says nothing.
3. The radio crackles, and a voice reads the river forecast: heavy rain upstream, the dam will release water tomorrow night. Harabeoji, who has not smiled in days, turns to Jun and says one word: "Good." Jun feels his ears go hot and his whole chest fill.
4. Nari, who has been wanting for three days to be the one Harabeoji says "good" to, goes out into the rain to feed the chickens, slamming the door. Jun writes a tick under his name and does not feel like winning.
5. Halmoni finds him at the low table, rubs the top of his head, and says quietly, "She is good at things too. You will see." It is the first time anyone in the house has told him to look instead of count.

## 8. The Color of the Water
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Nari teaches Jun to read water in the rain, and Jun teaches Nari to read a map; the adults grow worried.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 60%, dam release tomorrow night. Nari's river says: brown as tea, sticks and foam.*
1. Nari is annoyed at herself for being annoyed. She takes Jun, both in raincoats and rubber shoes (a borrowed pair for him), down to the river. It is fast and thick brown, with sticks, foam and a whole tree branch turning. She names what she sees: color, speed, sound. Jun repeats it back like a lesson.
2. On the porch afterwards, in the dry, Jun shows her the map on his phone again and explains what the blue lines and the bends mean. She traces the river with her finger: "That's the bend by the willows. That's the Lower Landing." He says, "I didn't know it had a name." She says, "It has lots of names. You just have to ask."
3. A small truce: they compare. She knows what the river is doing now; he knows what it does farther down. They realize, without saying it, that they are looking at the same river from two ends.
4. The adults are worried. Uncle stands on the porch in a raincoat looking at the river. Auntie packs rice balls and boiled eggs in cloth. Nobody says the word "flood". Nari has never seen her father look at the river like that.
5. That night Nari lies awake and hears the river, and through the thin wall hears Jun awake too, and neither says so. The rain hammers the roof. Something is changing, and they are both listening to it.

## 9. The Meeting in the Hall
Synopsis: (POV Jun) The village gathers, the grown-ups leave for the levee, and the children are left with a job they were told not to do.
Header: *Jun's phone says: dry for the next three hours. Nari's river says: a pause, then a push.*
1. The village hall in the evening: every family in wet raincoats, a huge pot of rice on the floor, the whole village talking at once. Jun has never been in a room where everyone knew everyone's name. Halmoni is given a bowl the size of a basin, and elders are served first.
2. The plan: the men will go to check the levee and the sluice gate at the lower fields with sandbags; the women will cook and carry hot rice and soup to them; the children are to stay at the house and keep the door shut. Halmoni tells Nari and Jun to stay put, then adds "Eat something," because it is the only order she ever gives twice.
3. Back at the empty farmhouse, the rain stops, a hush after three days of roar. The peppers: Uncle's first red peppers of the year are on mats in the far field across the river where the sun is best, under a tarp that had blown half off in the afternoon wind. If the rain comes back before morning the whole crop will be lost.
4. Nari says the pause will last a few hours. Jun's phone says the same: dry for three hours. For the first time they agree on a forecast. It feels strange, like stepping in the same footprint. Harabeoji is at the levee; the boat is at the near bank. "We could do it in an hour," says Nari. Jun says, "We were told to stay." Nari says, "We were told to keep the peppers." Neither is quite sure that is true.
5. They decide together, which is new: vests on, one hour, straight across and back. Jun writes it in his notebook: "Plan: cross, cover, return. 1 hour. Vests." Nari adds, in her own handwriting, "Stay with the boat."

## 10. Across the Water
Synopsis: (POV Nari) The children cross to the far field, cover the peppers, and tie the boat in two different ways.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 58%, one bar. Nari's river says: fast in the middle, quiet at the edges.*
1. The crossing: orange vests over their clothes, Nari at the oars, Jun in the bow with a flashlight and a tarp. The river is wider and faster than in the daytime, and the far bank, that seems so close, takes forever. Jun says nothing for once and holds the sides.
2. The far field: peppers laid out on straw mats under the half-blown tarp. They work together, shaking off the water, pulling the tarp tight, weighing the corners with stones. It goes faster than either expected, and neither says why.
3. The boat: it must be tied. Nari wraps the rope round the post and ties a hitch her grandfather taught her, the way it has always been done. Jun says there is a better one, a knot he saw in a video, with a loop. They argue: her knot is what Harabeoji uses; his is what the internet says is strongest.
4. They both tie, half of each, one over the other, and Jun says "Fine." Nari says "Fine." It looks a bit like a hitch and a bit like a pretzel. Neither is quite proud of it and each is a little too proud to say so.
5. They climb back to the last bit of tarp to finish the far corner, and the wind changes. Nari sniffs and squints at the dark sky upstream. A sound comes down the valley that she has never heard: a long low hush like a great animal breathing out. "Jun," she says. "Look at the river."

# PART THREE: THE RIVER

## 11. Loose
Synopsis: (POV Jun) A wall of water comes down from the dam, the rope slips, and the boat carries both cousins away.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 55%, no signal. Nari's river says: rising, fast, now.*
1. The river climbs the bank in front of their eyes: a rush of brown water, sticks and foam, roaring past the post. The dam has released early. The boat tugs on its rope, and the strange half-hitch, half-loop knot begins to slide.
2. They run for the boat. Nari reaches it first and grabs the rope; Jun grabs Nari; the knot lets go with a sound like a small cough. The boat swings into the current with Nari half aboard and Jun in the water to his knees, holding on to the stern. Nari hauls him in by his vest.
3. The Pear spins out into the middle of the river. One oar is jammed in the rowlock; the other is gone, floating away like a stick. Jun stares at the far bank shrinking. The far field, the tarp and the peppers slide out of sight.
4. Neither speaks. Jun's hands are shaking; the river is louder than he expected, and he is colder than he expected, and he is much more afraid than he expected. He says the only thing he can think of: "This is my fault. It was my knot." Nari says, "It was mine." "It was ours," says a small voice in his head that sounds a lot like Halmoni.
5. The dark comes down. The last light goes out of the sky. The shore is only a long black line. The boat drifts with the current, and the two cousins in their orange life vests sit very close in the middle of it.

## 12. Stay With the Boat
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Jun wants to swim for the shore; Nari remembers her grandfather's rule and holds firm.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 48%, one bar, blinking. Nari's river says: wide and fast; no bottom to stand on.*
1. Nari's mind is very clear and very cold. She checks: vests fastened, one oar, no bailer, one flashlight, one phone, Halmoni's cloth bundle of rice rolls. She says the list aloud so that it becomes something they can hold.
2. Jun says they should swim for the near bank and points at the black line, which looks close. Nari says no. He says she cannot know that. She says she does: the river in the middle is fast and deep, and the bank is farther than it looks, and Harabeoji's oldest rule is stay with the boat. "The boat is big. We are small. People look for boats."
3. They argue in fierce whispers because shouting would take breath. Jun says her rule is tradition and the map says the bank is 40 meters away. Nari says the map does not tell you what the water is doing. For the first time, Jun does not answer. He looks at the water and sees what she sees: the sticks racing, a whole bush going by.
4. "Okay," he says. "We stay." It costs him a great deal. "But I want to do something useful." Nari, surprised, says, "You hold the flashlight and read the map. I'll steer." She has never asked a city boy for anything in her life.
5. Jun clicks on the flashlight. In its small yellow circle the water looks very black, very fast and very big. He has a job. It is the first time all day that he has felt less afraid, and he has no idea it is because someone gave him something to do.

## 13. The Bend
Synopsis: (POV Jun) Jun's map and Nari's reading of the current find a sandbar, and the two knots become one.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 41%, a sandbar 1.2 km ahead on the left. Nari's river says: the tongue swings left after the bend.*
1. Jun keeps the phone out of the spray, screen dimmed to save the battery, and reads the map like a sacred text: a bend to the right, then a long sandbar on the left, then a bridge. He says it aloud; Nari says "Where's the tongue?" and he says "What tongue?" and she says "The smooth part. That's the channel."
2. She uses the one oar like a rudder, leaning her whole body on it, feeling the current with her palms: "It'll push us right, then swing left. If we let it, we land on the sandbar." Jun says, "The map says the same." They look at each other in the yellow light of the flashlight, surprised.
3. They do exactly what both say: Nari steers with the oar, Jun calls the bends from the map, and the boat slides off the fast water toward the left as if it had been waiting for permission. Something grinds under the bottom, and the Pear stops.
4. A sandbar: pale, long, still, with a few willows at the far end. They climb out in the shallows in their vests, and haul the boat up the sand together. It weighs a great deal, and they laugh once, breathlessly, like people who have run out of any other option.
5. The knot. They cannot leave the boat loose. Nari ties a hitch; Jun says it needs a loop to hold if the water rises. They try it his way, then her way, then look at each other. He does a loop and she does a hitch and they pull and it holds better than either alone. They tug it once, twice, and the boat does not move.

## 14. Night on the Sandbar
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Cold, dark, and a bundle of rice rolls: the cousins talk about the things neither has said.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 34%, one bar, message sending. Nari's river says: still fast, but our side is quiet.*
1. The night: frogs, the river's low roar, a sky washed clean between clouds, thousands of stars Jun says he has never seen in Seoul. Nari says she has never counted them. Jun says he could. They count until forty and lose track.
2. Halmoni's cloth bundle: rice rolls with vegetables and egg and pickled radish, a boiled corn cob each, a bottle of barley tea. They eat sitting on the overturned boat, leaning against each other for warmth without saying so. Nari finds that gimbap tastes best when you are frightened and someone else is eating it too.
3. Jun tells her about hagwon: seven classes a week, homework until eleven, the fourteenth floor with nobody home till dark, his parents tired and kind and never there. He says it in a very even voice, as if reading it from a screen. Nari says nothing for a long time. Then she says, "I'd hate that." "I know," says Jun. "It's not that I hate it. It's that I'm good at it, and I don't know what else I'd be good at."
4. Nari tells him what she has never told anyone: that she wants to see Seoul, the tall buildings, the subway with its trains under the ground. She says it as if confessing to a crime. Jun does not laugh. He says, "I'll take you." She says, "You'll get lost." He says, "I never get lost." They both start laughing, and cannot stop.
5. On the last bar of signal Jun writes one message to Uncle and Auntie, the only two numbers he has: "We are on a sandbar, both fine, boat is fine, vests on, we stayed with the boat." The phone spins, spins, and says: sending. The battery says 30 percent. They fall asleep sitting up.

## 15. The Water Comes Up
Synopsis: (POV Jun) Rain returns before dawn, the sandbar shrinks, and a plank becomes a paddle.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 12%, message not delivered. Nari's river says: the sandbar is getting smaller.*
1. Jun wakes to water lapping at his shoes. It is dark grey, not yet dawn, and rain is pattering on the boat and on the sand. The sandbar is half the size it was. The river is louder, and brown in the first light, and higher.
2. Nari is already up, looking at the water with her whole body. "It's rising a finger every ten minutes," she says. "We have an hour, maybe less. We have to go." Jun says, "With one oar?" Nari says, "With whatever we've got."
3. Jun looks at the boat: a flat-bottomed boat with a loose floorboard. He pulls it up, sees that it is one long plank with a wide end, and says, "That's a paddle." Nari says, "It's a floor." "It's both," says Jun. He has never been so happy to be wrong about a floor.
4. Nari tries it, and it is clumsy and heavy. Jun shows her how to hold it at an angle, like a spoon. Nari shows him how to lean into the stroke with the current instead of against it. Together they make it work; it takes four tries, and both of them are wet to the elbows.
5. They untie the combined knot together; it comes apart in one pull. Jun says, "Best knot ever." Nari says, "It was both." They push off the shrinking sandbar into the grey morning, with two paddles, two vests and 12 percent battery.

# PART FOUR: HOME

## 16. One More Bend
Synopsis: (POV Nari) The river carries them toward an old weir, and the cousins choose the narrow, quiet way through.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 9%, old weir 800 m ahead. Nari's river says: the water is smoothing, then dropping.*
1. Dawn: pale, pearl, the rain turning to drizzle, the far hills dim and enormous. Nari, paddling, feels the water go strange: smooth in front, wrinkled far ahead. "Something's coming," she says. Jun reads the map: an old weir, a low stone wall across the river with a drop.
2. The sound comes: a steady roar with white water at the bottom of it. Nari knows what a weir can do; every child in the valley knows. Her arms shake. She says she cannot steer against the current for long, and Jun, for once, does not say a word about the tradition.
3. Jun studies the satellite image in the failing light of the phone, and sees a narrow side channel on the far left, where the water goes through a gap in the weir wall, quiet, smooth and dark. "There," he says. "Left." Nari looks and sees the smooth tongue that leads to it, and says, "That's the tongue."
4. They paddle hard, both on the left side, both leaning, and shoot into the channel with a bump. It is only a few seconds and it feels like a year. The roar goes past on the right; the boat swings and slides, and then is quiet water again.
5. Behind them the weir booms. Ahead: a wide, slow reach with a small concrete pier and a stooped shape at the end of it with a long fishing rod. Nari says "Landing." Jun's phone says 8 percent, and then it goes dark.

## 17. The Lower Landing
Synopsis: (POV Jun) A fisherman named Mr. Cho pulls them in, boils them corn, and phones the village.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 0%, screen dark. Nari's river says: calm; someone is waiting.*
1. Mr. Cho, round and cheerful in a wide-brimmed hat and a vest with many pockets, is drawing in a line when a small pear-green boat with two orange life vests bumps gently into his pier. He looks at the children, the plank paddle and the muddy sneakers, and says, "Aigoo," very softly.
2. He pulls the boat in and hauls them up onto the pier one after the other. He does not ask who is right. He asks if they are hurt. They are not. He asks if they are hungry. They are. He puts on a pot on a small gas burner in a shed and boils corn.
3. Jun tells him, all in one breath, who they are, whose grandchildren, which village and where. Nari adds the name of the farm and the name of the boat. Mr. Cho knows Harabeoji: "The one who says four words a year?" "Five," says Nari.
4. Mr. Cho calls the village on his own phone. Jun hears the voice on the other end break, and go quiet, and then he hears Uncle say, from a long way away, only: "Are they there? Are they whole?" Mr. Cho hands the phone to Nari, and she says, "Appa," and does not manage anything else.
5. They eat hot corn on the pier in the drizzle, in the blankets Mr. Cho finds under a bench. Jun finds that his sneakers are no longer white and that he no longer minds. He says, "My phone died." Nari says, "Good. Now we're even."

## 18. The Long Way Back
Synopsis: (POV Nari) Uncle's truck arrives, Halmoni scolds and cries at once, and Harabeoji says almost nothing.
Header: *Jun's phone says: charging. Nari's river says: brown, but going down.*
1. The small blue truck comes bouncing down the gravel road at speed. Uncle jumps out before it stops and holds Nari and then Jun in a hug that lifts them both off the ground. He smells of rain, mud and river. He does not say anything for a long time.
2. In the truck bed with the Pear tied on top under a tarp, they ride home through drifting rain, past the paddies, past the levee (which held, with a lot of sandbags and a lot of neighbors), past the village hall where every light is on.
3. Halmoni is at the gate in her flowered clothes with her hair wild. She runs, as fast as her knees allow, and hugs Jun and Nari together. She scolds and cries at the same time, "Aigoo, aigoo, you foolish, foolish children," and slaps their backs in a way that is mostly a hug. Nari has never seen her grandmother cry.
4. Harabeoji stands on the porch in his boots and says nothing. He looks at the boat, at the plank paddle, at the vests, at the knot. He walks down to the boat and touches the rope where the knot is still tied. Nari's throat closes. He looks at the children a long moment and says: "Vests on?" "Yes," says Nari. "Stayed with the boat?" "Yes," says Jun. He nods, once, and goes inside.
5. Jun is fed, bathed, wrapped in a blanket, given a mat in the warmest corner and told, by Halmoni, that he is grounded and that she loves him. Nari tells him from the next mat that he is not grounded, he is loved. "Both," says Jun, who is learning.

## 19. What Harabeoji Said
Synopsis: (POV Jun) The peppers, the trouble and one plain sentence.
Header: *Jun's phone says: 100%. Nari's river says: calm.*
1. Morning: the sun comes out. The peppers! Jun and Nari run to the yard and find the mats spread out, and the peppers safe: the neighbors had crossed at first light by the bridge and covered the far field properly. Nari sits down in the mud. Jun sits down next to her. They laugh until Halmoni comes out and joins them for reasons of her own.
2. The trouble: Uncle and Auntie call them in for a talk that is not a shouting. It is worse; it is quiet. "You should not have crossed," says Auntie. "We should not have left you," says Uncle. Jun says, "We decided," and Nari says, "Together." Uncle says that is the part he cannot decide whether to be angry about.
3. Harabeoji speaks last, on the porch, with his cap on his knee. Two long sentences in a year: "A boat with two good rowers pulling different ways goes nowhere. Yesterday you pulled together." He looks at Jun: "The radio. The map. Good." He looks at Nari: "The river. The rule. Good." He puts on his cap: "The knot. Half and half. Not good. Then good."
4. The chores are the punishment, as promised: two weeks of washing the boat, hanging the vests, sweeping the porch and filling the pump bucket, together. Jun asks if he can do it wearing shoes he does not care about. Halmoni says, "Aigoo, that boy," and gives him her own old rubber shoes.
5. That night Jun opens his notebook to the score sheet: Nari 9, Jun 8. He looks at it for a long time, and then, very slowly, tears out the page.

## 20. The Last Day of Summer
Synopsis: (POV Nari) The holiday ends and the cousins say goodbye with a new scoreboard.
Header: *Jun's phone says: home. Nari's river says: home.*
1. The last day of the holiday: bright, hot, dry, the river quiet and a clear green again, the peppers glowing red on their mats in rows. Jun's suitcase stands by the door. It has, at some point, gained a layer of mud that will not brush off. He does not want to brush it off.
2. Halmoni fills his bag with rice rolls, a whole watermelon (which does not fit), a jar of homemade kimchi to carry home to his mother, and a promise about Chuseok in the autumn, when the whole family will gather. Harabeoji shakes his hand with both of his, and says "Come back." Three words. It is a lot.
3. At the bus stop under the rusty shelter, the cicadas scream. Jun holds out a folded page: the torn score sheet, taped back together. On it he has written, under a new heading, "US: 1". Nari laughs. She gives him a smooth flat stone from the river with the faintest curve of a boat drawn on it in pen.
4. The bus comes. Jun says, "I'll take you to Seoul. Winter break. You'll see the subway." Nari says, "I'll check it out first." "You'll hate the buildings." "I'll love the buildings," says Nari. "Then I'll tell you why they're wrong." They both grin.
5. The bus pulls away and Jun's face is at the window until the dust hides it. Nari walks home along the river with her hands in her pockets and the stone in one of them. She sniffs, squints at the sky, and says to nobody, "Clear till Thursday." Then she takes out a piece of paper from her pocket with Jun's phone number on it, and looks at it a long moment, and smiles.
