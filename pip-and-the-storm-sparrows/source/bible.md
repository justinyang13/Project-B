# PIP AND THE STORM-SPARROWS — story bible

Animal fantasy adventure for ages 10-12: a small bird in a big sky. Lyrical, exciting, funny, tender. No gore, no cruelty for its own sake, nothing spooky. Predators exist (hawks, cats) but are handled with gentle peril; no character is killed on the page. Birds do not use human tools or clothes; they live in trees, cliffs, reeds and rooftops, and they talk to each other.

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Pip, with a lyrical, fast-moving, nature-loving narrator who loves wind, weather and sky and tells the story with a light comic touch. Sentences that soar and dive, sensory bird's-eye detail (the smell of rain coming, the taste of a hot updraft, the way a hillside looks from above like a rumpled quilt), and short, funny, blunt bird dialogue. Bird speech has small habits: "Tweet-truth?" means "Really?"; birds measure distance in wingbeats and time in "sunups". Each chapter is titled with a weather phrase and begins with a one-line "Sky Report" in italics on its own line, then a blank line, for example: "*Sky Report: light wind from the west, clear, a small bird with big ideas.*" The sky reports get more dramatic as the storm season nears, then calm at the end.
Voice sample:
"*Sky Report: light wind from the west, clear, one very small bird with very big ideas.*

Pip was the smallest sparrow in Thistlewick Hedge, which is a thing you could have worked out for yourself by looking at her, or by listening to the other sparrows, who mentioned it often.

'Smallest sparrow in the hedge,' said Uncle Dorn, as he did every morning, 'and smallest sparrow she'll stay.'

Pip was eleven weeks old and weighed less than a walnut. She stood on the top twig of the hedge with her toes curled tight and her chest puffed out, and she looked at the sky, which was very large, and she thought: *Yes. That one. I'll have that one.*"

## The premise
Every autumn the great migration flock of Thistlewick Hedge flies south across the Grey Sea to the Warm Reaches. The Storm-Sparrows are the legendary elite of the flock, the fastest, boldest sparrows, who ride the edge of the autumn gales ahead of the flock and read the sky for the safe route. Every year the Elders choose new Storm-Sparrows at the Gathering by a flying test, the Trial of Three Winds. Only big, strong-winged sparrows are ever chosen. Pip is the smallest sparrow anyone has ever seen, a runt hatched a full week late, who has been told her whole life by almost everybody (her Uncle Dorn, the Elder Marra, the older fledglings) that she is too small, too light and too slow to ever be a Storm-Sparrow. She has decided she will be one.
She isn't allowed to enter the Trial. She enters anyway.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Pip**, 11 weeks, the smallest sparrow in Thistlewick Hedge: a tiny, round, brown-streaked sparrow with a crooked tail feather on the left (from a fall out of the nest), bright black eyes, and a habit of tilting her head when thinking. Stubborn, funny, brave, and tender, with a habit of talking to the wind as though it were a person. Her secret weapon (she doesn't know it yet): being very light, she rides thermals and gusts that heavier birds can't feel; her small size lets her fly where big birds cannot.
- **Uncle Dorn**, an old, plump, gloomy sparrow who raised Pip after her parents were lost in a storm two autumns ago; grumbles constantly out of love and fear; secretly proud. His refrain: "Small birds stay low."
- **Marra**, the Elder: a large, grey-streaked sparrow with a torn wing tip and a booming voice, keeper of the flock's rules and of the Trial. Strict and fair; the one who says "Storm-Sparrows are built, not wished."
- **Cinder**, 12 weeks, the star fledgling: large, glossy chestnut sparrow, arrogant, fast, sure to win the Trial. His mother is a famous Storm-Sparrow. Not evil, but casually cruel ("Tweet-truth? Pip?"). Later shows fear and a heart.
- **Nettle**, 11 weeks: a skinny, freckled young female sparrow with big feet, always anxious, always hungry; Pip's best friend; a brilliant reader of clouds who never speaks up.
- **Old Tuck**, a one-eyed, retired Storm-Sparrow who lives alone in the hollow of a lightning-struck oak at the edge of the hedge; ragged, dry, funny; the only one who doesn't tell Pip she can't. Once the smallest bird in his own class, he learned that being small meant knowing how to ride what big birds fight.
- **Skua Grey**, a huge, cold, patient sea hawk (a peregrine-like falcon) who hunts the coast along the flock's route; a threat, never a monster. **Mother Wren** (background), a busybody. **Gullet**, a friendly, dim, giant seagull who helps at the sea crossing.
- Places: Thistlewick Hedge (home), the Lightning Oak (Tuck's home), the Gathering Rock, the Windmill Fields, the Salt Marsh, the Cliffs of Calder, the Grey Sea, the Lantern Isles (stopover), and the Warm Reaches.

## The plan of the story
Ch1-4: Pip's home, everyone saying she can't, the announcement of the Trial, her decision to enter anyway, Old Tuck's first lesson. Ch5-9: training with Tuck, learning to read wind, comic fails, Cinder's mocking, Nettle joins in secretly, Pip disqualified from the Trial by Marra but flies it in secret at dawn. Ch10-14: the Trial of Three Winds: Pip finishes last but her flying in the third wind stuns everyone; Marra says no; the flock departs; then the great storm comes early and the flock is scattered over the Grey Sea; Pip flies the storm alone. Ch15-19: the storm crossing: Pip finds Nettle, Cinder, Uncle Dorn and others scattered; she rides the gale's updraft to spot the Lantern Isles; she leads the flock through the storm by wind-reading with her light body, Skua Grey stalks; Cinder faces his fear; Tuck's lesson pays off. Ch20: landfall in the Warm Reaches, Marra's understated apology, Pip as the first "Storm-Sparrow of the Small Wind."

## Motifs
"Small birds stay low" (Uncle Dorn's refrain; at the end he says "Small birds go where they like"); the crooked left tail feather (Pip's "rudder"); the Sky Reports; Pip talking to the wind; Nettle's cloud-reading; Tuck's "Ride it, don't fight it"; counting wingbeats.

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Never let anyone tell you that you can't. If you want something, you have to go get it. Pip hears "you can't" from every direction, including from people who love her. She doesn't win by pretending to be big; she wins by learning the sky as a small bird can, by practicing until her failures shrink, by using what makes her different, and by going for it when nobody gives permission. Do not make the other birds villains. Uncle Dorn's warnings come from fear of losing her. Never have the narrator state the moral except, at most, one plain line near the end.

## Things to avoid
No killing on the page. No human characters or human tools. No romance. Keep bird behavior charming and plausible. Don't repeat "you can't" too mechanically; vary. No modern slang.
