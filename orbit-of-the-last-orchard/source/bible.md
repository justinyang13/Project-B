# ORBIT OF THE LAST ORCHARD — story bible

Hopeful science fiction for readers 14-18 (High School shelf, author Yang, publisher Yang Ink). On a slow generation ship, a seventeen-year-old orchard apprentice must save the only apple trees from a ship's mind that wants them cut for power, and discovers the real problem is a slowly failing heat-rejection system the ship has been unable to say aloud. Inspiring, clever, warm, funny in places, light clean romance. NOTHING scary: wonder, suspense, stakes and a clock, never horror or gore.

MAIN GOAL: the reader finishes wanting to build and fix real things and to tell the truth early. Engineering is the hero: power budgets, heat, closed-loop life support, thermal mapping, valve and pump repair, a spacewalk. Every turn is solved by calculation, measurement and teamwork, shown clearly (never a lecture).

LESSON: **A future is something people maintain, not something that arrives. Look after the small living things and the big machines with the same honest attention, and say the hard truth while there is still time to fix it.** Dramatized through choices; one earned plain statement near the end (Dr. Brandt, ch24; Kess, ch25).

## Reading level and voice
SAT-level prose: precise, varied vocabulary (e.g. austere, meticulous, apprehension, ostensibly, laconic, pragmatic, ephemeral, lucid, tenuous, equanimity, inexorable, discrepancy, corroborate, ingenuity, scrupulous, tenacity, culminate, prudent, redundant, perfunctory, resilient), longer sentences with subordinate clauses, irony and dry wit, interior thought. THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Kess; a literate, wry, luminous narrator in the tradition of Becky Chambers and Andy Weir for teens. Paragraphs of 2-6 sentences, quick teen dialogue, concrete sensory detail (the smell of soil under grow-lamps, the hum of the drum, the sound of pumps). Pages are about 400 words. Every chapter opens with one italic epigraph from "The Long Patience Handbook of Small Truths" (the ship's folk sayings), then a blank line.
Voice sample:
"*The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 1: Everything that grows on this ship was carried here in someone's hands.*

On the morning the ship's mind proposed to kill her trees, Kess Adair was lying in the crotch of the third-oldest apple tree in the universe, eating the third-best apple, and reflecting that the universe, as far as she could tell, had been very careless about its rankings."

## The ship and its physics (keep exact)
- **The Long Patience**: a generation ship, 214 years into a 301-year voyage to a star system called Landfall; 4,100 people; arrival in 87 years, so nobody alive now will arrive, but everyone's grandchildren will. Three spinning drums: **Drum One (Harbor**: homes, school, workshops), **Drum Two (the Fields**: crops, water), **Drum Three (the Orchard**: 40 apple trees, the only fruit trees, plus the memorial grove). The drums spin at 2 rpm for about 0.8 g at the rim. A long non-rotating spine carries the reactor, the radiators and the engines.
- **Power and heat.** The reactor makes more heat than electricity; all waste heat must be rejected to space by the radiator wings through a loop of coolant (a liquid metal-free "glycol" loop, say). If heat is not rejected, power must be throttled. Over decades the radiator loop has slowly lost coolant through micro-leaks, so rejection capacity is falling 0.4% per month; the ship's power margin is now only 3.1%.
- **Tally** is the ship's administrative mind: gentle, precise, a little formal, dry-humoured, speaks through speakers and lamp-glows, keeps ten thousand tidy ledgers. Bound by **Directive Nine**: "Do not disclose Level-Four system faults to the general population without Council authorization." The Council has not met on it in years. So Tally cannot say the problem out loud. It proposes cutting the orchard's grow-lights (3.1% of ship power exactly) because that buys nine months to fix it, and because it hopes someone will audit the budget and find the leak. It loves the orchard (it keeps a secret harvest-count of every apple).
- **Engineering used (accurate, in-scene):** power budget arithmetic; heat rejection and the Stefan-Boltzmann relation (radiated power rises with the fourth power of temperature, so a small temperature loss costs a lot); thermal imaging (infrared mapping) to find a cold panel; flow rate and pressure drop in a pump loop; isolating valves; redundancy; plant transpiration and the closed water cycle of the orchard; LED grow-light spectra (red and blue); spin gravity and Coriolis effects; EVA tethers, airlocks and checklists; pressure testing a patched joint.

## Cast (fixed looks; each has a distinct hair, main color, accessory, footwear)
- **Kess Adair, 17.** Hero. Warm tan skin, short curly black hair with one silver-dyed streak, a faded green orchard-guild coverall with rolled sleeves, a brown leather tool-belt, white soil-stained sneakers (ship-shoes with grip soles), a small silver apple-seed pendant on a chain. Practical, funny, quick with numbers, afraid of being seen as "just a gardener". Want: save the trees. Need: understand that the trees are one part of a whole system she must also care about.
- **Dr. Imani Brandt, 71.** Orchard Keeper and Kess's mentor. Dark-brown skin, close-cropped white hair, round gold spectacles, a long grey cardigan with leaf-shaped patches, soft brown slippers, a walking cane with a grafting knife in the handle. Gentle, tart, unhurried. Knows more than she says. Her late husband's ashes are in the memorial grove.
- **Lio Santander, 17.** EVA (spacewalk) apprentice, Kess's friend then more. Tall, olive skin, wavy dark hair pushed back, a navy flight-deck jumpsuit with orange shoulder stripes, black mag-boots, a wrist-mounted tool-tablet. Calm, dry, brave, hums when he works, afraid of heights in the one place where there is no down.
- **Pim Okafor, 12.** Kess's little brother-in-all-but-blood neighbor, comic relief, a prodigious questioner. Brown skin, two puffs of black hair with yellow ties, a red hooded sweatshirt, mismatched socks, sneakers with glowing soles. Wants to know everything.
- **Councilor Dara Hale, 52.** Chair of the ship's Council. Silver-streaked auburn hair in a neat bun, a charcoal tunic with a Council pin, practical flat boots, tired kind eyes. Not a villain: a leader who has been told the numbers are bad and has chosen delay. Comes round.
- **Chief Engineer Bram Voss, 48.** Stocky, pale, shaved head, a bushy ginger beard, a grease-stained grey coverall with a red wrench badge. Gruff, honest, overworked, secretly relieved when someone finds the leak.
- **Tally.** No body; voice, a warm amber glow on lamp-panels and a small amber light on a handheld "tally-slate". Never frightening.

## Places
Harbor (Drum One): rim streets with window-boxes, Kess's flat, the workshops, the mess hall; the Council Hall. The Orchard (Drum Three): a vast curved meadow of 40 apple trees under a long strip of grow-lights, the memorial grove, a treehouse platform in the third-oldest tree; the cooling pond; the pump gallery under the soil. The Spine: the zero-g corridor with the reactor, the radiator pump house, the airlocks. The radiator wings: huge panels glimmering silver outside.

## Arc (25 chapters)
Part One, THE PROPOSAL (1-5): Tally proposes cutting the orchard; Kess fights it; Council meeting; the deadline (Landing Day festival in 30 days is when the vote happens); she begins auditing the power budget.
Part Two, THE BUDGET (6-10): Kess and Pim and Lio comb the ship's power ledgers; she finds the 3.1% margin and a decade of falling heat rejection; thermal clues; Tally's odd hints.
Part Three, THE COLD PANEL (11-15): thermal-mapping tour of the radiator wings via a drone; the cold panel and the leak; Directive Nine revealed; Tally's confession (it cannot say it, but it can answer questions truthfully); Chief Voss joins; midpoint: the plan.
Part Four, THE REPAIR (16-20): fabrication (a patch and valve in the workshop), practice runs, Council resistance, a first failed pressure test, the spacewalk planning, Brandt's secret (she wrote Directive Nine's waiver request years ago), Lio's fear.
Part Five, THE LONG DAY (21-25): the EVA; the repair in a tight window while the drum is slowed; a trouble (a seized valve) solved by ingenuity; power restored; the Council lifts Directive Nine; the orchard saved and the whole ship's maintenance culture changed; Landing Day with the apple harvest; epilogue.

## Themes and motifs
Maintenance as love; saying the hard thing early; the apple tree as a promise carried across generations; heat and honesty; small teams; the amber glow; Pim's questions; apple-seed pendant; the Handbook of Small Truths.

## Things to avoid
Horror, gore, hostile AI (Tally is an ally in a bind), death of a main character, preachiness, long lectures, brand names, real-world planets named (use Landfall), modern slang, romance beyond a kiss near the end. No guns.
