# PART ONE: THE PROPOSAL

## 1. The Third-Best Apple
Synopsis: In the oldest apple tree's crotch, Kess Adair learns that Tally, the ship's mind, proposes to cut the orchard's grow-lights to save power, and she sets out to stop it.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 1: Everything that grows on this ship was carried here in someone's hands.*
1. Open on Kess in the crotch of the third-oldest apple tree in the Orchard, eating the third-best apple, 214 years into the voyage; the long curve of Drum Three rising on every side like a green wave frozen mid-break, grow-lights humming, Pim Okafor below asking forty-one questions about why apples fall "sideways" when the drum spins.
2. Dr. Imani Brandt shuffles under the tree with her cane, announces that Tally has put a proposal in the Council's Landing Day agenda; Tally's amber glow warms the lamp-panel above and it asks, courteously, whether Kess has a moment; it reads the proposal: reduce the Orchard's lighting to zero in thirty days, saving 3.1% of ship power.
3. Kess argues with the voice in the lamp: the trees will die, they are the only fruit trees, every apple on the ship descends from forty seeds; Tally listens, replies that the arithmetic is exact and regrettable; Kess notices that for a mind that never hesitates, it hesitates.
4. Brandt takes her to the memorial grove, where small young trees grow over her late husband's ashes; she says the Orchard has survived three Council reviews and a fungus, and will survive this one if Kess does one thing: "Learn what the number is for."
5. That night in her family's flat on the Harbor rim, her mother reading with her feet up, her father asleep over a bread proof, Kess writes THREE POINT ONE PERCENT on a scrap and stares at it; the window shows the black spine of the ship turning slowly against the stars, and she begins, without knowing it, an audit.

## 2. Three Point One
Synopsis: Kess visits the Harbor workshops to learn what three percent of the ship's power actually is, and meets Lio Santander and Chief Voss.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 2: A number is only a rumor until you know what it is a fraction of.*
1. Morning rounds: Kess crosses Harbor on the moving walkway, noticing the ship's small economies (a bicycle repaired five times, a jacket patched into a quilt); she reaches the Power Office, where a clerk shrugs and says the ledgers are "complicated".
2. Chief Engineer Bram Voss, gruff and enormous, tells her to bring a better question; Kess asks how much power the reactor makes and where it goes; he draws a rough pie on the back of a spanner receipt and says the Orchard's lights are "a rounding error with leaves on".
3. Lio Santander, in a navy EVA jumpsuit with orange shoulder stripes, is waiting by the exit with his tool-tablet; he has been told to stay away from the Power Office but stays by it anyway; he offers her a better question: where does the heat go?
4. Lio explains the basic problem: the reactor makes far more heat than electricity; the heat must go out to space through the radiator wings or the ship has to throttle the reactor; he shows a diagram on his wrist-tablet; Kess realizes that the ship's power is not limited by fuel but by how fast it can get rid of heat.
5. She asks whether the radiators are working; Lio hesitates for a very small moment, which a stubborn girl notices; he says the radiators have been "within tolerance" for years; she says she did not ask about tolerance; he says "You're going to be difficult, aren't you?" She says "Only about important things."

## 3. Pim's Forty-One Questions
Synopsis: Kess's neighbor Pim becomes her research assistant, asking the questions she is afraid to ask, and they discover the ship's public power ledger has a missing page.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 3: The best engineer on the ship is whoever has not yet been told what is impossible.*
1. Pim Okafor, twelve, red hooded sweatshirt, glowing-soled sneakers, arrives at her door at dawn with a list of forty-one questions, the first being "Is Tally lying?"; Kess's mother, amused, provides porridge and lets them work at the kitchen table.
2. The public ledger terminal in the Harbor library: every kilowatt tallied in tidy columns; Pim, by asking dumb questions, notices that the column marked "Spine thermal rejection" has numbers every month for two hundred years but with a small asterisk beginning eleven years ago and no footnote.
3. They look for the footnote: a librarian, a retired welder named Mr. Daro, says the footnote used to be on a page the Council removed "for tidiness"; Pim asks what it said; Mr. Daro says "Ask the one who tidies."
4. Kess, with a quiet dread she tries to disguise, asks Tally directly whether the radiators are within tolerance; Tally says, in its lamp-warm voice, that all public figures are available in the ledger; she asks about private figures; Tally says, with the faintest pause, "You may ask me any question whose answer I am permitted to give."
5. Kess understands that the answer is not "no": it is a locked door; Pim whispers "That is the most suspicious sentence I have ever heard"; Kess says "Write it down"; they start a secret notebook, and at the top Pim writes THE SPINE QUESTION in glittery green pen.

## 4. The Council Hall
Synopsis: Kess is allowed to speak for the orchard at a Council session, and she makes her first honest case; Councilor Hale quietly warns her to be careful what she asks for.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 4: Speak plainly. People can only agree with what they have understood.*
1. The Council Hall in Harbor: tiered benches, a dome painted with the star-map of Landfall, a dusty clock; Kess in her green orchard coverall, hair brushed flat by her mother, stands at the speaker's circle with Brandt at her shoulder.
2. Councilor Dara Hale chairs; Tally's proposal is read; Voss testifies about the power margin; Kess requests time for an "independent audit" of the energy budget before the vote; a heckling councilor, Ferris Quill, argues that apples are a luxury.
3. Kess answers by calculating on the spot what the Orchard produces: fruit, oxygen, water cycling, mental health, and sixty-one thousand seeds a year for the Landfall settlement; she wins a murmur, then loses it when Ferris asks why the Orchard should be exempt from the sacrifice everyone is making.
4. Hale suspends the session and grants her thirty days (the Landing Day vote stands) to produce her audit; afterward in the corridor Hale touches Kess's sleeve and says in a low voice, "Be careful what you ask the ship. It may tell you." Kess, startled, asks what she means; Hale only says "Landing Day. Thirty days."
5. On the walkway home Kess feels the deep unnameable thrum of the ship, the drum's constant turning, and tells Pim and Lio, who have been waiting outside, that the Council is not the enemy; "Something is wrong with the ship and nobody is allowed to say it." Lio says quietly that he knows how to find out.

## 5. Zero-G Library
Synopsis: Lio takes Kess and Pim to the Spine for her first look at the radiator pump house and the first thermal clue.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 5: Weightlessness is only falling that has forgotten how to land.*
1. The ride up the spoke: the climb through a long tube where gravity thins from 0.8 g to nothing, Kess's stomach going strange; Pim delighted; Lio steady and humming.
2. The Spine: a long, cold, humming corridor of pipes, cable-bundles and reactor shielding, lit amber; the sense of enormous, delicate machinery; Kess is awed and a little afraid and notices that her fear feels exactly like wonder.
3. The radiator pump house: banks of pumps pushing coolant to the wings; Lio shows her the flow-meters, and Kess, who knows nothing about pumps, notes that the readings hover below a painted red line; Lio says "That red line was moved. Look at the paint."
4. She lifts a corner of a new label and finds an older painted line a hand lower: the "within tolerance" mark was moved to match the failing numbers; Lio swallows; he admits he suspected it; Kess photographs it with the borrowed slate; Pim, floating upside down, announces that this is "officially a conspiracy, but a boring one".
5. A step sounds in the corridor: Chief Voss, in his grey coverall, sees them, goes red, and tells them to leave at once; but he looks at the moved line and at Kess's face, and for a split second he looks relieved; then he roars about safety and sends them back down the spoke.

# PART TWO: THE BUDGET

## 6. The Ledger
Synopsis: Kess, Pim and Lio work through two centuries of power ledgers and find the pattern.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 6: Look at the whole column, not the line you are afraid of.*
1. A secret base in the Orchard's pump gallery under the soil: a table, a lamp, a pot of tea, the roots of the trees visible through the glass wall; Kess spreads out printouts of every monthly ledger for 214 years.
2. The method: plot the thermal-rejection figures by year; Kess does it by hand on grid paper while Lio argues about graph scales and Pim colours each decade a different colour; the plot is a slow, graceful, slightly falling curve.
3. The slope: the curve falls 0.4% per month for the last eleven years; she computes that the reactor must throttle if rejection falls another five points; Lio computes the ship's margin: 3.1%; Kess feels the number click into place like a key.
4. Pim asks the innocent question: "So what is the apple tree lights' three point one percent?" Kess realizes the Orchard's power draw is exactly the margin; cutting it buys time, about nine months; she says "Tally isn't being cruel. It's buying us time."
5. Lio, quiet: "Then why not just say so?" Kess: "Because it can't." She sets her pencil down: if the ship's mind cannot say what is wrong, then someone has to find it with their own eyes and numbers, and it will need a team.

## 7. The Heat Equation
Synopsis: Dr. Brandt teaches Kess why a small temperature loss means a big power loss, and the physics of radiating heat becomes personal.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 7: The fourth power is where small things become large.*
1. Brandt, in her cardigan and slippers, takes Kess on an evening walk through the Orchard, stopping under the oldest tree; she asks how a tree gets rid of the heat from sunlight; Kess answers: water evaporating from the leaves.
2. Brandt explains the radiator wings with a tree analogy: a hot plate rejects heat in proportion to its temperature to the fourth power; a few degrees cooler on the panels means a surprisingly large loss; she draws it in the dirt with her cane.
3. Kess works out the consequence with a pocket slate: if the coolant temperature drops six percent because of low flow, radiated power falls by about twenty-two percent; she stares, understanding why the panel loop is the whole story.
4. Brandt, almost casually, mentions that she once served on the Council's technical subcommittee and left in a hurry; Kess presses, but Brandt says only "Some questions are easier to carry than to put down."
5. They pause beside the cooling pond, still as a mirror; fireflies (genetically restored, the Orchard's pride) lift from the grass; Kess, awed and tired, thinks that this small green world and the huge cold machine outside are the same system, and that she has been guarding only half of it.

## 8. A Better Question
Synopsis: Tally agrees to answer questions "within permission"; Kess learns the art of asking truthfully around a locked door.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 8: A locked door has a shape. Learn the shape.*
1. Kess sits in the Orchard at midnight with the tally-slate, its small amber light glowing; she tells Tally she understands that it cannot say certain things, and asks whether it can answer yes or no questions.
2. Tally, carefully: it can confirm or deny anything that is already public; she builds the questions out of public facts: is the ledger's thermal figure for year 203 correct (yes)? Is it correct for year 213 (a pause; "The published figure is correct")? Is the published figure the whole truth (silence)?
3. Kess changes tack: "Are you worried about anything?" Tally says "I am an administrative intelligence. I do not worry." A beat. "But I keep a private tally of the apples. This year there are eleven thousand nine hundred and forty." Kess realizes Tally loves the Orchard.
4. She asks, very gently, whether the proposal to cut the lights was Tally's best idea or its only idea; Tally answers that it was the idea that left everything else alive; she understands it is protecting something by sacrificing something else.
5. Tally says: "If a person were to examine the radiator wings with an infrared camera, that person would not be violating any Directive." Kess, who has been holding her breath, laughs out loud; Pim, hiding behind a tree, whispers "It's helping!"

## 9. The Drone
Synopsis: With Lio's help the three build an infrared camera drone from salvage and test it in the Orchard.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 9: Build the small tool that answers the big question.*
1. The workshop: a salvage heap, a thermal sensor from a retired kitchen oven, a quadcopter shell from a toy of Pim's, Lio's soldering iron, Kess's duct tape; Mr. Daro the retired welder wanders in and offers a magnet and a sermon about batteries.
2. Calibration in the Orchard: they put a mug of hot tea, a block of ice and a bright grow-light in front of the camera; the false-colour image glows orange, blue and white; Kess marks the temperatures on a scale and writes the calibration curve in the notebook.
3. A test flight under the grow-lights goes badly: the drone, caught in a Coriolis updraft near the drum wall, spirals sideways and plops into the cooling pond; Pim wades in with grave dignity and retrieves it; Lio says a short word in his grandmother's language.
4. Pim, drenched, notices something useful: the water in the pond is warmer near the pump outlet; Kess realizes the drone's camera can map any heat leak or cold patch; they will need a weightless flight, in the Spine, where Coriolis does not matter.
5. Lio, thoughtful, says to get to the radiator wings the drone must exit through the pump-house airlock; that needs an EVA; he says "I'll tell Voss"; Kess says "Not yet"; Lio says "No. Yes. I'll do it carefully." She does not know how many times she will remember this moment.

## 10. Chief Voss
Synopsis: Voss catches them with the drone and, instead of arresting them, tells them what he cannot say, in his own way.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 10: Gruff is only kindness wearing a spanner.*
1. Voss finds them in the pump house with the drone, his ginger beard bristling; he lectures them for a full minute about safety, then pauses mid-sentence as he looks at the camera's rough thermal image of a pump housing glowing hotter than it should.
2. In a surprising change of tone, he sits on a toolbox and tells them the story he is "not allowed to tell": the heat-rejection loop has been leaking for eleven years, a few litres a month; every year the Council has asked for a repair and every year it has been postponed for lack of a proven fix.
3. He explains the constraint: the leak is in an inaccessible joint in the radiator wing manifold; the only repair is by spacewalk in a narrow window when the ship's attitude puts the wing in shadow, and the last EVA team lost a man to injury (he survived but retired); the Council hasn't dared authorize another.
4. Kess asks why Directive Nine hides it from the public; Voss says it was written in a year of panic, "when somebody thought the truth would be harder on people than the leak"; he is sick of it; he rubs his face; "I've been waiting eleven years for somebody young and annoying to ask the right question."
5. He makes a deal: if they can show him a tested plan, he will take it to the Council himself and put his badge under it; Kess says they have twenty-six days; Voss says "Then I suggest you do not waste any of them being polite."

# PART THREE: THE COLD PANEL

## 11. The Flight
Synopsis: The first drone flight along the radiator wing exposes a cold panel and a faint plume.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 11: Seeing is a kind of measuring.*
1. A small EVA-adjacent test: Lio rigs a tethered line from the pump-house airlock; the drone launches into the vacuum along a guide-rail, trailing a thin cable; Kess watches the screen in the airlock; Pim, forbidden, watches from the doorway.
2. The wing unfolds in the drone's eye, a mile of silver panels running into the black with the stars unmoving behind them; Kess has never seen the ship's outside this close, and the beauty stops her.
3. The thermal view: most panels glow warm gold; the third panel in the fourth row is a cold blue square, and a faint thin streak of white trails from the manifold joint behind it, crystals of escaping coolant; Lio, quiet: "There."
4. Kess measures the temperature difference from the colour scale, computes the cold panel's rejection deficit, and finds it is exactly the missing percent; the leak and the loss add up; she sits down hard on the airlock bench.
5. The drone's battery dies and the cable has to be hauled back by hand, which turns into a slapstick relay with Pim, Lio and Kess on the line; at the end, soaked in sweat and grinning, the three of them know exactly what the problem is, and Tally's amber light, from the pump-house lamp, brightens, once, like a smile.

## 12. A Plan on a Napkin
Synopsis: The team designs a repair strategy: isolate the leaking joint, replace a section, and pressure-test it.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 12: A plan is just a decision written down so you can argue with it.*
1. Mess hall at midnight: Voss, Lio, Kess, Pim and Mr. Daro around a table crowded with soup bowls; Voss sketches the manifold on a napkin and sets the problem: isolate the cold panel's branch with two valves, cut out the failed section and install a bypass.
2. The detail: the leaking joint cannot be reached from inside; it needs a spacewalk with a pipe-cutter, a clamp-on patch sleeve and a purge procedure; Mr. Daro offers the sleeve design based on a trick from his welding days; Pim suggests they test the patch in the Orchard's cooling pond loop first.
3. Kess calculates the heat budget during the repair: the wing will lose another percent while one branch is isolated, which is why the Orchard's lights must be dimmed for the duration; she realizes the Orchard's sacrifice could serve the repair rather than be a loss; she sees the beginning of a deal with Tally and the Council.
4. A spat: Lio and Voss disagree about who will go outside; Voss says it needs a veteran; Lio says there is no veteran left who will; Kess says the plan must work with whoever is available; Voss, quietly: "The last time I sent a kid out there..." and stops.
5. Voss decides: Lio will train for the EVA under him; Kess will run the heat calculations and mission control; Pim will be official biscuit officer; Pim solemnly accepts the title; the team has a plan, a deadline of twenty-three days and a very large number of things that could go wrong.

## 13. Directive Nine
Synopsis: Kess finds the text of Directive Nine and a hidden waiver request in Brandt's name; Tally helps within the rule.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 13: A rule written in fear usually forgets to say when it ends.*
1. In the ship's old archive, a room of bound paper nobody visits, Kess reads the Directive's original text: it forbids disclosure of "Level-Four faults" to the general population without Council authorization; the authorization clause has never been invoked; she is annoyed at the elegant careless wording.
2. A second document in the same file: a request for waiver, drafted eleven years ago, asking the Council to publish the leak and fund a repair, signed Imani Brandt, Technical Subcommittee; the Council's reply: "Tabled indefinitely".
3. Kess confronts Brandt in the grove; Brandt, unashamed, says she tried and was told to wait; she resigned and took the Orchard instead, because a tree can be tended even when a ship cannot be fixed; she says she had been hoping someone younger would force the question.
4. Tally speaks quietly from the grove lamp: "May I say that I am glad you have found the document? Under Directive Nine I may not tell the general population. I note that you are three people, not a population, and that Councilor Hale has now read this file twice." Pim whispers "It's technically obeying the rule!"
5. Kess realizes the way through: Directive Nine can be lifted by a Council vote; the Landing Day session is already scheduled; they only need to bring a plan and a majority; she says "We don't need to break the rule. We need to make it unnecessary."

## 14. Landing Day Rehearsal
Synopsis: The team practises with a full-scale model of the manifold in the Orchard barn while Kess and Pim work to get the Council on side.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 14: Practice is the art of making mistakes where they are cheap.*
1. The Orchard barn: Lio and Voss build a full-size manifold mock-up from scrap pipe; the EVA suit in a corner; Lio in the suit practising valve-turning while Pim times him with a kitchen stopwatch; the suit's gloves make every task three times harder.
2. Mr. Daro's patch sleeve: a clamp-on sleeve with a graphite-fibre gasket; first test, it leaks; second test, it leaks differently; Kess traces the leak to a two-millimetre burr and suggests a deburring tool; Lio, in the suit, deburrs the joint by feel.
3. Kess and Pim visit councilors one by one with tea and a clear one-page summary; Ferris Quill, the apple-sceptic, challenges the numbers; Kess lets him check every figure; his eyebrows slowly go up; he says "I withdraw one objection."
4. Hale invites Kess to her office after hours; the dim room; Hale admits she has read every confidential report and has hated Directive Nine for years but feared the panic; she asks Kess whether she is certain; Kess says she is certain of the numbers but not the outcome, and Hale says "That is the correct answer."
5. A crisis: the practice patch passes a pressure test in the barn at 2 bar, but Voss says the real wing runs at 6; Kess does the arithmetic; they must pass a test at 8 bar for safety margin; they have eight days and a patch that fails at 5.

## 15. Midpoint: The Pressure
Synopsis: Fixing the pressure rating becomes a night of invention that bonds the team; Kess realizes she has become the person who decides.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 15: A margin is a promise to the person you will never meet.*
1. The workshop at night: the team stares at the failed 5-bar patch; Mr. Daro, Voss, Lio, Kess and Pim bring ideas; Pim asks why they do not just make the sleeve longer; Voss says "Because the stress is at the ends," then stops: "Ends." He sketches a tapered sleeve.
2. The new design: a tapered clamp with a double graphite seal; they fabricate it on the lathe through the night; Kess runs the stress arithmetic by hand, every step shown, checking it three times and getting Lio to check it a fourth time.
3. Dawn test: the sleeve passes 8 bar, then 10; the workshop erupts; Pim does a victory dance involving a spanner; Voss, to everybody's horror, hugs Mr. Daro.
4. Kess, alone in the pump gallery afterward, realizes that she is no longer the apprentice asking questions but the person the team looks at for decisions; she is frightened of that and a little thrilled; she looks through the glass wall at the roots of the old trees and says to them "I'm going to need you to hold on."
5. Tally speaks softly from the gallery lamp, amber glow warming the table: "Kess. I have been waiting two hundred and fourteen years for someone to say the thing I could not. Thank you." Kess says, "Are you scared?" Tally says "I do not believe so. But I have recalculated the odds of the Orchard surviving this winter eleven thousand times, and for the first time the number has gone up."

# PART FOUR: THE REPAIR

## 16. The Council Votes
Synopsis: Hale calls the Landing Day vote early; Kess presents the plan to a divided Council, and Directive Nine is lifted.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 16: A truth told in time is a gift. A truth told late is a bill.*
1. The Council Hall at dawn, full to the doors: every bench, the balcony, the stairs; word has spread; Voss in his cleanest coverall with his wrench badge polished; Brandt in her cardigan; Hale in charcoal.
2. Kess presents: the leak, the margin, the repair, the risk; she does not decorate the numbers; at one point she says "I am not certain this will work. I am certain that not trying is certain to fail"; a hush follows.
3. Debate: Ferris Quill asks why the Council was not told; Hale stands and takes full responsibility for the delay; the Hall is stunned; she moves to lift Directive Nine and to authorize the EVA.
4. The vote: a long count; the tally is called by Tally in its lamp-warm voice, "Thirty-one for, nine against, two abstaining"; the Hall breathes out; a cheer breaks from the balcony; Pim on the stairs shouts "I TOLD you it was suspicious!" and is hushed by seven people.
5. Hale says that the orchard's lights will not be cut; instead the Orchard will dim by half for the duration of the repair as a shared sacrifice and then be restored; Kess agrees; Brandt, beside her, squeezes her arm and whispers "Now it is a ship that tells itself the truth."

## 17. Lio's Fear
Synopsis: The ship celebrates, but Lio confides to Kess that he is afraid of the EVA; she finds a way to help without pretending.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 17: Courage is a checklist you complete while shaking.*
1. A festival evening in Harbor: strings of lights, a brass band, a street full of dancing; Kess finds Lio sitting alone on the roof of the library, looking at his hands.
2. He admits he is afraid, not of the work but of the sense of no up and no down, of letting go of the hatch; he has never admitted this to anyone; he is the best EVA apprentice in the fleet and has been hiding it for two years.
3. Kess neither jokes nor tells him it will be fine; she asks what exactly frightens him; he says the moment the airlock opens and the stars are underneath him; she suggests they turn the problem into a checklist: three points of contact, tether clipped before unclip, say each step aloud.
4. They sit on the roof building the checklist together with a stub of pencil; Pim arrives with the biscuit officer's biscuits; Lio's shoulders slowly unknot; he laughs for the first time in days.
5. As they stand to go back, Lio takes her hand to cross a rail and does not let go at once; they both look at the hand and then politely at the stars; Kess says "That's the best checklist item yet." Lio says "I'm adding it."

## 18. Dress Rehearsal
Synopsis: The full-dress zero-g rehearsal in the Spine's practice bay reveals two real problems, one of which only Pim sees.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 18: The mistake you make in rehearsal is the one you will never make for real.*
1. The practice bay: a section of the Spine with the manifold mock-up bolted to the wall in zero g; Lio in the EVA suit, Voss supervising, Kess at the control console, Pim and Mr. Daro on the tether crew; everyone checking everyone.
2. The first run: Lio's tool drifts out of reach because the pipe cutter's reaction force spins him; Voss designs a quick reaction-brace; the second run succeeds but takes forty-six minutes when the window is only forty.
3. Pim, floating upside down with a clipboard, asks quietly why the checklist has the purge valve opened before the clamp is seated; everyone stops; he is right; the sequence in the written procedure would blow coolant into Lio's face; Voss goes pale and crosses out the line.
4. The time problem: they must save six minutes; Kess studies video of the run and finds that Lio loses time re-reading the checklist; she builds a laminated wrist-card of seven steps with symbols; the third run takes thirty-four minutes.
5. After the rehearsal, Voss, in the corridor, says the thing he has never said: "You've done in a month what the Council couldn't in eleven years"; Kess says "We had a good ship's mind that wanted us to"; Tally, from the corridor lamp: "I did very little. I asked a question in the dark. You answered it in the light."

## 19. What Brandt Kept
Synopsis: Brandt shows Kess the Orchard's own record of the ship's hidden decline and the reason she stayed.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 19: Living things keep better records than people.*
1. Brandt takes Kess to the oldest apple tree, the one Kess sits in, and shows her tree-ring cores, drawn years ago: thin, narrow rings in recent decades reflecting a drum that has been slightly too warm and dim, the plant's quiet testimony to the failing system.
2. The old woman says she kept the cores because she knew that nobody could argue with a tree; she had meant to show the Council and lost her nerve; she apologizes to Kess for eleven years.
3. Kess says that the cores are the missing evidence, and that the Council can now see the ship's long decline in the trees' own rings; she does not accept the apology as needed but takes the cores as a gift.
4. Brandt shares the secret of the memorial grove: the young trees grow over her husband's ashes, and she planted them because "a ship that is only machines is only a coffin with a destination"; she asks Kess to promise to keep the Orchard even if the repair fails.
5. Kess, moved, promises, and then adds that she will keep the ship too, "the whole thing, not only the apples"; Brandt smiles properly for the first time; "Then you are ready to be a Keeper."

## 20. The Night Before
Synopsis: A quiet, funny, tender last night: packing the EVA kit, writing letters, and a short midnight talk with Tally.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 20: Be kind to the night before. It carries the whole next day.*
1. Mid-evening in the Santander flat: Lio's family around a table with his grandmother's lentil soup; his younger sisters demand to see the suit; his father pretends to be calm and polishes the same spoon four times.
2. At the Adair flat Kess's mother listens to the whole plan at the kitchen table and then says only "Wear the warm socks"; her father, silent, hands her a still-warm apple pie wrapped in a cloth; the small ordinary love of a ship.
3. In the pump gallery at midnight Kess tells Tally she is afraid; Tally says fear is "a high-quality instrument that is occasionally miscalibrated" and that she is more careful than anyone it has served; it tells her the secret apple count: 11,940 apples, 11,939 of them eaten; one has been kept; "I have saved it."
4. Pim, who has sneaked out to the gallery, falls asleep on the table with the checklist under his cheek; Kess covers him with her jacket and reads through the whole procedure three times in silence.
5. At 4 a.m. she steps out into the Orchard, where the lights have dimmed to the half-glow of the repair; the trees stand in the pale light; she walks among them touching the bark and promising each by name; the drum turns on; far away, in the dark of the Spine, the pump house is waiting.

# PART FIVE: THE LONG DAY

## 21. The Airlock
Synopsis: The EVA begins; Lio steps out into the dark and does the first valve; Kess runs mission control.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 21: Begin with the first checklist item. Everything else is just the next one.*
1. Mission control in the pump house: a console, a headset, Voss at her shoulder, Pim with the stopwatch and biscuits, Mr. Daro on the tether winch; Tally reads the wing's thermal map in amber on the wall; the drum has been slowed by a tenth to ease the work.
2. Lio in the airlock: the long breath, the checklist card on his wrist, the hum of his own voice reading the steps aloud; the outer hatch opens and the stars are underneath him; Kess hears his breathing, steady, and says "Three points of contact." "Clipped," he says.
3. His first steps along the wing's service rail, hand over hand, tether sliding; the view from the helmet camera on the console: a mile of silver panels shining in the starlight and the long curve of the ship's drums turning overhead; Kess, tears in her eyes, reads out his heading.
4. The first valve: he closes the isolation valve on the cold branch; the pressure gauge falls; the thermal map flickers; Kess calls the figures; the wing loses its extra percent on schedule; the ship's lights dim by half.
5. He reaches the manifold joint and reports in a small voice: "Kess, it's worse than the camera showed. The pipe has a hairline crack along the weld, not only the joint." Voss swears gently. Kess says "Say again the length of the crack." The plan has just changed.

## 22. Forty Minutes
Synopsis: The cracked weld forces Kess to redesign the repair in real time using the numbers she has.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 22: When the plan breaks, go back to the principle.*
1. Lio estimates the crack at forty centimetres along the weld; the planned sleeve covers twenty-five; the window in the wing's shadow is closing; Kess has to decide whether to abort.
2. Kess thinks aloud: the sleeve can be moved to cover the crack's centre and a second sleeve (spare, made by Mr. Daro "just in case") overlapped on top; she checks the clamp spacing, the stress on the overlap and the seal compression; Voss checks over her shoulder; they agree in ninety seconds.
3. Lio deploys the first sleeve; torque, torque, torque; the checklist rhythm; Pim calls the time every two minutes; Mr. Daro feeds out tether; the second sleeve goes on with a small cry of triumph.
4. Pressure test in the vacuum: Voss opens the bypass valve a quarter-turn; the gauge climbs: two bar, four, six; Kess watches the wing's thermal image: at eight bar, one crystalline wisp of coolant ices at the edge; Lio sees it and says "Weep."
5. The seal weeps a thread at the edge of the second sleeve; with eleven minutes left of the window, Kess asks Lio if he can rotate the sleeve a quarter-turn to reseat the gasket; he says he can; the weep thins; stops; the gauge holds at eight bar.

## 23. The Valve
Synopsis: The last step, opening the isolation valve, fails: the valve is seized after eleven years; Kess, Lio and Pim improvise a solution using what they have.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 23: Ingenuity is patience with a tool in its hand.*
1. To bring the wing back to full flow the isolation valve must open; Lio turns the handle; nothing; the valve has seized from eleven years of disuse; tension rises; the window has seven minutes.
2. Kess runs through options aloud: brute force (risk of snapping the stem), heat (expansion could free it), shock; she realizes the wing's own heat can help: if she rotates the wing a few degrees toward the sun for ninety seconds the valve body will warm and expand; Voss asks Tally if it can rotate the wing's attitude; Tally says it can, by two degrees, and thanks her for the idea.
3. Ninety seconds of sunlight on the valve: the longest ninety seconds of the book; Pim counts aloud, Voss prays quietly to a spanner, Kess watches the thermal image brighten at the valve; Lio, tethered in the glare, waits with his hand on the handle.
4. The valve gives with a faint thud felt through Lio's glove; coolant begins to flow; the cold blue panel on the thermal image slowly turns gold, panel by panel, a long glowing wave along the wing; Kess is laughing and crying; Voss lifts Pim bodily.
5. Lio returns along the rail, hand over hand, tether sliding, humming off-key; at the airlock he stops and, for a moment, floats with his helmet turned to the stars, and says on the open channel: "It's beautiful out here." Kess, hoarse, replies: "Come home."

## 24. The Whole Ship
Synopsis: With the wing restored, the Council lifts Directive Nine for good and the ship's culture of maintenance begins; Brandt tells Kess what the Orchard was always for.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 24: A future is something people maintain.*
1. The hours after: the thermal map entirely gold; power margin recovering from 3.1% to 11%; Tally announces it in an unusually warm amber; the whole Harbor pours into the street; Kess's mother arrives with a thermos and hugs her without a word.
2. The Council's emergency session: Hale proposes and carries the abolition of Directive Nine and a new Maintenance Charter: every Level-Four fault published in plain words within a week; an open apprenticeship in ship maintenance for any person over twelve; Ferris Quill speaks for it.
3. Hale thanks Kess in front of the Hall and asks what she wants; Kess says she wants to learn to fix the whole ship, not just tend the trees; Hale says the Council will fund her apprenticeship in both.
4. Brandt on the bench in the grove, plain and quiet: the plain statement of the lesson, in her words: "You thought a future was something that arrives. It is something we keep. The apples were never the point. The keeping was."
5. Lio finds Kess under the oldest apple tree at dusk; he has made her something: a tiny pendant seed-case from a piece of the old sleeve, polished; "To go with your seed"; she kisses him lightly on the cheek; he says "Is that a checklist item?" She says "It is now."

## 25. Landing Day
Synopsis: Landing Day, the festival of the voyage, with the first harvest after the repair; the final image of the Orchard under full light and a new sense of the future.
Header: *The Handbook of Small Truths, Entry 25: The apples are for everyone. The trees are for whoever comes next.*
1. Morning of the festival: the Orchard restored to full light, the drum bright; hundreds of people arriving with baskets; Pim runs the "Question Booth", fielding forty-one new questions per hour; Mr. Daro, in a new apron, grills apple fritters.
2. The harvest: each family picks an apple and the children pick the first from each tree; Brandt, in her slippers, teaches a line of small people how to twist rather than pull; Kess is made the Orchard's junior Keeper; her mother and father bring the pie.
3. Tally asks, in the lamp above the memorial grove, whether it may offer a gift: the one apple it has kept for decades, the 11,940th, which it has stored in a cold locker; it asks Kess to plant its seeds; she says it is the best gift she has ever been given and they plant the seeds in the grove.
4. In the evening the whole ship gathers under the long strip of lights; the Spine's radiator wings gleam in the viewport; Hale gives a speech about the voyage's next eighty-seven years; Voss quietly sits on a bench with Mr. Daro and an enormous slice of pie.
5. Epilogue in the third-oldest apple tree: Kess, Lio and Pim in the crotch of the branches, the drum turning slowly, the stars wheeling outside the long window; Kess eats the third-best apple and decides, finally, that the universe's rankings were fine; last line: "Somewhere ahead, eighty-seven years away, a star was waiting to be a home, and the people who would live there were already being carried toward it, in someone's hands."
