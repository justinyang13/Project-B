# JUNO VALE AND THE TIDE THAT FORGOT — story bible

Middle-grade adventure (ages 10-12). Warm, funny, a little spooky, a little sad, hopeful ending. Third person past tense, close on Juno. Kid-appropriate: no gore, no romance, peril is gentle. Humor comes from Barnacle the crab and Juno's and Teo's banter. Vocabulary: rich but readable for age 10-12; short paragraphs; lots of dialogue; every page ends on a small hook or a nice beat.

## Setting
Saltmarsh Harbor: a small fishing town on a crescent bay. For eleven months the tide has not come in. Boats sit tilted in grey mud, the harbor bell hangs silent, gulls are grumpy, the fish market smells worse than ever. At the tip of the bay stands the Old Lighthouse, which holds the Tide Clock (a huge brass clockwork that pulls the sea in and out like breathing).

## Main characters (keep names, looks, and traits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Juno Vale**, 11. Curly dark-brown hair in two puffs, freckles, always wears a bright yellow raincoat. Curious, stubborn, talks to herself when nervous, hates being the new kid. Misses her Grandma Ivy. Secret fear: that she is not brave, only loud.
- **Grandpa Ozzie (Osgood Vale)**, 70s. Tall, bushy white beard, blue knitted hat, walks with a driftwood cane, bad left knee. Gentle, forgetful, tells terrible fish jokes. Runs the closed-down bait shop under the house. Grandma Ivy's husband.
- **Grandma Ivy Vale** (died a year ago, before the story). The last Tide Keeper. Appears only in memories, her Tide Keeper's Log (a green cloth notebook with a brass clasp), and her spiral shell whistle. Her advice: "The sea doesn't answer the loud. It answers the listening."
- **Teo Marchetti**, 12. Round glasses, messy black hair, a canvas tool belt, a green cap. Fixes anything, over-explains, secretly scared of deep water because he cannot swim well. Sarcastic but loyal. Lives above his family's bakery.
- **Biscuit**, Grandpa's fat orange cat with a chewed left ear. Does not talk (only Juno hears sea creatures, not land animals). Sleeps in the worst places.
- **Barnacle**, a grumpy, dramatic hermit crab who lives in a chipped blue teapot shell. Only Juno (holding the shell whistle) can hear him. Proud, complains constantly, secretly brave. Says "Honestly!" a lot. Was Grandma Ivy's friend.
- **Old Fathom**, an enormous, ancient sea turtle with moss on his shell and a slow, kind, rumbling voice. Stranded on the mudflats. Speaks in riddles when tired.
- **Queen Nautilia**, a giant nautilus who rules the Deep Court under the bay. Elegant, formal, proud, worried.
- **Mr. Cornelius Grubb**, 50s, tall, thin, shiny grey suit, tiny mustache, drives a long silver car. A property developer who wants to buy the harbor cheaply and build a parking lot and mini-golf resort. Not a monster: greedy and impatient, and he is the one who took the Moon Gear from the lighthouse (he thought it was a valuable antique clock part, and now displays it in his office). He does not know what it does. He softens at the end.
- **Mayor Pruitt**, jolly, tired, always eating a doughnut; **Mrs. Marchetti**, Teo's mom the baker.

## Family
Juno's mother (call her "Mom", first name Lena) is Grandpa Ozzie's daughter and Grandma Ivy's daughter. Juno's father is not in the story (never mention him). Mom works far away and must leave the day after arrival for a nine-day work trip; Juno stays with Grandpa Ozzie. The house is a tall narrow house above the closed bait shop on the harbor road; Juno's bedroom is the attic room under the roof.

## Secrets (do not reveal early)
- Grandpa Ozzie does NOT know the shell whistle is magic and does not know about the Tide Clock's keys; he only misses Ivy and says the sea has been sad. The whistle and log stay hidden in the attic tin box until chapter 2. Nobody in town knows the tide is magical, except Ivy once did. Juno's mom does not know about any magic and is away from the day after arrival until the last chapter.
- Nobody but Juno can hear Barnacle, Old Fathom or Queen Nautilia.

## Magic rules
- Only a Tide Keeper (someone holding the spiral shell whistle who truly listens) can hear the sea creatures.
- The Tide Clock needs its **Moon Gear** plus three **Tide Keys** to run: the **Moon Pearl**, the **Salt Compass**, and the **Storm Bell**. Grandma Ivy hid the keys in three places so no one could stop the tide by stealing them all.
- Winding the Clock must be done by a Tide Keeper at the top of the lighthouse at the turn of the highest moon-night ("Grand Low", the night of the full moon, in nine days).
- Time frame of the story: nine days, late summer, the week before school starts.

## Visual style (for illustrations)
Soft watercolor and ink children's book illustration, warm coastal colors, whimsical, gentle lighting, cream paper background vignette.

## Chapter outline (20 chapters, 5 pages each = 100 pages)
PART ONE: THE MUD
1. **The Town That Stopped Breathing.** Rainy arrival by car with Juno's mom (who must leave next day for a work trip; she'll be back in nine days). Boats stuck in mud, silent bell. Meeting Grandpa Ozzie, Biscuit. Juno feels like a suitcase nobody unpacked.
2. **The Attic.** Juno's room under the roof. Finds Grandma Ivy's tin box: the green Tide Keeper's Log and the spiral shell whistle. Memory of Grandma. First short note in the log: "When the whistle is blown by someone listening, the sea will answer."
3. **Barnacle.** Next morning Juno blows the whistle on the muddy shore and hears a furious little voice from a blue teapot: Barnacle. He explains the tide stopped when Ivy died and no Keeper wound the Clock. Nine days to the Grand Low, or the tide never returns and the sea forgets the bay.
4. **Teo Marchetti.** Juno tries to buy help at the bakery, meets Teo, who fixes a broken bicycle and does not believe her until Barnacle insults him and Teo hears... nothing; so Juno proves it by making Barnacle spell out a secret (Teo's private fear of water) with pebbles. Teo joins, reluctantly.
5. **Mr. Grubb Makes an Offer.** Town meeting in the fish market. Grubb offers to buy the harbor land; Mayor Pruitt is tempted; Grandpa Ozzie stands up but forgets what he wanted to say. Juno notices a shiny brass gear tooth caught in Grubb's coat pocket.
PART TWO: THE THREE KEYS
6. **What the Log Said.** Kids read the log by flashlight: the riddles for the three keys and the warning about the Moon Gear. Riddle 1 (Moon Pearl): "Where the tide goes to sleep, look for what the moon keeps." Barnacle translates old sailor's words, badly.
7. **The Old Lighthouse.** They climb the lighthouse: the huge Tide Clock is silent, its center missing the Moon Gear. Spooky dark, echo, bats (harmless). Find a note in Ivy's handwriting; someone took the gear.
8. **Old Fathom.** On the mudflats they find the giant turtle stranded, dry and sad. They pour buckets of water on him (comic effort with the whole bakery's help, secretly). He tells the story of Ivy and the first Tide Keepers, and gives the first clue: the Moon Pearl sleeps in the wreck of the "Dolly Marigold".
9. **The Wreck of the Dolly Marigold.** In the mud, the old wrecked ship. Teo must crawl inside; he fights his fear. A jellyfish-free puzzle: the ship's wheel opens a hidden box. They find the Moon Pearl. It glows when Juno hums.
10. **The Silver Car.** Grubb catches them at the wreck, is kind on the surface, asks about the "pretty pearl", offers Juno money. She refuses. Grubb's car has a small brass tooth-shaped mark on the door. Teo suspects Grubb. Biscuit gets stuck in the silver car's trunk (comic).
11. **The Deep Court.** Juno and Barnacle are summoned by Queen Nautilia to a tidepool gateway; the Deep Court shows the sea shrinking away. The Queen doubts a child can be a Keeper. She gives a test: listen to the Court's silence and name what is missing. Juno answers: the moon's song. The Queen gives the second clue (Salt Compass is in the bell-buoy at Gull Rock).
PART THREE: THE STORM
12. **Gull Rock.** Boat trip in Grandpa's rowboat dragged over the mud by Teo's dad's cart; then a small stretch of shallow water. Gulls and a fierce gull named Admiral Squawk (only Juno hears? No, gulls are land-air animals, no talking; just noisy and comic). Teo's fear of water. They find the Salt Compass in the bell-buoy, but Teo drops it into a crack and Juno risks the wet climb.
13. **Grandpa Forgets.** Juno comes home hopeful; Grandpa forgets who she is for a minute and calls her Ivy. Devastated. Grandpa admits he stopped the shop and let the town slide when Ivy died. Juno's fear: she is not brave, only loud. Sad but warm.
14. **Grubb's Office.** Juno and Teo sneak into Grubb's rented office above the hardware store to look for the Moon Gear (Barnacle hidden in a bag, complaining). They see the gear in a glass case. Grubb comes back early; they hide in a cupboard; overhear that Grubb plans to buy the harbor at Friday's council vote. He does not know what the gear is.
15. **The Falling-Out.** Caught (Grubb thinks they are lost children, but calls the Mayor). Argument between Juno and Teo: Teo blames her impulsiveness, she blames his fear. Teo leaves. Barnacle tells Juno she is being quiet at last, and it is the first time she hears the sea properly.
16. **The Storm Bell.** A storm arrives from the sea (the first real weather in a year; the town is terrified). Juno goes alone to the last hiding place (the drowned church bell in the reef, under the old boathouse at the stone pier), guided by Barnacle. She is stuck; the tide-less storm is strange. Teo appears in a rowing boat, afraid, but coming.
PART FOUR: THE GRAND LOW
17. **Two Keepers.** Together they retrieve the Storm Bell. Teo tells the story of his fear; Juno tells hers. The storm rings the bell for the first time in a year: the town wakes and comes down to the pier to see. Grubb watches from his silver car.
18. **Borrowed Time.** The last day: get the Moon Gear from Grubb. Juno goes to his car (alone, letting Teo stay with the bell). She doesn't shout: she listens. Grubb admits his own childhood on a dried-up lake and his fear of being poor. She shows him what the gear does with the shell whistle and the sea's voice (the Deep Court speaks through a tidepool where he can hear). He gives the gear back. Not fully redeemed, but changing.
19. **The Grand Low.** Night of the full moon. Whole town climbs the lighthouse steps by lantern (Grandpa Ozzie leads with his cane, remembering). Juno sets the four pieces into the clock. It doesn't start. Panic. Grandma's log's last page: "You must say who you are." Juno says it out loud, the shell whistle sounds, and the Clock ticks.
20. **The Tide Comes Home.** The sea rolls back into the bay under the moon. Old Fathom swims free. Boats lift. The bell rings. Grubb, at the back, cancels the offer and buys a doughnut. Mom returns; Grandpa laughs. Juno writes the first new entry in the Tide Keeper's Log. Last line about listening and being home.
