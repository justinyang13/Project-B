# THE LAST RIVER BEARER: story bible

Grand quest fantasy with real water engineering, for readers 14-18 (High School shelf, author Yang, publisher Yang Ink). Setting: a fictional Chinese river-gorge country (Chinese names, terraces, gorges, towns, customs; the river is fictional). ART STYLE: **fantasy-real** (Project-I/wiki/fantasy-real.md), NOT anime; wide scenic pages allowed (see Layout). Born from Project-I/images/fantasy-real/126.png (a girl calls water in a misty gorge).

LOGLINE: The Clear River that feeds her valley has stopped. Wen Lan, 16, who can move any water she can touch, sets out to walk up the dying river to its source, two thousand kilometers away, and finds out who turned it off, and what her power costs.

MAIN GOAL: the reader finishes wanting to understand how rivers, floods, weirs and terraces work, to walk somewhere far on purpose, and to share what is held. Engineering is the hero (the "ledger" of the magic is real hydraulics). Every turn is solved by measurement, craft and cleverness shown in scene, never lectured.

LESSON: **A river belongs to everyone who drinks downstream.** Power is a debt owed to the people below you; what you take, someone else gives up. One earned plain statement near the end (Auntie Mo, ch24; Wen, ch25).

## CULTURAL HANDLING (user: "Chinese river-gorge country")
- Fictional realm, fictional Clear River (the Qing), fictional towns; Chinese-inspired names (pinyin) used lightly and consistently; no invented "Chinese words" beyond names and a few real, common ones used correctly: ayi/Auntie, shifu (master/teacher), baba/mama (parents), nainai (paternal grandmother). Keep English for everything else.
- Verified real texture to use (sources: Wikipedia and visit/UNESCO-type pages, 2026-10-11): (1) **Trackers (qianfu):** in the Yangtze Three Gorges men hauled boats up rapids with bamboo hawsers, sometimes 100 or more on a rope; the trade is now tourism. (2) **Dujiangyan (Li Bing, about 256 BC):** the Min River is split without a dam by a "fish-mouth" levee, a "flying sand" spillway and a "bottleneck" channel; still working; no dam needed. A famous maxim attributed to it: dig the channel deep, keep the weir low. (3) **Yuanyang/Hani rice terraces (Yunnan, over 1,300 years old, UNESCO):** forest on the mountain top holds water, channels carry it down to terraces, slopes 15-75 degrees, from about 100 m to 2,000 m. (4) **Yellow River silt:** loess makes the river yellow; silt raises the riverbed so the river can run higher than the land ("hanging river"; at Kaifeng about ten meters above the ground), forcing ever higher dikes; floods have been among history's deadliest. (5) **Plateau headwaters:** above 4,000 m, cold, glaciers and wetlands shrinking (Yangtze source wetlands down about 29 percent), glacier-fed sources. (6) **Waterwheels** (Lanzhou-style giant wooden wheels lifting river water to fields), bamboo pipes, stone weirs, terraces, tea, mist.
- Do NOT claim real religion/folklore details beyond: river temples, incense, lanterns at festivals, a river spirit tradition in general terms. No invented rites.

## Reading level and voice
SAT-level prose (precise, varied vocabulary: tenacity, meticulous, equitable, laconic, inexorable, sediment, torrent, ephemeral, deference, tenuous, culminate, resilient, perfunctory, incremental, ostensibly), longer well-built sentences, dry wit, interior thought. THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, close on Wen. Awe in the landscapes, tension in the journey; nothing graphic: peril is weather, rapids, cold, thirst, deadlines, betrayal without violence. Light clean romance at most a held hand. No em dashes.
Voice sample:
"*The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 1: Water is never lost. It is only somewhere else.*

On the morning the river did not come, Wen Lan went down to Hollow Pool with two buckets and the strong conviction that the river had simply overslept."

## Wen's power (water only; the LEDGER)
- She can lift, steer, hold, freeze and release water she can touch or hear (within her own arm-span at first; later much farther, with practice and cost).
- She CANNOT make water. She can only move it; everything she lifts is borrowed from somewhere (a pond, a cloud, a puddle, a person's thirst: never from a person). Moving water uphill uses her strength like lifting it by hand: heavy loads leave her parched, cold, shaking, dizzy.
- Salt water burns her skin and mind (so the salt flats are a real trial). Fast, wide moves risk wave surges (shock waves) she must predict.
- THE HIDDEN COST (twist 5): each big use leaves a thin frost of ice/salt at her fingertips and in her hair (a white streak). She hides it. It is turning her slowly toward ice. Plain truth, shown gradually: white streak, cold hands, her breath fogging in summer. It stops only when she gives the water back (the ending).
- Engineering of the magic: what she can do equals what a real hydraulic engineer can do with channels, siphons, levees; the book teaches the real thing first, then she does it by hand.

## The world and the route (the realm of the Clear River, 2,000 km source to sea)
Eight landscapes, each a set piece (3 chapters each, roughly): 
1. **Hollow Pool and the village of Qingshi (near the delta):** her home; the pool dried; the first act of power.
2. **The Dry Delta:** mudflats, stranded boats, ghost harbor of Haikou-style (fictional) port town Silt Harbor; the first betrayal setup.
3. **The Terrace Plains:** a hundred villages and stepped rice terraces fighting over a trickle; forest on the mountain tops; her first lesson in sharing.
4. **The Gorge of Hanging Steps:** cliff gorge, trackers hauling boats up rapids on bamboo hawsers, stone stairs cut in the cliff, a flash flood she must hold.
5. **The White Basin (salt flats):** a dead lake, white crust, mirage; her power burns; a cistern town built on old lake-bed pipes.
6. **Floating Lantern Town (Fu Deng):** a market town of rafts and stilt houses on a wide slow reach; water merchants, who sell water by the cup and are paid by the Cistern Lord; the spy is exposed.
7. **The Glacier Valley and the Cloud Sea:** ice walls, a frozen river, a mountain pass above a sea of mist; her mother's ice.
8. **The Source (the Crater Lake at the roof of the world):** the final reveal and the Thousand Gates.

## Cast (fixed looks; each has distinct skin, hair, main color, accessory, footwear, height)
- **Wen Lan, 16.** Hero. Light warm-tan skin, black hair in a high ponytail tied with a faded blue cloth (HAIR STAGES by chapter: ch1-8 all black; ch9-18 one thin white frost streak at the left temple; ch19-24 the whole ponytail is white; ch25 white fading back to black at the roots), a plain pale-linen tunic with short sleeves and a woven grey sash, a dark charcoal wrap skirt over dark wide trousers rolled to the calf, soft cloth-wrapped traveling boots (the SAME outfit for the whole book), a small bamboo water-gourd on a cord across her back and a small travel pack. (This matches the girl in the fantasy-real image 126.png that the user loves: ponytail, pale tunic, dark lower garment.) Normal height (about 160 cm). Stubborn, curious, funny when scared, a gifted listener; ashamed of how much she wants to be useful. Want: to turn the river back on. Need: to understand she cannot own it.
- **Auntie Mo (Mo Ayi), 52.** Ferrywoman, later mentor. Medium-brown weathered skin, grey hair in a tight bun under a conical bamboo hat, a faded blue cotton jacket, patched grey trousers, bare feet on the boat; strong forearms, a bamboo pole; short, wide, laconic. Truth-teller.
- **Master Gao (Gao Shifu), 50.** Caravan master and guide for the first half; charming, tall, silk-collared green traveling robe with brass buttons, a grey-streaked topknot with a jade hairpin, a neat beard, polished black boots, a ledger of routes under his arm. TWIST: the Cistern Lord's agent.
- **Shen Yu, 17.** A river-surveyor's apprentice who joins Wen at Silt Harbor. Pale skin, short messy black hair, round brass-rimmed glasses, a brown canvas vest over a white shirt with ink-stained cuffs, a surveyor's staff and a rolled map case, grey cloth shoes. Clever, anxious, chatty. TWIST: sent to watch her; stops reporting; turns. The tallest teen.
- **Lord Bao Heng, 58.** The Cistern Lord. Heavy, pale, silk robe in deep red with gold thread, a round black cap, a soft smile. Never seen in person until ch22 (appears in letters, seals, hired agents). Believes he is saving the realm from drought by storing water. Not a mustache-twirler.
- **Lin Hua, 45 (the mother).** Wen's mother; the previous river-bearer; frozen in the glacier since Wen was four (revealed ch19-23). Tall, pale ivory skin, long black hair, a deep-blue travelling coat.
- **Old Tianzhu, 70.** A terrace-keeper in the Terrace Plains; kind, funny. Bald, brown skin, a straw hat, a long grey beard, a hoe.
- **The river.** Never speaks; the sound of it is the book's heartbeat.

## Arc (25 chapters)
Part One, THE DRY POOL (1-5): Hollow Pool, the first act of power, the village's decision, Auntie Mo's ferry, Wen sets out; Master Gao offers to guide.
Part Two, THE LOWLANDS (6-10): Silt Harbor and the Dry Delta (stranded fleet); meeting Shen Yu; the Terrace Plains (the sharing trial); first sight of the missing-water pattern.
Part Three, THE GORGE AND THE SALT (11-15): the Gorge of Hanging Steps (trackers, the flash flood); the White Basin; Wen's power burns; midpoint: the discovery of a hidden reservoir (the first cistern) and the first suspicion of Gao.
Part Four, THE FLOATING TOWN (16-20): Floating Lantern Town; Gao's betrayal; Shen Yu's confession and turn; the escape; the climb begins; the frost on her hands.
Part Five, THE SOURCE (21-25): the Glacier Valley, the Cloud Sea, her mother in the ice; the Crater Lake; Lord Bao Heng; the Thousand Gates, staged along the river (a flood-wave plan), the river wakes, mother melts free, the long walk home.

## Themes and motifs
Water is borrowed; the ledger; downstream; the white frost streak; the blue cloth tie; her water-gourd (she never drinks from it until the end); the Bearer's Almanac epigraphs; mist and wide skies; "walk, don't summon".

## Layout (for the factory)
5 pages per chapter as usual; pages 1 and 3 of every chapter are WIDE scenic pages (their IMAGE line starts with "WIDE:"), rendered 1536x864 (16:9) and shown full-width in the reader; the others square 768x768 or 1024x768. Wide pages show a SMALL Wen in a huge scene (cinematic matte painting).

## Things to avoid
Horror, gore, death of a main character, villain cartoons (Lord Bao and Gao believe they are protecting people), preachiness, long lectures, brand names, modern slang, invented religion or folklore, invented Chinese words, stereotypes (no "mystical Oriental" cliches), guns. Romance: a held hand at most.
