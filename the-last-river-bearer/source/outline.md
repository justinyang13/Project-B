# PART ONE: THE DRY POOL

## 1. The River That Overslept
Synopsis: The Clear River has not come for three days; at Hollow Pool, Wen's grief makes the last puddle rise, and she learns she can move water.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 1: Water is never lost. It is only somewhere else.*
1. WIDE: Dawn in the terraced valley of Qingshi: Wen goes down to Hollow Pool with two buckets, expecting the river to have simply overslept; the pool is a bowl of cracked mud with a dark thumbprint of water in the middle, mist in the gorge above, stranded fish gasping.
2. Her neighbors gather: the third dry day, empty terrace channels, the waterwheel still, rice seedlings yellowing; a worried farmer quotes Wen's late grandmother's saying that the river sleeps, another mocks it.
3. WIDE: Alone at the pool Wen kneels by the last puddle and whispers to it; it lifts into a trembling sphere the size of a melon and hangs in the air; she freezes; it falls in a splash; she tries again with her breath held.
4. She calls the whole puddle into a spiral of mist and droplets around her, delighted for three seconds, then terrified: a cold ache runs up her arms, and she hears the far end of the village channel go dry.
5. Auntie Mo the ferrywoman, who has watched from the bank, says quietly "So it is true," tells Wen to put the water back (she does, shaking), and says the river has been stopped upstream by hands, not weather, and that Wen's mother would have known.

## 2. What the Pool Remembered
Synopsis: The village meets, a plan forms, and Wen decides to walk to the source.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 2: A drought is weather. A drying is a decision.*
1. WIDE: The village hall at night, lamps low: farmers argue over the last well; the headman suggests selling the terraces; Wen sits at the back with one hand tucked under her sleeve.
2. Auntie Mo tells the room what only ferrymen know: the boats have been stranding further down for two seasons, and the river has not run dry at the mouth; it has run dry everywhere at once, like a tap turned.
3. WIDE: Wen's father, a quiet rice-grower, walks her home through the dark terraces and gives her a folded blue cloth, her mother's travelling scarf; her mother walked up the river when Wen was four and did not come back.
4. Under a lamp Wen studies her mother's map of the whole Clear River, sea to source, two thousand kilometers, with notes in a tight careful hand; she measures it with a thread and counts more than a hundred days on foot.
5. At dawn she packs a bamboo gourd, rice cakes and the blue cloth tied in her ponytail; at the village gate Auntie Mo waits with her ferry pole, saying she is going downriver anyway, so they may as well share the first road.

## 3. The Ferry in the Mud
Synopsis: The ferry cannot float; Wen learns draft, silt and the first rule of moving water.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 3: Water is lazy. It takes the lowest road and calls it wisdom.*
1. WIDE: Auntie Mo's flat ferry lies stuck on a mud bar a quarter kilometer from the water; Mo explains draft (how deep a boat sits) and teaches Wen to read a pole marked in hands.
2. They try poling and towing; Wen proposes lifting the water under the boat; Mo says do the arithmetic first: how many buckets is a boat's weight? Wen computes it on a mud-smoothed board and goes pale.
3. WIDE: Mo shows the safe way: dig a trench ahead, pile the mud into a low levee to guide the thin water; Wen digs by hand and learns to dig the channel deep and keep the levee low.
4. A small convoy of stranded boats gathers: a salt-trader, a family with ducks, a monk with a bell; the mud bar becomes a village for an evening; the salt-trader grumbles that the river has been tapped upstream.
5. At twilight Wen lays a ribbon of the river's last pool along the trench like a silver road and the ferry floats; the crowd cheers; Mo sees the frost on Wen's fingertips and says nothing yet.

## 4. Master Gao's Offer
Synopsis: At the edge of the Dry Delta a charming caravan master offers to guide Wen upriver.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 4: Be careful of help that arrives before you have asked for it.*
1. WIDE: They reach the edge of the Dry Delta: a mudflat to a horizon of ghost-grey light, a harbor of stranded ships leaning in the silt like snapped trees; Silt Harbor beyond.
2. A tall man in a green traveling robe with a jade pin and a ledger under his arm, Master Gao, steps from a mule caravan; he has heard of a girl who made a ferry float.
3. WIDE: Over fish soup in a half-empty inn Gao lays out the road: seventeen towns, six passes, thirty-eight bridges; he offers Wen a place in his caravan all the way; Mo is silent and watchful.
4. Wen asks why a successful man would take a girl with a gourd; Gao smiles and says he knew her mother once.
5. That night Wen puts her hand in the harbor water and feels, faintly, the whole river's length, thin as a thread, drawn tight toward something far away.

## 5. The Dry Delta
Synopsis: The caravan crosses the delta; Wen learns to read silt and meets a surveyor boy.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 5: A river builds the land it will one day drown.*
1. WIDE: The caravan crosses the delta on an old causeway: dried reeds, mud cracked into hexagons, boats stranded in rows; Gao says the mud is the river's own silt, a thousand years of it.
2. They meet Shen Yu, a pale surveyor's apprentice with round glasses and ink-stained cuffs, planting red flags and reading a staff through a brass tube; he is measuring how far the river has fallen: three meters.
3. WIDE: Shen Yu talks too fast; he shows a level and explains how slope sets speed and silt raises the bed when water slows; Gao watches him with something that is not quite a smile.
4. Wen asks whether something upstream is stopping the river; Shen Yu goes quiet, says it is the question he is not allowed to measure, then asks to come with them; Gao agrees with unusual speed.
5. At day's end they stand at the edge of the delta: a vast pale road of dry riverbed winds into a sea of mist; Wen takes her first full look at the road she will walk.

# PART TWO: THE TERRACE PLAINS

## 6. A Hundred Villages
Synopsis: The caravan enters the Terrace Plains, where a hundred villages fight over a trickle.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 6: The wealth of a hill is whatever the forest keeps.*
1. WIDE: A huge green staircase of rice terraces climbs both sides of a valley, each field a mirror of sky, a dark forest crowning the ridge; Shen Yu explains how the forest holds rain and releases it down the channels over months.
2. The channels are nearly dry; sentries guard the sluices; an upper village and a lower village are at the edge of a fight over one gate; an old terrace-keeper, Tianzhu, with a straw hat and a hoe, stands between them.
3. WIDE: Wen asks to see how the water is shared; Tianzhu shows an ancient stone gate with notched marks: each field takes its fill and the rest passes on; no one has broken it until the water got too little.
4. Gao proposes kindly that the upper villages take more now and the lower ones be paid later; the upper headman relaxes, the lower headman goes white.
5. That night Wen sits with Tianzhu on a terrace wall; he says a terrace is a promise made of mud, each field keeping it for the one below.

## 7. The Sharing Trial
Synopsis: Wen is asked to divide the last stream fairly, with her power and her head.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 7: Fairness is a number everyone can see.*
1. WIDE: At dawn both villages demand Wen use her gift to decide; whoever she gives the water to, wins; the crowd chants her name; she hates it.
2. She refuses to choose and asks Shen Yu to help measure: float a leaf to time the stream, measure the cross-section with a rope, count each terrace's area and need.
3. WIDE: They find the stream is just enough if every field takes only what it needs and if water is delivered by turns; Shen Yu draws a schedule; the old notched gate was designed for exactly this.
4. She uses her power only to clear a clog and hold a small surge so the first turn can start; the gate runs by itself; Tianzhu puts his straw hat on her head and calls her little bearer.
5. Gao praises her cleverness and suggests she travel faster and leave the villages; Wen notices that whenever something is fixed Gao seems a little disappointed, and writes it in her mother's map margin.

## 8. The Trackers' Road
Synopsis: At the foot of the great gorge, trackers haul a boat up a rapid and Wen helps.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 8: A rope is a river held in the hands of a hundred people.*
1. WIDE: The caravan enters the mouth of the Gorge of Hanging Steps: cliffs a thousand meters high, a narrow green ribbon of river, stone stairs cut in the rock, mist rising up the cliff face.
2. A hundred trackers in rope harness haul a cargo boat up a rapid on bamboo hawsers, singing to keep their steps in time; each step must fall with the next or the rope snaps.
3. WIDE: The boat jams on a rock; a young tracker slips; Wen calls a thin sheet of water over the rock to lift the hull a hand's breadth; the rope slackens and the boat slides free.
4. The foreman, broad old Aunt Zhu, grips Wen's shoulder and says the whole river sings about a bearer who will come to turn the water back.
5. Zhu sings the verse; Wen realizes it is about her mother; Shen Yu looks at his boots; for one moment Gao looks afraid.

## 9. Stairs in the Cliff
Synopsis: Climbing the cliff stairs, the party learns the gorge's danger: flash floods.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 9: A storm that falls somewhere else is already on its way to you.*
1. WIDE: The party climbs thousands of stone steps up the cliff beside the river; Shen Yu explains that rain far upstream becomes a flood wave that reaches the gorge hours later; the sky is clear.
2. At a high platform they see a flood mark cut six meters above the present water, the ancient stair-builders' warning.
3. WIDE: A boy runs down shouting that the sky above the high mountains turned black an hour ago; Shen Yu does the arithmetic aloud (distance divided by wave speed): forty minutes to the narrowest point.
4. Wen and the trackers hold the first surge at the gorge's neck, she lifting the rising water into a long controlled spiral as Shen Yu calls the numbers while the trackers haul boats and families up the stairs; her breath fogs in the heat.
5. The flood passes like soft thunder; no one is lost; Wen collapses on the stair with a single thin white frost strand at her left temple; Shen Yu notices; Aunt Zhu notices; neither speaks.

## 10. The Price of a Cup
Synopsis: In a gorge town a water-seller shows the first sign that the river is being sold.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 10: When water has a price, someone has put a gate where there was none.*
1. WIDE: A narrow town clings to the cliff in tiers; the fountain is dry; a stall sells sealed clay jars stamped with a round-gate seal, clean water by the cup at ten times the price of rice.
2. Shen Yu examines the seal: it is not local, it is a Cistern seal he has seen on tax rolls; Gao quickly says it is a common merchant mark.
3. WIDE: The water-seller's daughter says the water arrives by night in covered carts from upriver; a wet track in the dust leads north.
4. Auntie Mo, who has followed the caravan, says the trackers whisper of great round stone pools in the hills, sealed with the round gate, filled every night.
5. Wen tests Gao with a lie about tomorrow's road; Mo agrees to follow the other path and watch; Wen lies awake wondering whether she is wrong to doubt a friend.

# PART THREE: THE GORGE AND THE SALT

## 11. The First Cistern
Synopsis: Wen and Mo find a hidden reservoir and see how large the theft is.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 11: What is hidden is usually larger than what is shown.*
1. WIDE: Mo and Wen climb a dry side valley by moonlight following the wet track to a vast circular stone basin roofed in grey slate, a great hidden black pool.
2. Guards patrol the rim with lanterns; the intake runs back to the Clear River by a secret tunnel; Wen feels new grief: her river, imprisoned.
3. WIDE: Shen Yu, who followed them, paces the rim and finds the basin holds about eleven days of the river's flow; if freed at once it would flood three towns below.
4. Wen touches the basin wall and feels the water want to go home; she holds back and calls a curtain of mist to hide them as the guards approach.
5. Back at camp Gao waits with a lantern, smiling; its brass frame is stamped with a small round gate; Wen says nothing, but her fingers go cold.

## 12. A Spy's Report
Synopsis: Shen Yu confesses part of the truth; Wen decides to let Gao think she suspects nothing.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 12: Sometimes the safest lie is to be believed.*
1. WIDE: At dawn Shen Yu confesses he has been sending messages by tin-whistle code to someone in each town, told it was for the river office; he now suspects otherwise.
2. They read the code together: the messages report Wen's location, strength and deeds; Wen forgives him and decides to feed false reports that she is tired and nearly ready to turn back.
3. WIDE: Mo says the mule-boys are paid in silver stamped with the round gate; they are loyal to money, not to Gao; she advises Wen not to argue, since an argument would tell the whole river she knows.
4. The caravan climbs into the barren uplands toward the White Basin; at a stream Wen finds the cold has reached her wrist.
5. Shen Yu says a bearer's power may be heat leaving her body into the water, like a spoon warming in tea; Wen asks how much she has left to spend; neither mentions her mother.

## 13. The White Basin
Synopsis: The caravan crosses a vast dead lake of salt, where her power burns her.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 13: A dead sea is a lesson written in salt.*
1. WIDE: A basin the size of a province of white crust under a pale blue sky, a shimmering mirage of a lake that is not there; the caravan crosses on a causeway of salt blocks.
2. Water is rationed; the mules fail; the brine pools between the salt hexagons burn Wen's fingers like wasps; she cannot call them; the first time her power fails her.
3. WIDE: Shen Yu explains that a lake with no outlet concentrates salt as the sun evaporates it; they build a solar still from a salt-block frame and a sheet of oilcloth and collect a cup of pure water an hour.
4. Mo notices Gao never drinks from the caravan's barrels, only from his own sealed jar marked with the round gate, and tells Wen at night.
5. At the basin's center a tall blue mirage lake appears with a floating town in it; Wen realizes the town is real, only upside down in the shimmer: Floating Lantern Town lies beyond the salt.

## 14. A Cistern Town in the Salt
Synopsis: The caravan reaches a dry town fed by one long clay pipe; Wen learns a pipe can also be a trap.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 14: A pipe is a promise that runs only one way.*
1. WIDE: A walled town on the lake-bed's rim whose wells are brackish and whose one fresh source is a long clay pipe from the north; the elder greets Gao with great respect; a round gate is carved over the gatehouse.
2. A woman collapses from thirst; Wen, risking everything, calls water from the morning mist caught in a cloth into the woman's cup; Shen Yu is astonished that she used no pool at all.
3. WIDE: Gao watches closely and asks Wen to show her full power for the elder; Mo says no, Wen says yes; Shen Yu realizes the journey has been a test.
4. Wen raises a column of brackish well water and filters it through a siphon and the town's own clay pipe; the town has sweet water for the first time in a year; frost runs from her wrist to her elbow.
5. That night she wakes freezing with her breath white in the desert heat; Mo holds her hands in a basin of warm sand and tells her not to do it again unless the river itself is at stake; Wen says it was.

## 15. The Midpoint: A Pipe of Silver
Synopsis: Wen follows the pipe to its end and learns who is buying the water.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 15: Follow the pipe, and the story will tell itself.*
1. WIDE: Wen, Mo and Shen Yu trace the buried pipe across a ridge into a grove of dry poplars to a tall slate roof: a cistern much bigger than the first.
2. Carved on the cistern wall is the round gate and a name, BAO HENG, Cistern Lord, with a date eleven years earlier, the year Wen's mother vanished.
3. WIDE: In a side chamber they find the Lord's stone-bound ledger of every town's water debt in silver; Shen Yu sums it: the Lord now holds about three years of the river's flow.
4. Gao enters with six mule-boys and a pleasant smile; he says he hoped they would not find this until the Source; he is regretful, not angry; he says Lord Bao is only a man afraid of drought.
5. Gao asks Wen to come willingly to Floating Lantern Town; she says no; a wall of hired men steps from the dark; Mo shouts "Run!" and a lamp is kicked over.

# PART FOUR: THE FLOATING TOWN

## 16. Escape by Water
Synopsis: Wen escapes the cistern by turning the stored water into a tool.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 16: When you cannot out-walk a problem, out-think its shape.*
1. WIDE: The cistern fills with lamp smoke and shouts; Wen backs against the great basin wall; Shen Yu pulls at a bronze sluice wheel; Mo shields them with her pole.
2. Wen cannot lift the basin, but she can hold a thin knife of water against the sluice gate's weak point and let the whole stored lake's pressure do the work, while Shen Yu shouts that each meter of depth pushes a ton on every square meter of gate.
3. WIDE: The gate bursts outward; a controlled torrent runs down the service channel; Wen steers it in a long curve around the hired men, who scatter onto the dry bank; she has drowned no one and is sick with relief.
4. They run through the poplar grove under a rising moon; Gao stands in the soaked empty cistern, does not follow, and blows three notes on a small brass whistle.
5. Mo says the notes mean the bearer is awake and the Lord now knows she is coming; "He knew you were coming," says Mo, "he did not know you were this strong."

## 17. Floating Lantern Town
Synopsis: They arrive in the great raft-town where water is sold by the cup, and the spy's last secret comes out.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 17: A town that floats must trust the water that holds it.*
1. WIDE: A grand dusk scene: a market city on thousands of linked rafts and stilt houses on a vast shallow lake, strung with red lanterns, boats, stalls and rope bridges; the only place on the river with real water, because the Lord allows it.
2. Wen, Mo and Shen Yu enter in disguise as fisherfolk; water-merchants in red robes sell by the cup from porcelain jars to a patient line.
3. WIDE: Shen Yu is called away by a messenger; Wen follows and finds him in a teahouse handing a tin whistle to a smooth-faced official, promising to deliver the bearer; his hands shake.
4. Wen says only his name; he says it began as a job to pay for his sister's schooling and ended as a friendship; "I am a coward"; Wen: "Both can be true; choose which to keep."
5. Shen Yu throws the whistle into the lake and walks out beside her; a great bell begins to toll across the water: the town has seen the bearer.

## 18. The Lord's Letters
Synopsis: Wen reads the Lord's letters to her mother and learns what he believes.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 18: Even a villain has a reason, and the reason is usually fear.*
1. WIDE: Hiding in a rain-sealed warehouse on a raft with Mo and Shen Yu, Wen finds a leather case of unsent letters to Lin Hua, her mother, the oldest dated eleven years ago.
2. The Lord argues that the glaciers at the source are shrinking, a drought will kill a hundred thousand people, and he stores water against that day; he asks her mother to join him.
3. WIDE: Her mother's one reply: "You are storing water for a drought you are causing. A river is not a bank. Take only what you will give back. I am going to the Source to see for myself."
4. Shen Yu confirms the glacier has shrunk by a third in a generation; the Lord is not lying about the danger, only about the answer; Wen uneasily agrees with half of what he says.
5. Mo says a frightened man stores water and a wise one shares it; footsteps hammer the raft outside: soldiers have found them.

## 19. The Frost Reaches the Heart
Synopsis: Wen's power backfires; the cost turns visible and she learns the truth about being a bearer.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 19: Every gift is a loan.*
1. WIDE: The soldiers surround the raft; Wen raises the lake into a wall to hold them out; ice races from her fingers to her shoulders and her whole ponytail turns white from root to tip.
2. Shen Yu, terrified, says the oldest river-office scroll states that a bearer's power is drawn from her own warmth, and if the frost reaches the heart she turns to ice; old bearers lived short lives or walked to the cold place to be kept.
3. WIDE: Wen sits very still; she understands about her mother: "She did not leave me. She was already freezing;" Mo holds her hand until the shaking stops.
4. They drop through a trapdoor into a little boat; Wen cannot use her power without risking the last of her warmth; she must escape by oar and wit; Shen Yu steers by the stars; Mo rows.
5. At the far edge of the town they reach the fresh river again, a thin bright channel into the mountains; Wen looks at her white hair in the water and says "Then I will not waste what is left."

## 20. The Long Climb
Synopsis: The three travel toward the glacier on foot and by boat; a quieter chapter of friendship and one big idea.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 20: A road is made by those who walk it twice.*
1. WIDE: The river becomes a bright mountain stream between pine and rhododendron slopes; yaks graze; strings of colored cloth flutter over the water; the three walk in cold sun taking turns with the pack.
2. Without her power Wen learns by hand: wading, gauging a current with a thrown stick, fording diagonally at the widest shallowest spot; Shen Yu teaches, Mo corrects, they laugh for the first time in a week.
3. WIDE: In a herders' village Wen asks how they share: every morning the oldest woman pours a measured cup into each household's jar and no one cheats because everyone can see.
4. At the village spring Wen feels the Lord's reservoirs like a held breath in her bones and begins to count the gates all down the river; there are at least a thousand.
5. Shen Yu has the idea that changes the book: each reservoir has a gate and a keeper; if every town opened its own gate on a signal, the river would wake all at once in a flood wave that can be predicted and staged. "A thousand gates," says Wen; "A thousand people," says Mo.

# PART FIVE: THE SOURCE

## 21. The Glacier Valley
Synopsis: They enter the glacier valley at the roof of the world and find the frozen river.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 21: Ice is only a river that is waiting.*
1. WIDE: A vast white valley of ice walls and blue crevasses, a frozen river thousands of years old glowing blue-green in the high light; the air is thin and bitter; Wen's breath clouds and her white hair crackles.
2. Shen Yu measures the glacier's retreat from old marker stones now far from the ice; the wetlands below are cracked; the Lord's drought is real; Wen says there are two tasks, ending the hoarding and slowing the drying, and they are not the same.
3. WIDE: They cross a frozen river on a snow bridge with a rope; the ice sings; Wen, unable to use her power, loses her grip with cold hands; Mo catches her.
4. In an ice cave they find a line of carved keepers' stones, each with a small girl and a water gourd; the last is newer and has her mother's face and a date eleven years ago.
5. Wen kneels before it and breaks; Mo holds her; Shen Yu turns away and quietly cries; above them the ice creaks like a great door opening.

## 22. The Cloud Sea
Synopsis: They cross a high pass above the clouds; Lord Bao arrives at last and speaks with Wen.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 22: The people who frighten us most are usually afraid.*
1. WIDE: The top of a pass above a sea of white cloud that fills the valleys like a frozen ocean, peaks standing out like islands, the sun blindingly gold.
2. A palanquin carried by twelve men arrives; out steps Lord Bao Heng, heavy and pale in a deep red robe and a round black cap with a gentle face; he is alone but for a servant and says he is here only to talk.
3. WIDE: They sit on a flat rock above the cloud sea with a pot of tea; he shows her accurate drought charts and says he knew her mother and she was the best person he ever met.
4. Wen says her mother wrote that a river is not a bank; the Lord says the drought is real; Wen says water held is water not shared; "Then show me the answer," he says.
5. He gives her a day and says "The source is not what you think," pity crossing his face; Gao appears behind the palanquin, soaked and silent, and mouths "Forgive me."

## 23. The Mother in the Ice
Synopsis: Wen finds her mother at the Source and learns the real choice.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 23: The last thing a river gives is its beginning.*
1. WIDE: The crater lake at the roof of the world: a ring of black peaks around a perfect round lake of impossibly clear deep-blue ice-water, a silent cathedral of rock and sky.
2. Under a skin of clear ice in the center, a woman in a deep-blue coat stands upright, hair floating, eyes closed, a small bronze gourd in her hand: Lin Hua.
3. WIDE: Wen kneels on the ice with her hand flat on it; her mother's voice comes through the water; she froze herself to hold the glacier together and slow the melt; she could not leave the river unheld.
4. The choice every last bearer faces: freeze to hold the Source another generation, or give back what she has borrowed; the second way is harder because it needs everyone; "The river does not need a keeper. It needs a thousand hands."
5. Wen: "I will teach them." The ice trembles; at the far rim the palanquin has arrived and the Lord looks out over the ice with his hand over his mouth.

## 24. A Thousand Gates
Synopsis: Wen begins the great release by teaching every town to open its own gate on a signal.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 24: A river is strong only where it is shared.*
1. WIDE: On the crater rim Wen, Shen Yu and the Lord spread a map; Shen Yu works out the flood wave's travel time from the length, the slope and a wave speed of about seven kilometers an hour: nine days to the sea, each town's gate opening at its own hour.
2. The Lord says his cistern keepers will open on his command; the only thing he cannot do is persuade the towns they are not being cheated; Wen: "That part is mine."
3. WIDE: Auntie Mo speaks her plain sentence: a river belongs to everyone who drinks downstream; the first people who forgot were not wicked, they were frightened; the Lord weeps without hiding it.
4. Wen walks back down town by town with Mo, Shen Yu and a message from the Lord, teaching gate-keepers a signal of smoke, drum and bell passed from town to town faster than any wave.
5. In the first town Gao rows up and opens its gate before anyone asks, stands in the mud with the round-gate whistle and drops it in the water; Mo says nothing; the first gate is open.

## 25. The River Wakes
Synopsis: The Thousand Gates open in a chain; the river wakes; Wen's mother melts free; Wen walks home.
Header: *The Bearer's Almanac, Entry 25: The river does not remember being stopped.*
1. WIDE: On the ninth night at first light the first gate opens at the Source; a thread of blue-white water runs over the crater's lip; far below a ribbon of silver begins to move through the mountains and plains, gate after gate opening in a chain like lamps being lit.
2. Wen, hair white, hands cold to the elbow, calls water for the last time in the book, not from a pool but from the whole thaw her mother is releasing, carrying it in one great spiral over the cliff, giving back what she borrowed; the frost leaves her fingers one by one.
3. WIDE: The ice cracks; Lin Hua rises gasping into sunlight; Wen runs across the lake and catches her; her mother laughs and cries and says "You are so tall;" Mo and Shen Yu watch from the shore; the Lord kneels on the rock and bows.
4. They walk home along the river past each town: terraces where Tianzhu pours the first cup into every jar, the gorge where Aunt Zhu's trackers sing, Floating Lantern Town settling on a bright rising lake; Wen's hair regains its black near the roots.
5. At Hollow Pool the whole village waits; the water laps the stones; Wen kneels, puts her hand in, and does not call it; she lets it run through her fingers, opens the bamboo gourd she has not drunk from in a hundred days and drinks at last from the river, while far up the mountains a thousand gates stand open in the morning light.
