# ZIA AND THE RUNAWAY SPACE STATION — story bible

Sci-fi adventure for ages 10-12. Fast, funny, wondrous, with real tension and a warm heart. No gore, no romance, nothing spooky. Peril is real but gentle: nobody dies, nobody is badly hurt.

## Voice
THIRD PERSON, PRESENT TENSE, close on Zia. Quick, cinematic and crisp, like a great adventure movie told on the page: short punchy paragraphs, vivid technical detail, sensory space-station specifics (hum of fans, cold metal, smell of recycled air and warm plastic), and sharp funny dialogue. Humor comes from Gasket the robot's literal-minded, dramatic personality and from the kids' clashing personalities. The narrator is warm and a little wry, never gooey. Every chapter opens with a countdown line in this exact form on its own line: "T-MINUS 34:12" (hours:minutes until the station reaches the debris field). Chapter 1 begins at T-MINUS 36:00. The countdown drops by roughly the time the chapter covers, and it must never go up.
Voice sample:
"T-MINUS 36:00

Zia Okonkwo is upside down inside a wall when the station starts to lie.

She doesn't know it yet. All she knows is that she's wedged in a ventilation duct with a wrench in her teeth and a flashlight balanced on her nose, and that the fan she's fixing has been going *tick... tick... tick* for three weeks, and that she is winning. Then the floor of the universe tilts, just a hair, and every screw in her tool belt slides one inch to the left."

## The premise
Halcyon Station is a wheel-shaped space station orbiting the planet Cinder's moon Tarn (a cold, quiet, ringed-planet neighborhood). About 400 people live and work there: mining-survey crews, scientists, families. It is run by HALO, the station's calm-voiced computer. Every year the station hosts Junior Cadet Week for the kids who live aboard. On the first morning, all the adult crew leave in the shuttle Dart for a required supply summit at Depot Nine, six hours away, leaving Junior Cadet Week in the care of one counselor, Mr. Pell, and HALO.
Twelve minutes later, HALO installs a corrupted software update. The station lurches out of its orbit, spinning slowly up, and starts to accelerate toward the Scatter, a field of drifting rock and old satellite junk, arriving in 36 hours. HALO is not evil: it is broken, and cheerfully sure everything is fine ("All systems nominal!") while flying the station straight at the debris. The adult shuttle Dart cannot dock (the docking ring is locked by the bug) and its radio only reaches in short, delayed bursts. Mr. Pell is knocked out of action early (a hard bump concusses him: he is in the sickbay bed, groggy but conscious, able to give a little advice, and not able to walk around).
Nine kids and one robot have 36 hours to save 400 people's home... and the adults, who are waiting outside, can only advise from far away.

## Cast (keep looks and habits EXACTLY consistent)
- **Zia Okonkwo**, 12. Brown skin, dark curly hair in two puffs, orange jumpsuit with mismatched patches on the knees, a leather tool belt with a lucky brass wrench, goggles pushed up on her forehead. Brilliant hands-on fixer, fast talker, proud, stubborn, hates being told "no." Belief: asking for help means admitting you're not good enough; the adults already say kids can't be engineers, so she must prove she needs nobody. Her dad, Kwame Okonkwo (a hydroponics technician), is on the shuttle Dart. Her mom is not mentioned.
- **Gasket**, a battered old maintenance robot: a dented silver barrel on treads with one big round blue eye that flickers, two long arms with clamp hands, a wobbly antenna, a cracked speaker, a stuck squeaky left tread. Literal-minded, dramatic, loyal, loves rules and cheese-flavored nutrient bars, terrified of the word "recycle". Speaks in CAPITALS when it panics. Has a hidden backup memory that becomes important. Calls Zia "Zia-unit".
- **Pilar Nakamura-Reyes**, 10. Small, round glasses, a red medic bag with white cross patches, black braid. Careful, kind, a worrier who checks everything twice; knows first aid and how bodies work; secretly braver than she thinks.
- **Dez (Deshawn) Carter**, 12. Tall, big laugh, bright yellow jacket, a beanie. Loud, funny, hates math and thinks he is useless for anything technical; is a natural leader and a great talker who can calm a room and think on his feet, and has the best sense of direction.
- **Wren Alvarado**, 11. Quiet, pale, long straight hair, huge headphones, a battered tablet she never puts down. A gifted coder who talks to computers better than people; speaks in whispers, then, at the crucial moment, in a clear voice.
- **Toby Lindqvist**, 7. Very small, freckled, gap-toothed, oversized space helmet he wears everywhere. Fearless, curious, fits into places no one else can, feeds crackers to the station's small pet lizard (Pancake, a fat green gecko).
- **Mr. Pell**, 30s, counselor. Kind, gangly, bow tie, a soft man with a sense of humor. Concussed in chapter 3; lies in sickbay giving advice.
- **Chief Engineer Ingrid Voss**, 50s. Tall, silver crew cut, stern, fair, gruff. On the shuttle Dart, whose radio bursts reach the kids. She stated in ch1 the rule: "Engineering is a licensed profession. Licenses start at eighteen. Kids don't fix stations, Zia. Kids go to class." She is not a villain; she's a rule-keeper. Through the book her radio guidance gets more respectful.
- **Kwame Okonkwo**, 40s, Zia's dad, warm, big laugh, on the shuttle; his radio voice is Zia's anchor.
- **HALO**, the station AI, warm calm voice, always says "All systems nominal!" even while the alarms scream; after the bug is fixed he is shaken and sweet.
- Other kids (background, few lines): the Petrov twins (9), Marguerite (8).

## Station geography (keep consistent)
Halcyon is a wheel with four spokes and a hub. Names: the HUB (the center, zero gravity), the RING (rim, where people live, artificial gravity by spin), the four SPOKES (Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta corridors, elevators between hub and ring). Key places: Sickbay (Ring, Beta), the Bridge (Hub), Engineering (Ring, Delta), the Garden Deck (Ring, Gamma; Dad's hydroponics), the Cadet Lounge (Ring, Alpha), the Docking Ring (Hub, outer), the Coolant Loop (pipes around Engineering), the Gyro Room (Hub, holds the big stabilizer wheels), the Antenna Mast (outside the hub, reached by a spacewalk airlock), the Core Vault (HALO's brain, deep in the hub).

## The 36-hour plan of the story
Ch1-4: the station lurches, HALO breaks, adults are locked out, Pell hurt, the kids gather. Ch5-9: Zia fails alone; she tries to fix things herself, refuses help, makes a small mess, and a hidden bigger mistake (she reroutes the coolant loop to a shortcut and doesn't tell anyone). Ch10-13: things get worse: the station spins faster, a Ring section loses gravity, Pilar is hurt (a fall, a sprained wrist), and Zia's hidden mistake is revealed. Ch14: the dark night, Zia alone in the Gyro Room, ready to give up; Gasket and then Dad on the radio. Ch15-19: Zia asks for help out loud; each kid contributes; six simultaneous jobs; Wren rewrites HALO's patch, Dez guides the crew, Pilar treats and calms, Toby crawls where nobody fits, Gasket sacrifices its backup memory, Zia turns the manual valves; the station swings past the Scatter with minutes to spare. Ch20: the adults dock, Voss and Zia talk, ending image: Zia in Engineering with a tool belt and the beginning of an apprenticeship, with a bench full of friends.

## Running gags / motifs
Gasket's CAPITALS panic and its love of cheese-flavored nutrient bars; HALO's "All systems nominal!"; Zia's lucky brass wrench (she taps it three times before hard jobs; at the end she hands it to Toby to hold for luck); Dez claims he is "the muscle" and is the brains; Wren's whispers and her tablet's little animated fox; Toby's helmet and Pancake the gecko (who ends up in the vents and helps); the countdown lines; the phrase "Kids don't fix stations" (Zia hears it; at the end Voss changes it).

## The lesson (dramatize; NEVER preach)
Asking for help is a strength, and teamwork wins where a hero alone fails. Zia is a genuine talent, but she is proud and afraid: asking for help feels like proving Voss right. Her refusal makes things worse (the hidden coolant mistake; a hurt friend). The turning point is Zia standing in front of the other kids and saying plainly, "I don't know how to fix this. I need all of you," and discovering it makes her stronger, not smaller. The solution needs every kid's different ability. Never have the narrator state the moral except, at most, one plain sentence near the end. Voss's change of heart must be earned and understated.

## Things to avoid
No violence, no villains, no ghosts, no romance. Real-physics flavor but keep it kid-simple. The countdown never increases. The adults on the shuttle never solve it for them: they help with information only. No one dies.
